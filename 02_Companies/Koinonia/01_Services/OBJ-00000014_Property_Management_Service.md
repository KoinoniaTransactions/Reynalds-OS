## Purpose

This object is the canonical working business definition for the property management service offered by Koinonia Properties.

It exists to keep the Koinonia Properties public website, future service documentation, and operating decisions aligned around the same approved service scope while the business and website are still in development.

Koinonia Properties is a separate company and public product from Koinonia Transactions. This business record is stored inside Reynalds OS for continuity; its repository location does not make Koinonia Properties a service line, section, alias, or extension of Koinonia Transactions.

This object governs public service positioning and scope. It does not, by itself, approve final pricing, geographic coverage, contractual terms, guarantees, private portal functionality, payment systems, application systems, or other operating claims that require separate verification or implementation.

---

## Core Positioning

Koinonia Properties provides property management built on clear communication, steady systems, and responsible care.

The public experience should make property management unmistakable and should help each audience reach the correct next step without presenting unfinished systems as live capabilities.

---

## Primary Audiences

### Property Owners

Owners need confidence in the management process, clear service scope, communication expectations, maintenance coordination, leasing support, and an understandable first step.

### Tenants and Rental Applicants

Residents and applicants need clear routes for rental availability, applications, maintenance guidance, policies, communication, and secure account access when that access is available.

### Real Estate Investors

Investors need repeatable property operations, leasing support, maintenance coordination, communication, and portfolio-aware management conversations.

### Vendors

Vendors need a clear contact path for maintenance coordination, service instructions, and invoice or work-order communication through the approved operating process.

---

## Canonical Service Scope

Koinonia Properties may publicly describe its property management work through the following service areas, subject to property-specific scope and approved operating procedures:

- rental analysis and property review;
- rental marketing and leasing support;
- tenant screening coordination;
- lease administration;
- rent collection systems and payment-process coordination;
- maintenance coordination;
- owner communication, updates, and reporting;
- move-in and move-out coordination;
- vendor communication and coordination;
- ongoing property-management support.

These are service categories, not a promise that every property receives every item under every engagement. Property-specific responsibilities, exclusions, approval thresholds, costs, and procedures belong in the applicable service agreement and approved operating policies.

---

## Owner Entry Path

The preferred owner entry point is a rental analysis.

The first conversation should begin with the property and the owner’s goals, including information such as:

- property address and property type;
- current condition and occupancy;
- current rental status or upcoming vacancy;
- known maintenance or turnover concerns;
- owner goals and target timing;
- desired management scope when known.

The public website should use this information to begin a useful conversation, not to collect sensitive financial, identity, payment, or private account data.

---

## Management Process

The canonical high-level management process is:

1. Review the property and owner goals.
2. Prepare the management plan and clarify responsibilities.
3. Coordinate leasing or onboarding as applicable.
4. Manage the approved rent, maintenance, communication, and reporting workflows.
5. Deliver organized owner updates and ongoing support.

Public copy may simplify this process for readability but should not contradict it or invent specific timing guarantees.

---

## Public Website Role

The Koinonia Properties public website should:

- explain the company and property-management service clearly;
- route owners toward rental analysis and owner-service information;
- route prospective tenants toward current rentals and application guidance;
- route current residents toward maintenance, policies, contact, and secure-access instructions;
- route vendors toward the appropriate inquiry path;
- explain pricing factors and service scope without inventing unapproved fees;
- publish only verified service-area claims;
- keep sensitive information out of ordinary public-site intake.

The public website is not the private property-management platform.

---

## Private Systems Boundary

Payments, applications, maintenance records, owner statements, lease documents, private account history, and other protected account activity belong in an approved private property-management system or other approved secure workflow.

Until a specific platform, privacy handling, security model, accounting workflow, and operating procedure are approved and implemented, the public website should route users to the appropriate next step rather than imply that these private capabilities are live inside the website.

---

## Pricing and Service Levels

Final pricing is not established by this object.

Until approved pricing is recorded in a dedicated canonical pricing source, public copy should explain that pricing depends on factors such as:

- property type;
- requested service scope;
- occupancy or leasing status;
- timing and property condition;
- portfolio size or complexity.

Leasing-only, full-service management, and portfolio management may be evaluated as service structures, but they are not canonical packages or guaranteed offers until explicitly approved and recorded.

---

## Service Areas

This object does not establish specific city, county, or market coverage.

Public service-area claims should remain limited to verified operating coverage. When coverage is not yet approved, the website should ask for the property address and confirm availability rather than publish unsupported geographic claims.

---

## Communication Standard

Koinonia Properties should communicate with a professional, organized, calm, confident, responsive, helpful, thoughtful, and detail-oriented tone.

Public language should favor:

- clear responsibilities;
- documented next steps;
- practical owner and resident guidance;
- responsible care of the property;
- transparent scope;
- appropriate routing to secure systems.

Public language should avoid:

- internal development or build-plan language;
- unverified statistics or testimonials;
- invented guarantees or response times;
- unsupported market-coverage claims;
- language that makes unfinished portal, payment, application, or maintenance systems appear live;
- language that merges Koinonia Properties with Koinonia Transactions.
