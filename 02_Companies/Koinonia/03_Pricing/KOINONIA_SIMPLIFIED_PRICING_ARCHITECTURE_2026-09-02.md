# Koinonia Simplified Pricing Architecture — 2026-09-02

Status: Working Recommendation / Owner Review Required  
Owner: Koinonia  
Purpose: Simplify how Koinonia pricing is presented to Realtors while separating Koinonia's management/coordination fees from variable outside expenses required to fulfill a request.

This document does not replace canonical pricing rules until approved and reconciled.

---

# 1. Core Pricing Principle

Koinonia should not present Realtors with a giant menu of micro-fees.

The customer should primarily see and understand **the Koinonia fee for taking responsibility for the work**.

When fulfillment requires photography, drone, video, printing, postage, advertising spend, staging, specialty web work, repairs, or another outside service, that expense should remain separate from the Koinonia fee because the cost legitimately varies with the property, market, vendor, scope, timing, and client choice.

Working principle:

> **Koinonia charges for the management, coordination, execution, and accountability we provide. Variable outside expenses are approved separately at the actual or agreed cost required for the job.**

The outside provider is an implementation detail. The Realtor should not have to source, schedule, brief, chase, or manage that provider themselves.

---

# 2. Important Transparency Rule

Koinonia does not need to market the service as "you will pay our vendors."

The client-facing language should instead be simple and truthful:

> **Koinonia service fees cover our coordination and execution. Media, advertising, printing, postage, and other outside production costs are additional when needed and are approved before purchase.**

This communicates the economic reality without making the Realtor feel they are buying a collection of disconnected vendors.

Recommended default commercial rule:

- Koinonia coordination/management fee is clearly defined.
- Outside expenses are passed through at actual/approved cost unless a different written package rule applies.
- Avoid hidden markup on outside expenses.
- If Koinonia wants margin for managing outside services, earn that margin through the visible Koinonia coordination/management fee rather than an invisible vendor markup.

This strengthens trust and makes internal accounting easier to understand.

---

# 3. Do Not Price Every Capability Separately

The five service umbrellas describe capability. They do **not** require five complicated pricing grids.

Koinonia should use a small number of pricing units across the business.

Recommended front-end pricing units:

1. **Per File / Listing**
2. **Per Field Assignment / Event**
3. **Per Project / Request**
4. **Monthly Partnership**
5. **Variable Outside Expense — only when required**

That is enough to price nearly the entire one-stop model.

---

# 4. Recommended Client-Facing Pricing Structure

## A. Transactions — Fixed Per File

Keep transaction pricing highly visible and simple.

Current approved foundation:

- Prepaid Transaction Management: **$389**
- Pay at Closing: **$599**

These are already easy for a Realtor to understand.

Do not clutter the public page with prices for every internal transaction task.

The Realtor buys the managed file, not deadline tracking, lender follow-up, title communication, document organization, and closing prep as separate line items.

---

## B. Listings — One Koinonia Management Fee + Outside Costs

This is where the simplification principle matters most.

The Realtor should not see:

- photographer fee;
- drone coordination fee;
- sign coordination fee;
- flyer fee;
- lockbox coordination fee;
- vendor scheduling fee;
- media upload fee;
- open-house setup coordination fee;
- separate listing launch admin fee;
- separate seller update fee.

Instead, Koinonia should sell a clearly defined **Listing Coordination / Listing Management fee** for the Koinonia work.

Then, when needed:

- photography = additional approved outside cost;
- drone = additional approved outside cost;
- video = additional approved outside cost;
- staging = additional approved outside cost;
- print/postage = additional approved outside cost;
- paid ads = additional approved media spend;
- specialty contractor/service = additional approved outside cost.

Koinonia handles sourcing/coordination where included in the listing service.

### Recommended product direction

Keep the number of listing prices small.

Potential final structure:

**Listing Launch** — one flat Koinonia fee  
**Full Listing Management** — one flat Koinonia fee  
**Hand Us the Listing** — one integrated Koinonia fee or simple combination rule

Do not add a separate public fee for every capability inside those products.

Final dollar amounts should be set after scope and workload are finalized.

---

## C. Licensed Field Coverage — Simple Base Pricing With Clear Variables

Field work is the area where a single universal fixed price may not be realistic because time, distance, notice, number of stops, and assignment duration materially change fulfillment cost.

The website should still stay simple.

Recommended presentation:

> **Licensed field coverage starting at $X**

or

> **Simple per-assignment pricing. Final price is confirmed before coverage is booked.**

Then explain that price may vary for:

- distance;
- short notice;
- evenings/weekends/holidays;
- multi-property tours;
- extended property attendance;
- special access requirements.

Do not publish a 20-row mileage/time matrix unless operational data later proves it materially improves conversion.

Open houses can remain a fixed event price when the standard scope is predictable, with exceptions quoted for extended/custom requirements.

---

## D. Marketing — Management Fee + Media / Production Costs When Needed

Marketing should use the same philosophy as listings.

The Realtor buys Koinonia's managed marketing work.

Examples included in the Koinonia fee may include:

- strategy;
- copy;
- content planning;
- graphic/content production;
- email campaigns;
- scheduling/publishing;
- listing campaign execution;
- reporting;
- coordination.

Expenses that vary should stay separate:

- ad spend;
- professional photography;
- drone/video capture;
- printing;
- postage;
- premium specialty production;
- specialty website/software costs when required.

### Recommended recurring structure

Prefer a small number of monthly marketing choices rather than pricing every deliverable.

Example architecture to test:

- **Marketing Essentials**
- **Marketing Partner**
- **Marketing Office**

Exact final tier names and prices remain under review.

The public page should focus on the outcome and included management level, not the internal cost of every asset.

---

## E. CRM & Business Operations — Monthly or Defined Project

Avoid making Realtors buy individual hours for every routine task unless needed internally for capacity control.

Two clean ways to buy:

### Defined project

Examples:

- CRM cleanup;
- database migration;
- workflow/SOP build;
- database reactivation project;
- system setup.

Koinonia gives one quoted project fee before work begins.

### Monthly partnership

For ongoing work, use a small number of recurring levels with clear scope/capacity.

Hours may remain an **internal capacity guardrail**, but the website should sell the result and support level rather than leading with “3 hours / 7 hours / 12 hours” unless those hours are necessary to set customer expectations.

The final recurring architecture should be evaluated alongside the broader Realtor Management Partnership so Koinonia does not end up with overlapping monthly plans.

---

# 5. Front-End Pricing Should Answer Only Three Questions

A Realtor visiting the pricing page primarily wants to know:

1. **What does Koinonia charge for its service?**
2. **What kinds of costs could be additional?**
3. **Will I know the total before you commit my money?**

The website does not need to explain Koinonia's vendor accounting model.

Recommended general note:

> **Outside expenses such as professional media, advertising spend, printing/postage, staging, and specialty services are not included unless specifically stated. Koinonia coordinates these services and confirms the applicable cost with you before purchase.**

---

# 6. Recommended Website Pricing Presentation

Do not put a price under every capability card.

Use the Services page to explain **what Koinonia can do**.

Use a separate Pricing / Ways to Work With Us section to explain the commercial model.

Working layout:

## Transaction Management

**$389 prepaid**  
**$599 pay at closing**

Short scope statement.

## Listing Support

**Flat Koinonia management fee — final product price to be set**

Short statement:

> Media and other property-specific outside expenses are additional when needed and approved before purchase.

## Licensed Field Coverage

**Starting at $X / assignment — final rate to be set**

Short statement about distance/timing/assignment scope.

## Marketing

**Monthly plans / project pricing — final packages to be set**

Short statement:

> Advertising spend, print/postage and specialty production are additional when used.

## Ongoing Business Support

**Monthly partnership pricing — final architecture to be set**

Short statement about recurring support and broader Realtor Management options.

## Something Else?

> **Tell us what you need. We will confirm a clear Koinonia fee and any required outside cost before work begins.**

---

# 7. The Strongest Simplicity Rule

The website should rarely require the Realtor to mentally add multiple Koinonia fees together for one outcome.

Example:

Bad customer experience:

- listing coordination $X
- photographer coordination $Y
- sign coordination $Z
- marketing coordination $A
- open-house coordination $B
- seller update fee $C

Better customer experience:

> **Full Listing Management — $X Koinonia fee**

Then only genuinely variable outside costs are additional.

That is the core pricing simplification principle.

---

# 8. Coordination Fee Versus Pass-Through Cost

Internally, every integrated product should separate:

## Koinonia Revenue

What Koinonia earns for:

- intake;
- project ownership;
- scheduling;
- communication;
- coordination;
- direct production;
- quality control;
- follow-through;
- accountability.

## Outside Cost

Money required to purchase something from outside Koinonia, such as:

- professional media;
- advertising inventory;
- printing/postage;
- staging;
- repairs/contractor services;
- specialty software or specialist execution.

This distinction should be clear in accounting even when the customer experience feels like one coordinated Koinonia engagement.

---

# 9. Recommended Pricing Reset

Before setting more individual prices, pause and redesign the pricing catalog around the simplified architecture.

Recommended order:

1. Finalize the exact products beneath the five service umbrellas.
2. Decide which products are fixed-fee versus recurring versus variable field assignment.
3. Decide the standard Koinonia management fee for each product.
4. Identify which outside costs can occur within each product.
5. Define approval/payment treatment for those outside costs.
6. Remove unnecessary micro-fees and overlapping charges.
7. Reconcile existing $389 / $599 transaction rules.
8. Review standalone Contract/Document Support compensation structure before carrying forward its current public price.
9. Build a client-facing pricing page with only the small number of prices needed to make the buying decision easy.

---

# 10. Working Owner Recommendation

Adopt the following pricing philosophy:

> **Simple Koinonia fee. Variable outside costs only when the job requires them. No scavenger hunt. No nickel-and-diming.**

For integrated work, Koinonia should earn its money through a clearly understood management/service fee rather than layering multiple coordination charges or quietly marking up every outside provider.

The Realtor should experience Koinonia as the company that gets the work handled — not as a referral directory or a billing wrapper around a pile of vendors.
