# Hand Us the Listing — Public Claim & Fulfillment Readiness

Date: 2026-09-03  
Owner: Koinonia  
Status: Controlled Launch Standard  
Applies to: OBJ-00000015 — Listing & Seller Support; OBJ-00000020 — Hand Us the Listing; OBJ-00000025 — Hand Us the Listing Pricing Rule

## Decision

**Hand Us the Listing is approved for controlled public launch at $350 per standard listing, provided the client-specific brokerage/MLS/advertising access gates below are followed.**

Koinonia does **not** need universal direct MLS access in order to sell or fulfill this product.

The product operates in one of two approved MLS modes:

1. **Authorized Direct-Access Mode** — Koinonia uses its own approved office-admin/assistant/other authorized account and brokerage-granted permissions to prepare, input, or maintain listing data as permitted.
2. **Broker-Submit Mode** — Koinonia prepares the complete listing-launch/update packet and the Realtor, managing broker, participant, or authorized office administrator performs the MLS submission/change.

**Shared Realtor/MLS credentials are not an approved fulfillment method.**

This two-mode model removes direct MLS access as a universal launch dependency while preserving the white-glove customer experience.

# 2. Public Claims We Must Not Make

Do not state or imply:

- “Koinonia is your listing brokerage.”
- “Koinonia lists the property for you” when that wording could imply the brokerage relationship belongs to Koinonia.
- “We can directly enter/edit every MLS.”
- “Just send us your MLS password.”
- “We handle seller pricing and strategy.”
- “We negotiate for the seller.”
- “We make disclosure/material-fact decisions.”
- “We independently approve advertising, listing data, price/status changes, or showing instructions.”
- “Everything is included” or “unlimited.”
- licensed field attendance is included in the $350 listing fee unless a later written package expressly adds an allowance.

The correct distinction is:

> **Koinonia manages the operational work around the Realtor's listing; the Realtor and brokerage retain the brokerage relationship, professional judgment, and required approvals.**

# 3. Regulatory / MLS Basis for the Operating Model

Current Colorado and REcolorado source checks support the controlled model above.

## Colorado REAP framework

Colorado Division of Real Estate Commission Position 20 recognizes real-estate administrative professionals, including transaction coordinators/managers and assistants, and permits substantial clerical/administrative support under broker direction. Examples include gathering listing information, distributing broker-prepared objective property information, administrative transaction tasks, scheduling repairs/services when authorized, and certain property access/showing/open-house activity when properly authorized.

The controlling distinction remains whether an activity is administrative/ministerial versus Real Estate Brokerage Services requiring the appropriate license, brokerage relationship, authority, and supervision.

Operational consequence:

- Koinonia may coordinate and perform approved administrative listing work.
- Realtor/brokerage decisions must remain with the Realtor/brokerage.
- licensed/brokerage activity cannot be converted into ordinary admin work merely by putting it inside the Koinonia package.

## Written listing / brokerage authority

Colorado Rule 6.14 requires seller listing contracts to be in writing before Real Estate Brokerage Services are performed.

Operational consequence:

Before Koinonia publishes/coordinates a live listing or performs work that depends on the active listing relationship, intake must confirm that the Realtor/brokerage has the required seller listing authority in place.

Koinonia does not independently determine whether a brokerage agreement is legally sufficient; the Realtor/brokerage remains responsible for that determination.

## Advertising identity and authority

Colorado Rule 6.10 requires brokerage advertising to identify the Brokerage Firm and prohibits misleading advertising about the Broker or Brokerage Firm. It also places responsibility on the Broker/Brokerage Firm for advertising accuracy, including third-party electronic advertising performed on the broker's behalf.

Operational consequence:

- property advertising produced by Koinonia must be based on approved listing facts;
- client/brokerage identity and required attribution must be preserved;
- Koinonia must not present itself as the listing brokerage when it is not;
- property-specific marketing should normally publish through Realtor/brokerage-authorized channels;
- use of Koinonia-owned public channels for a client's property is not the default and requires written permission plus required brokerage attribution/compliance.

## REcolorado access

REcolorado states that Incomplete listings are accessible to the listing agent, participant/managing broker, or office admins. REcolorado also states that brokers, agents, admins, and assistants should use their own Matrix accounts, with authorized impersonation/delegated access where available.

Operational consequence:

- no password sharing;
- direct listing input/edit is available only through Koinonia's own properly authorized access path;
- otherwise Broker-Submit Mode is used.

## Property photos/video

Colorado DRE recommends clear seller/landlord authorization for interior photography/video and controlled use of images because of privacy/security concerns.

Operational consequence:

Koinonia coordinates professional media under Realtor/seller-approved authority and uses/distributes property media only within the approved marketing purpose and permissions.

# 4. Client Activation Gate — Required Before Work Begins

Every Hand Us the Listing engagement must capture the following minimum client/listing setup.

## Realtor / brokerage

- Realtor name and contact information;
- brokerage legal/trade name;
- managing/employing broker or office contact when applicable;
- Realtor confirmation that use of Koinonia support is permitted by their brokerage/office policy;
- brokerage-specific checklist, compliance requirements, and required approvals supplied or identified where applicable.

## Listing authority

Realtor confirms that the brokerage has the required written seller listing authority before Koinonia performs live-listing/advertising activity that depends upon that authority.

## MLS operating mode

Select one:

### A. Authorized Direct-Access Mode

Record:

- MLS/platform;
- Koinonia user's own account/access type;
- office/admin/assistant/delegation or impersonation permission;
- scope of allowed actions;
- any brokerage-required final approval before activation/change.

### B. Broker-Submit Mode

Record:

- person responsible for final MLS submission;
- preferred handoff format;
- expected review/turnaround method;
- change-request workflow after launch.

Never request or store a Realtor's personal MLS password as the operating model.

## Communication authority

Record whether Koinonia may communicate directly with:

- seller;
- photographer/media provider;
- sign/lockbox provider;
- stager;
- cleaner/contractor/landscaper;
- showing service;
- other approved provider.

Direct seller communication defaults to administrative scheduling, information collection, and approved status/process communication. Pricing strategy, negotiation, disclosure judgment, legal interpretation, and other professional advice escalate to Realtor.

## Spending authority

Record one of:

- approval required before every outside purchase;
- standing authorization up to a defined amount/category;
- approved vendor/budget schedule.

Outside costs remain separately identifiable.

## Marketing approval

Capture:

- brokerage/Realtor brand assets;
- required brokerage attribution/disclosures;
- approved property facts;
- marketing channels;
- final approval method;
- seller/media usage restrictions if any.

# 5. Standard Fulfillment Workflow — Launch Ready

## Stage 1 — Listing intake

1. Create listing engagement record.
2. Confirm Realtor/brokerage and authority gate.
3. Select MLS operating mode.
4. Collect property/listing information.
5. Collect/organize seller disclosures and listing documents supplied by Realtor/seller.
6. Record brokerage checklist and approval points.
7. Build launch timeline.

### Koinonia owns

Organization, checklist, missing-item follow-up, timeline, coordination.

### Realtor/brokerage owns

Listing relationship, legal/compliance sufficiency, pricing decisions, disclosure/material-fact judgment, professional advice.

---

## Stage 2 — Property preparation / vendor management

Koinonia may coordinate approved needs such as:

- photography;
- video/drone/floor plan/3D;
- staging;
- cleaning;
- repairs/handyman;
- landscaping;
- sign;
- lockbox;
- specialty listing services.

Required control:

- confirm material outside cost before purchase unless standing authority exists;
- document appointment/access responsibility;
- escalate scope/cost/property-condition decisions to Realtor.

Koinonia's $350 fee covers normal coordination effort; the outside provider invoice does not become a hidden Koinonia micro-fee.

---

## Stage 3 — MLS / launch preparation

Koinonia prepares:

- organized listing data;
- property description draft from approved facts;
- media ordering/upload plan;
- documents/disclosures supplied for upload;
- virtual-tour/property links;
- showing instructions supplied/approved by Realtor;
- showing-service setup information;
- launch QA checklist.

Then:

- Direct-Access Mode: authorized Koinonia user performs permitted input/change and routes required final approval; or
- Broker-Submit Mode: Koinonia sends a complete submission/update packet to the authorized Realtor/office admin.

The product promise is management of the workflow—not universal ownership of the MLS login.

---

## Stage 4 — Base listing marketing

Standard $350 product includes the Koinonia coordination/production layer for a defined base listing-marketing set:

- approved property messaging/copy;
- standard Just Listed social asset/post package;
- standard email/e-flyer listing announcement asset;
- standard flyer/open-house asset when applicable;
- QR/tracked property link when useful;
- open-house promotional asset when an open house is ordered;
- normal status/lifecycle asset support as defined by the current product scope.

Controls:

- use approved property facts only;
- Realtor/brokerage approval before publication where required;
- preserve brokerage attribution;
- do not independently change list price, property facts, or material representations;
- do not imply Koinonia is the listing brokerage.

Marketing Management clients should not be double-charged for the standard listing-marketing layer already included in Hand Us the Listing.

---

## Stage 5 — Active-listing support

Koinonia may manage:

- showing-feedback collection/organization;
- feedback summaries;
- routine seller-update preparation from approved information;
- approved status/price/media/showing-instruction change workflow;
- vendor/property follow-up;
- listing refresh checklist;
- back-on-market administrative reset;
- withdrawal/expiration administrative closeout;
- sign/lockbox removal coordination.

Realtor approval is required for decisions involving pricing, strategy, negotiation, material seller communication, disclosure/condition judgment, and other brokerage decisions.

---

## Stage 6 — Open house / field support

Hand Us the Listing includes normal coordination and base promotional work for an ordered open house.

The actual licensed host/field assignment remains separately priced under Licensed Field Coverage unless a future written package includes it.

Do not blur this boundary merely to make the listing package sound larger.

---

## Stage 7 — Accepted-offer handoff

When an offer is accepted:

1. close the listing-side launch/active-listing checklist;
2. carry forward seller/property/contact/document information already captured;
3. start Transaction Management under its current pricing rule;
4. do not charge a duplicate Koinonia intake/setup fee;
5. preserve one continuous client experience.
