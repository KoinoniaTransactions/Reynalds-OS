# Koinonia Service Expansion Study — 2026-09-02

Status: Research / Decision Proposal — NOT CANONICAL  
Owner: Koinonia  
Department: Operations  
Purpose: Determine which additional Realtor work Koinonia should absorb without diluting the established "real estate operations partner" positioning.

> This document is research and a proposed operating direction. It does not authorize new service claims, pricing, brokerage activity, or paid advertising. Canonical service objects remain the source of truth until specifically updated and approved.

---

## Executive Conclusion

Koinonia should not respond to market opportunity by creating a long list of disconnected services.

The strongest expansion is structural:

1. **Create one genuine new service family: Listing Operations.**
2. **Broaden Licensed Showing Coverage into Licensed Field Coverage.**
3. **Keep Professional Open House Coverage as a productized field-coverage offering rather than a separate foundational service family.**
4. **Turn lead/database follow-up, post-close care, and recurring client follow-through into defined modules inside Business Operations Support.**
5. **Offer Vacation / Overflow Coverage as a cross-service package, not a new service family.**
6. **Do not activate generic physical Closing Coverage until the brokerage-designation, written-agreement, responsibility, E&O, and workflow requirements are specifically resolved. Continue using "Closing Preparation" as the launch-safe marketing claim until then.**

Proposed eventual five-family architecture:

1. Transaction Management
2. Contract Preparation & Writing
3. Listing Operations — NEW
4. Licensed Field Coverage — EXPANDED / RENAMED from Licensed Showing Coverage
5. Business Operations Support

This preserves the established brand position: **Koinonia is a real estate operations partner, not merely a transaction coordinator and not a task marketplace.**

# 1. Existing Koinonia Foundation

The current canonical Service Catalog contains four production-certified service objects:

- Transaction Management
- Contract Preparation & Writing
- Licensed Showing Coverage
- Business Operations Support

Transaction Management already covers contract-to-close coordination, deadline management, communication coordination, transaction organization, compliance tracking, document management, status updates, closing coordination, and post-closing file completion.

Business Operations Support already covers administrative task coordination, CRM cleanup and follow-up tracking, client communication drafting, checklist/process management, business file organization, template management, calendar/task support, reporting, workflow documentation, and operational improvement.

Licensed Showing Coverage already contains provider-assignment architecture, availability/location/licensing criteria, access verification, completion records, feedback, and provider compensation/revenue tracking. It was therefore designed as an operational coverage system rather than only a personal showing service performed by one individual.

## Current canonical/public mismatch

The public Koinonia website currently presents five support paths and already markets **Professional Open House Coverage** as a $200 standalone service, while the canonical Service Catalog still contains only four child service objects.

The public site currently describes:

- Transaction Support
- Contract & Document Support
- Licensed Showing Coverage
- Professional Open House Coverage
- Monthly Operations Partnership

This mismatch should be reconciled after this study is approved. The existence of Professional Open House Coverage in public production does not, by itself, justify creating Open House Coverage as a foundational service family. It fits more cleanly as a productized capability within a broader licensed field-support family.

# 2. Research Question

The correct question is not:

> What other services can Koinonia sell?

The better question is:

> What work does a producing Colorado Realtor repeatedly have to do that competes with prospecting, relationships, negotiation, and selling — and which of those responsibilities can Koinonia responsibly take ownership of, systematize, and fulfill at a profit?

Each opportunity was evaluated against the following factors:

- Realtor time burden
- Frequency of need
- Willingness to outsource/pay
- Fit with Koinonia's established positioning
- Ability to systematize
- Ability to fulfill beyond Jeremiah personally
- Legal/licensing complexity
- Liability / professional-judgment exposure
- Competitive differentiation
- Cross-sell value into recurring Koinonia relationships
- Whether the work belongs under an existing service family or justifies a genuinely new one

# 3. Colorado Regulatory Guardrails

This is an operational summary, not legal advice. Final service design must be reviewed against the client's brokerage policies, Koinonia's licensing structure, E&O coverage, written agreements, and current Colorado law/rules.

## 3.1 Colorado expressly recognizes REAP / transaction-coordinator work

Colorado Real Estate Commission Position 20 identifies Real Estate Administrative Professionals (REAPs) as including assistants and transaction coordinators/managers.

The Commission's current task list for unlicensed REAPs and licensed REAPs not acting as a broker in the transaction includes, among other activities:

- completing forms prepared for and directed by the broker as a scrivener;
- distributing objective property information prepared by the broker;
- gathering listing information;
- if authorized by the seller or listing broker, providing property access and conducting showings or open houses;
- completing administrative tasks needed to help brokers fulfill uniform duties;
- sending disclosures;
- ordering title commitments and distributing contract copies;
- collecting due diligence documents;
- reviewing a transaction file for missing documents and office-policy compliance;
- preparing market analyses for broker approval/submission with required disclosure;
- collecting/receipting certain funds; and
- scheduling repairs or property services when an existing agreement authorizes the broker to do so.

This is important because it confirms that the operational opportunity is broader than traditional contract-to-close paperwork.

## 3.2 August 12, 2026 written-agreement change

Colorado law changed effective August 12, 2026. Current guidance states that a licensee must have a written agreement establishing the brokerage relationship and conspicuously disclosing compensation before performing activities that require a real estate license for the consumer.

This creates a design requirement for any Koinonia service that crosses from ministerial/administrative support into licensed brokerage services for a consumer.

## 3.3 Closing attendance is a special category

Colorado practice rules provide that when a broker has a brokerage relationship with a consumer, the broker must review closing documents and attend closing or be reasonably available. If that broker is unavailable, another broker designated by the brokerage firm may review/attend on the broker's behalf and assumes joint responsibility described by the rule.

Therefore Koinonia should **not** casually promise generic "Closing Coverage" as though it were comparable to sending someone to unlock a property.

A future closing-attendance service would require a specific approved model addressing at least:

- designation by the client's brokerage firm;
- written consumer/brokerage relationship requirements where applicable;
- authority and responsibility for review;
- escalation paths;
- brokerage office-policy compatibility;
- E&O coverage;
- compensation and documentation.

Until that operating model is formally approved, marketing should continue to use **Closing Preparation** rather than physical Closing Coverage.

# 5. Competitive / Market Evidence

## 5.1 Colorado listing coordination is already a paid category

Midnight Orchid TC Colorado currently offers Listing Coordination at **$200/listing**, including listing paperwork, seller disclosures/signatures, MLS input support, photo/sign/lockbox coordination, brokerage listing compliance, listing-date tracking, and under-contract handoff.

Colorado RESS markets pre-listing through post-closing support including photographer scheduling, sign/lockbox installation, MLS input, showing-service setup, virtual-tour/photo/disclosure uploads, listing changes, feedback collection, marketing, CMAs, open houses, and other administrative/field services.

Market implication:

**Listing Operations is not an invented service category. Agents already understand and purchase it.** Koinonia can differentiate by connecting it to field coverage, transaction management, contract support, and recurring operations rather than selling it as isolated listing data entry.

## 5.2 The field-support category is broader than showings

Showami currently markets licensed/on-demand support for:

- buyer showings
- open houses
- inspection access
- appraisal-related requests
- property photography / condition reports
- lockbox runners
- pre-listing walk-throughs
- property checks
- final-walk-through/task requests

ShowingMarket similarly describes request types including buyer showings, open houses, rental tours, lockbox runs, photo visits, and inspection access.

Market implication:

The phrase **Licensed Showing Coverage** unnecessarily narrows a service architecture that could responsibly encompass multiple property-based operational tasks.

Koinonia should not become a task marketplace. It can, however, offer a relationship-based licensed field-coverage layer and use internal providers or third-party platforms as fulfillment resources when appropriate.

## 5.3 Post-close / CRM operations are an emerging concierge category

Ophir Solutions in Denver markets post-closing client relationship management including anniversary reminders, review campaigns, nurture sequences, referral engagement, and branded client touchpoints.

Other real-estate operations providers sell CRM cleanup, pipeline organization, lead follow-up systems, review requests, database reactivation, and post-close communication.

Community discussions among Realtors repeatedly describe:

- transaction coordination consuming evenings;
- administrative work reducing prospecting time;
- leads getting buried in disconnected systems;
- inconsistent CRM follow-up;
- the need for a documented workflow rather than simply more software.

Market implication:

Koinonia's existing Business Operations Support is correctly positioned, but it should become **more concrete and productized** so Realtors immediately understand what recurring support actually removes from their workload.

# 7. Recommended Service Architecture

## Family 1 — Transaction Management

Keep the current canonical family.

Primary promise:

**Once a client is under contract, Koinonia keeps the file organized and moving through closing.**

Capabilities include:

- file opening and validation
- timeline/deadline management
- title/lender/party coordination
- due diligence tracking
- inspection/appraisal coordination
- repair documentation follow-up
- closing preparation
- post-close file completion

Do not dilute this family by moving pre-listing work into it.

---

## Family 2 — Contract Preparation & Writing

Keep the current canonical family.

Primary promise:

**The Realtor provides the business terms and professional decisions; Koinonia turns those instructions into organized, review-ready documents.**

No major structural expansion needed at this time.

---

## Family 3 — Listing Operations — NEW

### Proposed mission

Take ownership of the repeatable operational work from signed listing engagement through listing launch, active-listing administration, and accepted-offer handoff so listing agents can focus on pricing strategy, seller relationships, negotiation, and generating the next listing.

### Proposed lifecycle

Seller / Listing Intake → Documents & Property Information → Launch Plan → Vendors / Photography / Sign / Lockbox → MLS / Showing Setup → Go Live → Active Listing Operations → Open House / Feedback Support → Accepted Offer Handoff

### Proposed included capabilities

- seller/listing intake checklist
- listing document organization and signature follow-up under approved boundaries
- seller disclosure collection and organization
- property-information gathering
- photographer scheduling
- staging / vendor scheduling as directed
- sign installation/removal coordination
- lockbox installation/removal coordination
- MLS draft/input support subject to brokerage/MLS permissions
- photo, disclosure, and virtual-tour upload support
- showing-platform setup
- listing launch QA checklist
- listing-live confirmation to agent/seller
- open-house scheduling and materials coordination
- listing-specific flyer/social asset preparation using agent-approved information
- showing-feedback collection
- weekly seller-update preparation
- price/status changes at Realtor direction and within system permissions
- accepted-offer handoff into Transaction Management

### Explicit exclusions

- listing strategy
- pricing opinion presented as Koinonia's professional judgment
- negotiation
- seller advice
- brokerage supervision
- unauthorized MLS changes
- independent legal/document interpretation

### Why this deserves its own service family

This is transaction-specific operational work with a clear start/end state, commonly purchased per listing, and materially different from ongoing general Business Operations Support.

---

## Family 4 — Licensed Field Coverage — EXPANDED

### Proposed mission

Provide dependable in-person real-estate field coverage when an agent cannot physically be at a property, while protecting the agent/client relationship and following clear role boundaries.

### Proposed productized capabilities

**Launch-ready / strongest candidates**

- Buyer Showing Coverage
- Professional Open House Coverage
- Inspection Access / Attendance Coverage
- Appraisal / Vendor Access Coverage
- Lockbox Pickup / Drop-Off / Installation Tasks
- Sign Placement / Pickup Tasks where permitted and practical
- Property Photo / Documentation Visit with explicit permission/instructions
- Pre-Listing Property Walk-Through / Information Capture under broker direction
- Property Check / Vacancy Documentation under broker direction

**Needs additional operating-policy review before public launch**

- Final Walk-Through Coverage
- Closing Attendance / Closing Coverage
- Any field request involving advice, negotiation, condition/value opinion, or independent professional judgment

### Key design rule

**Koinonia owns the service relationship; fulfillment source is an internal operational choice.**

The Realtor should not have to care whether a request is fulfilled by Jeremiah, another Koinonia-approved provider, or an approved third-party coverage platform, provided the agreed service standard, licensing/authority requirements, confidentiality, and documentation are maintained.

Koinonia should not market itself as a marketplace.

# 8. New Cross-Service Package: Vacation / Overflow Coverage

Vacation Coverage should not become its own service family.

It is a packaged promise that activates whatever approved Koinonia capabilities are needed while the Realtor is unavailable.

Potential coverage menu:

- transaction monitoring
- deadline watch
- contract/document preparation from agent instructions
- showing coverage
- open-house coverage
- inspection/property access
- CRM/task triage
- client/vendor communication support within approved scripts/authority
- urgent-work escalation

Positioning concept:

> **Your business doesn't stop because you finally took a day off.**

The package must clearly define what Koinonia can resolve, what it can only coordinate, and what still requires the responsible Realtor or brokerage.

This package is strategically strong because it sells the value of the entire operations relationship rather than a single task.

# 9. Pricing Research and Initial Hypotheses

No pricing below is approved. It is a starting point for unit-economics work.

## Listing Operations

Observed Colorado competitor benchmark:

- approximately $200/listing for a core listing-coordination package from a current Colorado provider.

Broader 2025–2026 listing-coordination research shows common per-listing pricing roughly in the $150–$329 range depending on scope/rush level.

Koinonia hypothesis:

- do **not** compete as the cheapest MLS-entry provider;
- target a more complete operational launch package;
- likely starting test range: **$249–$299 per listing**;
- create a bundle discount when paired with Transaction Management;
- evaluate a "Listing-to-Close" bundle rather than making agents assemble every component manually.

## Licensed Field Coverage

Current Koinonia:

- showing coverage starting at $50;
- professional open house standalone $200 up to 3 hours.

Future field-task pricing should use a consistent formula based on:

- provider labor/time
- drive distance
- expected on-site duration
- urgency / same-day request
- licensing/authority requirements
- Koinonia coordination margin
- platform/provider cost when outsourced

Avoid one flat price for every field request.

## Business Operations modules

Current monthly tiers already provide a sound container:

- Starter $299 / month
- Growth $599 / month
- Partner $999 / month

Before changing those tiers, first clarify deliverable modules and measure actual hours/capacity.

# 11. What Koinonia Should Not Become

## Not a generic task marketplace

Third-party field platforms may be useful fulfillment infrastructure, but Koinonia's customer value is the trusted operations relationship.

## Not a generic virtual-assistant company

Business Operations Support should remain real-estate-specific, structured, visible, and outcome-driven.

## Not a social-media agency

Listing-specific marketing execution is a natural extension of Listing Operations. Full-scale social-media management would create a materially different business and should not be allowed to distract from the core operating model.

## Not a lead-selling company

Koinonia can improve lead handling and follow-through without becoming a lead generator/reseller.

## Not a substitute for the responsible Realtor/brokerage

Advice, negotiation, strategy, brokerage supervision, and professional judgment remain with the responsible Realtor/brokerage unless a specifically approved brokerage relationship and operating model says otherwise.
