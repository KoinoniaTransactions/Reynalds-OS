# Koinonia Standard Product Scopes — 2026-09-03

Status: Working Recommendation / Owner Review Required  
Owner: Koinonia  
Purpose: Define the standard scope, inclusions, boundaries, outside expenses, and scope-change triggers for the five proposed Koinonia products before final pricing is established.

This document is a commercial product-definition artifact. It does not replace canonical service objects, pricing rules, brokerage/legal review, or detailed internal SOPs until approved.

---

# 1. Product Scope Philosophy

Koinonia should price **outcomes and managed responsibility**, not individual clicks, phone calls, emails, or routine coordination steps.

A Realtor should not wonder whether every normal action creates another fee.

## Standard-scope rule

When a client purchases a Koinonia product, the standard Koinonia fee should cover the **normal direct work, coordination, follow-through, and quality control reasonably required to deliver that product as defined here**.

That does not mean:

- unlimited work;
- unlimited revisions;
- unlimited properties or appointments;
- unlimited rush requests;
- unlimited creative production;
- unlimited licensed field time;
- unlimited vendor costs;
- unlimited advertising spend;
- unlimited custom systems work;
- work outside legal/brokerage authority.

## Scope-change rule

Do not nickel-and-dime clients for routine work.

If a request becomes **materially different from the standard product**, Koinonia should pause only the expanded portion and confirm the revised scope or price before proceeding.

Common scope-change triggers:

- substantially more properties/stops than a standard assignment;
- unusually long field attendance;
- exceptional distance/travel;
- same-day/rush fulfillment that changes staffing cost;
- repeated major revisions caused by changed client direction;
- custom work outside the defined product outcome;
- unusually complex/multi-office brokerage requirements;
- a marketing project materially larger than the agreed recurring scope;
- a partnership client consistently using capacity beyond the agreed service level.

The client should experience this as **clear scope management**, not surprise add-on billing.

# 2. Outside Expense Rule Across All Products

Koinonia fees pay for Koinonia's work.

Outside costs required to purchase third-party goods/services are separate unless a written product/package specifically says otherwise.

Typical outside expenses:

- professional photography;
- drone/video;
- floor plans / 3D tours;
- staging;
- repairs / contractors;
- sign fabrication/installation when externally sourced;
- printing;
- postage;
- paid advertising/media spend;
- specialty software purchased specifically for the client;
- premium specialist production;
- other approved third-party property or marketing services.

## Client-facing treatment

Do not frame the experience as:

> “Here are all the vendors you have to pay and manage.”

Use:

> **Koinonia coordinates the work. Any outside expense needed for your property or campaign is confirmed with you before purchase.**

## Default accounting recommendation

- No hidden vendor markup by default.
- Koinonia earns margin through its visible service/management fee.
- Outside expense should be separately identifiable internally even if Koinonia facilitates payment.
- Realtor approval is required before committing material outside spend unless a standing written authorization/budget exists.

# 3. Product 1 — Transaction Management

## Product outcome

A complete, professionally organized contract-to-close coordination process that keeps deadlines, documents, participants, and closing preparation moving while the Realtor retains professional judgment and client representation.

## Standard start point

Koinonia receives an executed contract or another approved trigger establishing an active transaction file.

## Standard end point

Closing/possession workflow is completed and the transaction file is organized/closed out according to the approved process.

## Included by default

### File intake and setup

- receive executed contract/file;
- create transaction record/file structure;
- identify transaction parties and key contacts;
- extract contract dates/deadlines;
- create transaction timeline/checklist;
- identify obvious missing required documents/information;
- organize received transaction documents.

### Deadline and milestone management

- track contractual deadlines;
- maintain milestone checklist;
- surface approaching deadlines;
- send routine reminders/follow-up;
- escalate exceptions to Realtor.

### Participant coordination

Routine administrative coordination with approved participants such as:

- title/closing company;
- lender;
- inspector;
- appraisal process contacts;
- HOA/document providers;
- insurance contact at client/Realtor direction;
- approved repair/service providers.

### Document administration

- track required documents/signatures;
- identify missing items;
- organize received documents;
- route approved documents/information;
- support amendments/addenda/notices from Realtor direction where lawfully included in the approved operating model;
- maintain version/file organization.

### Inspection / appraisal / repair administration

- inspection scheduling coordination when requested;
- deadline tracking;
- appraisal status follow-up;
- repair/vendor scheduling coordination;
- repair documentation follow-up;
- surface exceptions requiring Realtor decision.

### Closing preparation

- coordinate closing scheduling information;
- closing-preparation checklist;
- final-walkthrough scheduling (not representation at walk-through unless separately approved/licensed);
- possession/key logistics coordination where within approved scope;
- closing reminders;
- final file completion/archive.

### Routine communication

- Realtor status updates;
- routine participant follow-up;
- routine milestone confirmations;
- approved client-update drafts/templates where applicable.

## Included-volume principle

The standard transaction fee should cover **normal coordination volume for one transaction**, not a count of emails, phone calls, reminders, or standard document touches.

Do not charge per reminder, deadline, lender follow-up, title email, inspection scheduling message, or routine status update.

## Not included / separate scope

- legal advice;
- negotiation strategy;
- choosing contract terms for Realtor/client;
- brokerage supervision;
- substantive client representation decisions;
- property showings/field attendance;
- open houses;
- extraordinary dispute/crisis management beyond coordination/escalation;
- litigation/legal claims support;
- nonstandard ancillary projects unrelated to the transaction;
- outside vendor/service costs.

## Scope-change examples

Potential revised/custom scope may apply if:

- transaction has extraordinary complexity beyond normal residential coordination;
- multiple related contracts/properties materially expand work;
- institutional/commercial/new-construction process creates materially different workload;
- repeated restarts or unusual dispute administration substantially changes the engagement.

Do not use these exceptions to create routine add-on charges.

## Contract/document preparation

Contract/document preparation remains an important Koinonia capability, but exact compensation structure remains subject to brokerage/legal review.

Product design principle:

- do not charge a separate micro-fee inside a managed transaction merely because a standard amendment/addendum must be prepared from Realtor instructions if the approved compensation model allows it to be included;
- standalone pre-contract document requests require separate approved compensation architecture before final public pricing.

# 4. Product 2 — Hand Us the Listing

## Product outcome

A managed listing-side operational engagement from listing intake through launch and active-listing support to accepted-offer handoff, so the Realtor does not personally project-manage every administrative, vendor, field, and marketing detail.

## Standard start point

Realtor submits a new listing engagement with sufficient authority/information for Koinonia to begin approved work.

## Standard end point

One of the following occurs:

- accepted offer is handed into Transaction Management;
- listing expires/withdraws/terminates and approved closeout is completed;
- engagement is otherwise ended by the Realtor under agreed terms.

## Included by default

### Listing intake and launch planning

- seller/property intake organization;
- property information collection checklist;
- listing-document/disclosure organization;
- missing-item follow-up;
- listing launch timeline/checklist;
- brokerage/listing requirements tracking based on approved client setup;
- Realtor decision/approval points surfaced clearly.

### Property-preparation coordination

Koinonia coordinates approved needs such as:

- photography/media scheduling;
- staging consultation scheduling;
- cleaning coordination;
- repair/handyman coordination;
- landscaping coordination;
- sign coordination;
- lockbox coordination;
- floor-plan / 3D / drone / video coordination.

The Koinonia listing fee includes normal coordination effort. The actual outside provider expense is additional.

### MLS / listing-launch administration

Within approved brokerage/MLS permissions and Realtor direction:

- listing-data organization;
- MLS draft/input support;
- property-description drafting from approved facts;
- photo ordering/upload coordination;
- document/disclosure upload coordination;
- virtual-tour/link setup;
- showing-service setup;
- showing instructions from Realtor/seller direction;
- syndication/listing-live verification;
- listing-live QA;
- launch confirmation/status.

### Standard listing marketing

The standard listing product should include a **defined base listing-marketing execution layer** rather than charging separate Koinonia fees for every asset.

Recommended baseline to price/model:

- property messaging/copy from approved facts;
- one primary Just Listed social asset/post package;
- one email/e-flyer style listing announcement asset;
- one printable/open-house flyer template when applicable;
- QR/tracked property link when useful;
- open-house promotional asset when an open house is ordered;
- lifecycle status assets for under-contract/sold where included by final product rule.

This baseline should be finalized with Marketing Management so clients are not double-charged for overlapping production.

### Active listing administration

- showing-feedback collection/organization;
- feedback summary support;
- routine seller-update preparation from available approved information;
- price/status change administration from Realtor instruction;
- photo/document/showing-instruction changes;
- listing refresh administrative checklist;
- back-on-market administrative reset;
- approved withdrawal/expiration closeout;
- sign/lockbox removal coordination;
- accepted-offer handoff.

### Open-house coordination

If Realtor requests an open house while Hand Us the Listing is active:

Included Koinonia listing-management work should cover:

- scheduling/coordination;
- basic marketing connection;
- property/event information organization;
- coordination with the assigned licensed host.

The **licensed host's field assignment** may remain separately priced unless the eventual Hand Us the Listing price explicitly includes an open-house allowance.

This prevents double-charging for coordination while still recognizing real licensed field labor.

## Outside expenses

Additional when required and approved:

- photography;
- drone/video;
- 3D/floor plans;
- staging;
- cleaning/repairs/landscaping;
- external sign/lockbox/vendor charges;
- printing/postage;
- paid advertising;
- specialty property websites/production if beyond standard included scope;
- other property-specific third-party services.

## Included-volume principle

The Realtor is buying management of **one standard listing engagement**, not a fixed number of emails/vendor calls/status changes.

Routine coordination needed to launch and administer the listing is included.

## Not included / separate scope

- seller/Realtor pricing strategy;
- CMA conclusions or pricing decisions;
- negotiation;
- legal advice;
- material property representations not approved by Realtor;
- inspection/property-condition opinions;
- repairs/vendor invoices themselves;
- licensed field labor unless specifically included;
- unlimited custom marketing campaigns;
- unlimited print/ad spend;
- unusually extensive seller concierge/remodel/project-management work beyond normal listing preparation;
- Transaction Management after accepted offer, unless a future bundled listing-to-close product explicitly includes it.

## Preferred contract-stage rule

At accepted offer:

- Koinonia completes the operational handoff automatically;
- seller/property information carries forward;
- Transaction Management begins at the client's applicable transaction fee/rule.

Do not charge a new intake/setup fee merely because the record moves from listing to transaction.

## Scope-change examples

Confirm expanded scope before proceeding when:

- the seller requests substantial renovation/project management;
- numerous/custom marketing deliverables exceed standard listing marketing;
- multiple properties are treated as one listing engagement;
- repeated relaunches/major direction changes materially recreate the launch;
- specialty luxury/commercial/new-construction campaign requires substantially different production.

# 5. Product 3 — Licensed Field Coverage

## Product outcome

A trusted, appropriately licensed/authorized person covers an approved property/client assignment when the Realtor cannot be physically present.

## Standard unit

One confirmed field assignment/event with a defined location, time/window, expected duration, and task.

## Standard assignment types

### Buyer showing coverage

- single-property showing;
- approved multi-property tour;
- overlapping-client coverage;
- distance-conflict coverage;
- evening/weekend coverage subject to availability;
- vacation/overflow showing coverage.

### Open-house coverage

- professional licensed hosting;
- basic setup/teardown;
- use of approved property information/materials;
- visitor registration/lead capture process;
- completion summary and lead handoff;
- property securement at end of assignment.

### Property/access coverage

- inspection access;
- appraisal access;
- photographer/media access;
- stager/vendor/contractor access;
- repair-completion access;
- measurement/floor-plan access.

### Approved property tasks

- property documentation visit;
- vacancy/property check;
- lockbox/key pickup/drop-off;
- lockbox install/removal where permitted;
- sign/property task where permitted;
- visual securement/status check under written direction.

## Included by default in one standard assignment

- request intake/review;
- appointment/access confirmation;
- assignment coordination;
- review of Realtor instructions;
- standard travel within the approved base service area/radius defined by pricing policy;
- performance of the defined field task;
- standard completion documentation/feedback;
- issue escalation if needed.

## Pricing principle

Unlike transaction/listing management, field labor is directly affected by duration, distance, timing, number of properties, and availability.

Therefore the fee should be based on **assignment scope**, not one universal unlimited price.

The website should still remain simple:

- publish a straightforward starting/base rate where helpful;
- confirm final price before assignment is booked;
- avoid a giant public mileage/time matrix.

## Normal price variables

- travel/distance beyond standard base area;
- short notice/rush;
- evening/weekend/holiday if economics require;
- multiple properties;
- extended attendance;
- unusual access/logistics;
- assignment complexity.

These are not “surprise add-ons”; they are part of determining the assignment price before confirmation.

## Not included / gated

- negotiation;
- legal advice;
- unapproved client representation decisions;
- property-condition warranties/technical opinions;
- repair/contractor work;
- unsafe/unauthorized access;
- final-walkthrough representation unless specifically approved under brokerage model;
- closing attendance/document-review responsibility unless specifically approved;
- possession/condition dispute handling;
- activities outside licensing/brokerage authority.

## Outside expenses

Usually limited, but may include:

- parking/tolls/special access costs;
- externally purchased signs/materials;
- other client-approved direct expense specific to the assignment.

Final pricing policy should decide whether routine local travel is simply built into the base rate rather than itemized.

# 6. Product 4 — Marketing Management

## Product outcome

Koinonia owns an agreed real-estate marketing workload and keeps it moving consistently, without requiring the Realtor to personally plan, produce, coordinate, publish, and chase every marketing item.

## Commercial modes

### A. Marketing Project / Campaign

A defined one-time outcome with one quoted Koinonia fee.

Examples:

- farm campaign;
- database reactivation;
- brand setup;
- listing campaign outside Hand Us the Listing;
- website/landing page project;
- buyer/seller guide;
- event campaign;
- brokerage/referral campaign.

### B. Recurring Marketing Management

One monthly managed-marketing relationship with defined cadence, channels, production capacity, approvals, and reporting.

Do not create multiple public tiers until actual unit economics/customer demand justify them.

## Recommended standard recurring scope to model

The first standard recurring Marketing Management product should be broad enough to feel managed but constrained enough to operate profitably.

Working scope for pricing analysis:

### Strategy / planning

- onboarding/brand profile;
- monthly marketing priorities;
- content calendar;
- campaign recommendations based on Realtor activity/goals.

### Organic content

- agreed recurring social content cadence;
- captions/copy;
- branded static/carousel assets;
- faceless/templated short-form content where appropriate;
- listing/open-house/status posts using available Koinonia information;
- scheduling/publishing to agreed channels.

### Database/email

- one recurring email/newsletter rhythm;
- approved database segmentation/campaign support;
- review/referral touchpoints when included in plan.

### Brand / presence

- routine profile/content updates;
- Google Business Profile content/support where eligible and authorized;
- basic review-generation workflow support.

### Reporting

- concise monthly performance summary;
- next-action recommendations;
- campaign/source tracking where available.

## Capacity principle

Do not publicly sell “unlimited marketing.”

Define an internal/contractual monthly production capacity based on:

- number of channels;
- core content pieces;
- video complexity;
- email/campaign volume;
- revision expectations;
- active listings/events;
- custom project work.

The website can sell the managed outcome without leading with hours.

## Standard listing-marketing overlap

Hand Us the Listing should include a defined base listing-marketing package.

If a Realtor also has recurring Marketing Management:

- do not charge twice for the same standard listing assets;
- the recurring relationship should either absorb/integrate those assets or grant a clearly defined credit/allowance;
- specialty campaign work beyond standard listing marketing can be separately scoped.

## Included revisions

Routine corrections and reasonable revisions necessary to meet the agreed brief should be included.

Repeated strategic redesign after prior approval or substantial change of direction can trigger a scope review.

Do not bill tiny copy edits individually.

## Outside expenses

Additional when used and approved:

- ad spend;
- printing/postage;
- professional photography/video;
- drone/3D/floor plans;
- specialty web/software;
- premium animation/design/production specialists;
- on-location production;
- other third-party marketing execution.

## Not included / separate scope

- guaranteed leads/appointments/closings;
- unlimited custom creative;
- unlimited on-location filming;
- unlimited paid-ad management across arbitrary budgets/platforms;
- material property/market claims not approved by Realtor/brokerage;
- legal/compliance advice;
- large custom website/software builds unless included in project scope;
- client ad/media spend.

# 7. Product 5 — Koinonia Partnership

## Product outcome

A recurring white-glove support relationship in which Koinonia owns an agreed portion of the Realtor's ongoing operations and keeps the recurring work moving without the Realtor having to request every small task individually.

## Positioning

This is not “buy assistant hours.”

It is:

> **A recurring support relationship built around the parts of your business you want off your plate.**

## Standard partnership foundation

Every Koinonia Partnership should include:

### Relationship setup

- business/brand/CRM/workflow intake;
- brokerage/approval requirements;
- communication preferences;
- recurring-priority definition;
- recurring calendar/cadence;
- agreed systems/access;
- service boundaries;
- escalation rules;
- approved outside-spend/authorization rules.

### Recurring operations management

Within agreed scope, examples can include:

- CRM/pipeline maintenance;
- lead/client next-action tracking;
- past-client/review/referral workflows;
- calendar/task organization;
- recurring communication drafting;
- database segmentation;
- workflow/checklist maintenance;
- vendor coordination;
- recurring reports/briefs;
- process/SOP upkeep;
- defined recurring marketing;
- vacation/overflow preparation;
- recurring business administration.

### Regular review

- recurring priorities/status review;
- exceptions requiring Realtor decision;
- periodic recommendation of process improvements;
- capacity/scope review.

## What the base partnership should NOT automatically include

Do not assume the monthly fee includes unlimited usage of expensive/high-variable products.

By default, unless the final plan explicitly includes allowances:

- full Transaction Management files remain separately priced;
- Hand Us the Listing engagements remain separately priced;
- Licensed Field Coverage labor remains separately priced;
- large marketing projects remain separately scoped;
- outside expenses remain additional.

The partnership can provide:

- preferred pricing;
- included allowances;
- priority scheduling;
- bundled usage;

but these should be deliberate economic decisions made when pricing the plan.

## Why this structure matters

If one monthly price automatically includes unlimited transactions, listings, field assignments, marketing, CRM, and admin, one unusually active client can destroy unit economics.

The partnership should provide **continuity and managed capacity**, not uncapped consumption of every Koinonia product.

## Capacity boundary

The contract should define a capacity/service level even if the website does not lead with hours.

Possible internal models to evaluate during pricing:

### Model 1 — Managed-capacity band

Client purchases a defined support level with expected monthly workload range.

### Model 2 — Core recurring scope + product allowances

Example concept:

- ongoing CRM/business operations included;
- recurring marketing included to defined cadence;
- X listing/transaction/field credits or preferred rates.

### Model 3 — Base relationship + usage pricing

Lower monthly relationship fee covers recurring operations/priority/continuity, with per-file/per-assignment usage discounted.

Do not select the final model until unit economics are modeled.

## Scope-change rule

If recurring usage consistently exceeds the agreed service level, do not issue random micro-invoices.

Review the plan and move the client to the appropriate capacity/scope.

## Outside expenses

Same universal rule applies:

- actual approved media/vendor/ad/print/specialist expenses remain outside the Koinonia fee unless explicitly included.

## Not included

- personal errands unrelated to Realtor business;
- legal/tax/financial advice;
- brokerage supervision;
- unapproved professional judgment;
- unlimited emergency/on-call access;
- unlimited custom projects;
- uncapped usage of every other Koinonia product unless a future economically validated package expressly provides it.

# 8. One-Off Projects / “Ask Koinonia” Scope

“Ask Koinonia” is an intake route, not a product.

When a legitimate need does not fit the five named products, Koinonia can quote a defined project/request.

Examples:

- CRM cleanup/migration;
- database reactivation;
- SOP/workflow build;
- listing presentation;
- website/landing page project;
- brokerage/referral campaign;
- custom marketing collateral;
- team onboarding/setup;
- unusual administrative project.

## Project pricing rule

One defined outcome + one quoted Koinonia fee.

Do not expose an internal task-by-task price sheet unless there is a strong commercial reason.

Outside expenses remain separate where needed.

# 9. Cross-Product Double-Charge Rules

The one-stop model will fail if clients feel they are paying Koinonia twice for the same work.

Adopt these principles before pricing:

## Listing + Marketing

Standard Hand Us the Listing fee includes the defined base listing-marketing package.

Marketing Management should not separately charge the client for those same baseline assets.

## Listing + Field Coverage

Listing fee includes **coordination** of approved open house/property field needs.

Separate Licensed Field Coverage fee pays for the actual licensed field labor unless a package/allowance explicitly includes it.

## Listing + Transaction Management

Hand Us the Listing includes seamless accepted-offer handoff.

Transaction Management fee pays for the post-contract contract-to-close lifecycle.

Do not charge a second setup/intake fee for duplicative data already captured.

## Partnership + Other Products

Partnership pricing must explicitly state whether other products are:

- included;
- allowance-based;
- discounted;
- normally priced.

Never leave this ambiguous.

## Marketing + Outside Media

Marketing Management/listing-management fee covers coordination.

Photography/video/print/ad spend is an outside expense, not another Koinonia coordination fee.

# 10. Client-Facing Scope Language

Avoid legalistic walls of text on the sales page.

Recommended general explanation:

> **Your Koinonia fee covers the normal coordination and execution included in your service. If your request requires a materially different scope or an outside expense such as professional media, advertising, printing, or another specialty service, we confirm it with you before committing the cost.**

Professional-boundary language:

> **You remain the Realtor. Koinonia handles approved operational execution and coordination while professional advice, negotiation, legal/compliance decisions, and required brokerage approvals remain with you and your brokerage.**

# 11. Scope Design Decisions Now Ready for Pricing

The following should be treated as the working standard before dollar modeling:

## Transaction Management

- one managed transaction lifecycle;
- routine coordination volume included;
- no micro-fees;
- current $389 / $599 structure remains the pricing baseline to validate.

## Hand Us the Listing

- one managed listing-side lifecycle through accepted offer;
- normal listing/vendor coordination included;
- base listing-marketing execution included;
- outside property/media costs additional;
- licensed field labor separate unless specifically allowed;
- Transaction Management begins at accepted offer under its applicable pricing rule.

## Licensed Field Coverage

- assignment/event based;
- standard coordination + actual field task included in confirmed assignment fee;
- price varies only where assignment economics materially vary.

## Marketing Management

- one-time project/campaign or recurring management;
- first launch should favor one core recurring plan before inventing tiers;
- agreed cadence/capacity, not unlimited production;
- outside media/ad/print costs additional.

## Koinonia Partnership

- recurring managed-capacity relationship;
- ongoing CRM/business operations foundation;
- may include recurring marketing depending final scope;
- high-variable per-file/per-field products not automatically unlimited;
- final economics should determine allowances/discounts/inclusions.

# 12. Immediate Next Step

With scope defined, the next work should be **unit economics and pricing design**.

For each product calculate/model:

1. expected Koinonia labor/coordination time;
2. direct labor/provider cost;
3. software/payment/overhead assumptions;
4. expected variability/risk;
5. gross-margin target;
6. competitive/customer willingness-to-pay range;
7. simple final client-facing price;
8. interaction with Koinonia Partnership discounts/allowances;
9. cancellation/failure/rush rules only where economically necessary.

Do not rewrite the public website until the price/scope pair for each core product is coherent.
