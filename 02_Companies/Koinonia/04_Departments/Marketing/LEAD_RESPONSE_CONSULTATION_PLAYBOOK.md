# Koinonia Transactions — Lead Response & Consultation Playbook

Status: Build-Mode Operating Playbook  
Business: Koinonia Transactions  
Owner: Marketing + Sales  
Effective Date: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

Define exactly what happens after a Realtor, brokerage leader, referral source, or other qualified prospect raises their hand.

This playbook governs:

**Inbound signal -> Relationship record -> Response -> Diagnosis -> Consultation -> Smallest sufficient engagement -> Quote / service handoff -> Next action -> Follow-up / close**

This document is a build-stage system definition. It does not authorize contacting any new lead during build mode.

Until activation is explicitly approved:

- do not send new lead-response messages
- do not call new prospects
- do not schedule consultations with newly sourced prospects
- do not send quotes/proposals to newly sourced prospects
- do not automate sales follow-up

---

# Core Sales Principle

Koinonia should not begin with:

**Which service can we sell this person?**

Koinonia should begin with:

**What pressure are they actually experiencing, what outcome do they want, and what is the smallest useful engagement that solves the immediate problem?**

Use:

**Diagnose first. Prescribe second. Present third.**

Recommendation formula:

**Because you told me [problem] is causing [consequence], I would start with [service] so we can [specific desired outcome].**

The first engagement should earn the second engagement.

---

# Response Priority

Every meaningful inquiry should become or update one Koinonia Relationship record.

Priority order:

1. current active service need
2. direct consultation request
3. qualified buying-intent DM/comment
4. warm introduction / referral
5. brokerage leader conversation
6. general service question
7. nurture / long-term interest

Do not let cold prospecting activity outrank a real inbound lead.

---

# Response-Time Standards — Future Implementation

## Active / current need

Examples:

- showing needed today/tomorrow
- open house needed this weekend
- active transaction/file problem
- direct request to hire Koinonia

Target:

**Respond as soon as practical during normal operating hours.**

Operational working standard:

- acknowledge within 1 business hour when feasible
- if a full answer is not immediately possible, send a short acknowledgment and set a specific follow-up time

## Consultation request / Meta lead / website form

Target:

**Same business day.**

Faster response is preferred during normal business hours.

## Social DM / comment with buying intent

Target:

**Same business day.**

Respond on-platform first unless another channel is clearly better and appropriate.

## General question / non-urgent inquiry

Target:

**Within 1 business day.**

## Brokerage / professional partner inquiry

Target:

**Within 1 business day.**

Do not create a promise of 24/7 response unless Koinonia actually operates that way.

# Intake by Channel

## Website Consultation

Capture:

- contact information
- source / UTM
- requested consultation type
- notes/problem language
- campaign/source detail
- next action

The current consultation API already creates or updates a Relationship, timeline event, and follow-up task on the integration branch.

Do not create a duplicate relationship manually if the website intake has already done so.

## Meta Instant Form

Capture:

- role
- pressure selected
- timing
- contact information
- campaign/creative
- platform/source

First response:

**You selected [pressure]. Before I recommend anything, I'd like to understand the actual situation. What's happening in the business that made that one stand out?**

## TikTok / Instagram / Facebook DM

Capture only when there is meaningful business context.

Use:

- platform
- content/video ID if known
- exact prospect language
- pressure
- next action

Respond in the active channel first.

Move to email/phone only when useful and appropriate.

## Comment

Answer public factual questions publicly when appropriate.

Move to DM/private channel only when the discussion requires personal scheduling, pricing detail, client context, or other non-public information.

## Email

Reply to the actual email before trying to move the person into a scripted sequence.

A reply stops generic follow-up automation.

## Brokerage / Professional Introduction

Capture:

- introducer/referrer
- organization
- relationship context
- reason for introduction
- exact issue if provided

A real introduction should never be treated like a cold lead.

# Pressure Categories

Use these categories for structured reporting while preserving exact prospect language.

1. Transaction / file capacity
2. Contract / document workload
3. Showing / schedule conflict
4. Open house / listing capacity
5. CRM / follow-up / business organization
6. Referral / no-capacity client opportunity
7. Brokerage / team operations
8. Unclear / other

Do not force a person into a category before enough information exists.

Use **Unclear / Other** temporarily when necessary.

# Diagnosis Framework

Every diagnosis should answer five things.

## 1. What is happening?

What is the operational situation?

## 2. What is the consequence?

What happens if nothing changes?

Examples:

- client service suffers
- agent works evenings/weekends
- file details fall back on agent
- opportunity gets turned away
- follow-up becomes inconsistent

## 3. How urgent is it?

- now
- this week
- within 30 days
- later / exploratory

## 4. What support already exists?

Examples:

- TC
- assistant
- team ops
- brokerage tools
- nothing formal

Never assume existing support is inadequate.

## 5. What outcome does the Realtor want?

Examples:

- protect client time
- stop missing follow-up
- cover one appointment
- host one open house
- get one file organized
- reduce recurring operational workload

---

# Diagnostic Questions by Pressure

## Transaction / File Capacity

Primary questions:

- **What part of the file keeps falling back on you?**
- Where in the contract-to-close process do you feel the most pressure?
- Do you already have a TC or file system in place?
- Is this one transaction or recurring capacity?

Do not assume a full transaction-management engagement if one narrow issue is the actual problem.

## Contract / Document Workload

Primary questions:

- **Is the decision already known, or are you still working through what should be in the agreement?**
- What document/workflow is creating the pressure?
- Is this a one-time assignment or recurring workload?

Boundary:

Koinonia supports document preparation/workflow within licensed and approved scope; Realtor judgment and client decisions remain with the Realtor/client as required.

## Showing / Schedule Conflict

Primary questions:

- **What date/time and location are you trying to cover?**
- Buyer or listing-side need?
- What access instructions/process applies?
- Is this one showing block or recurring coverage need?

Do not collect sensitive access information in public comments/social threads.

## Open House / Listing Capacity

Primary questions:

- **What property/date are you trying to cover, and what is already planned?**
- Is the need hosting only or preparation/promotion/hosting?
- What does the listing agent want handled before/after?

## CRM / Follow-Up / Business Organization

Primary questions:

- **Which part feels least organized right now: pipeline, follow-up, tasks, CRM, calendar, or workflow?**
- What is currently falling through?
- What system/tools already exist?
- Is the goal cleanup, recurring support, or both?

Do not recommend a full monthly partnership before understanding the actual recurring workload.

## Referral / No-Capacity Client Opportunity

Primary question:

- **Do you want to keep the client and need capacity help, or have you decided you do not want/cannot take the client?**

This question protects the support/referral separation.

If keep client:

Diagnose support need.

If refer client:

Move to separate referral workflow.

Never convert a support relationship into a referral without explicit Realtor choice.

## Brokerage / Team Operations

Primary questions:

- What pressure is showing up repeatedly across agents/team members?
- What support already exists internally?
- Is the need education, overflow capacity, or an operational resource?
- Who should be involved in deciding whether Koinonia fits?

# Consultation Preparation Checklist

Before a scheduled consultation:

- review Relationship record
- review exact prospect language
- verify source/campaign
- identify likely pressure category
- note current support/workaround
- identify open questions
- review applicable service/pricing truth
- confirm geographic/licensing fit
- prepare one or two likely smallest-entry options
- do not pre-decide the sale before the conversation

If the consultation came from a specific social post/ad, review that content so the conversation remains context-aware.

---

# Consultation Structure

Recommended target length:

**15–30 minutes** for a normal initial consultation.

Longer only when the complexity genuinely requires it.

## Part 1 — Context

Ask what prompted them to reach out now.

## Part 2 — Pressure

Understand the operational problem and consequence.

## Part 3 — Existing Support

Understand what they already use.

## Part 4 — Desired Outcome

Clarify what "better" looks like.

## Part 5 — Recommendation

Recommend the smallest sufficient engagement.

## Part 6 — Next Step

End with one clear action and date.

Do not finish with:

"Let me know if you ever need anything."

Finish with a concrete next step or explicitly close/hold the relationship.

## Transaction pressure

Potential first step:

- one transaction
- prepaid $389 or pay-at-successful-close $599 option as appropriate under current pricing rules

Do not default to a monthly relationship if one file proves the relationship better.

## Contract / Document pressure

Potential first step:

- one defined document assignment
- starting at $75 under current public pricing

## Showing pressure

Potential first step:

- one defined licensed showing assignment
- starting at $50 under current public pricing

## Open House pressure

Potential first step:

- one standalone open house at current public pricing ($200)
- or applicable listing-engagement open-house structure under current rules

## Recurring operations pressure

Potential first step:

- defined operations cleanup or appropriate monthly tier

Current public monthly tiers:

- Starter $299 / up to 3 hours
- Growth $599 / up to 7 hours
- Partner $999 / up to 12 hours

Do not promise an automatic monthly-tier service discount unless canonical pricing changes.

## Referral pressure

Separate path:

- 40% referral fee on qualifying successfully closed referred business

Not a sixth Koinonia Transactions service.

# Recommendation Standard

Use:

**Because you told me [problem] is causing [consequence], I would start with [service] so we can [specific desired outcome].**

Example:

**Because you told me overlapping buyer schedules are forcing you to choose which client gets your time, I would start with one licensed showing block so you can protect both client relationships without changing the rest of your business.**

This is better than:

"You should sign up for our full service package."

---

# Quote / Service Handoff

A quote/proposal should only include what is relevant to the diagnosed need.

Before sending:

- service/scope confirmed
- price confirmed against canonical pricing
- timing confirmed
- boundaries clear
- payment structure clear
- responsible party clear
- next action/date clear

Do not send outdated pricing from website copy if it conflicts with canonical pricing.

Do not include unrelated services just to make the proposal look larger.

---

# Conversion Definition

Do not define conversion only as "consultation booked."

Track stages such as:

- meaningful inquiry
- qualified conversation
- consultation
- recommended engagement
- quote/proposal sent
- first paid engagement
- repeat engagement
- cross-service engagement
- advocate/referral source

This supports the full acquisition engine rather than only top-of-funnel metrics.

# Objection Handling Principles

## "I already have a TC."

Response:

Respect it.

Explore only if the person identifies a gap such as:

- showing coverage
- open houses
- document overflow
- occasional transaction overflow

If no gap exists, exit professionally.

## "I don't need help right now."

Clarify whether timing is simply later.

Do not force urgency.

## "It's too expensive."

Return to the smallest sufficient engagement and value/consequence.

Do not immediately discount.

## "I can do it myself."

Agree that they may be able to.

The relevant question is whether doing it themselves is the best use of time under current pressure.

## "My brokerage already provides support."

Ask whether that support fully covers the pressure they described.

Do not undermine brokerage resources.

## "I don't understand the difference between support and referral."

Clarify:

- keep client + need operational capacity = support
- intentionally do not/cannot take client = referral

# Follow-Up After Consultation

Same day or within 1 business day when practical:

- recap the diagnosed pressure
- state the recommended smallest engagement
- provide the agreed next action
- include only relevant collateral/links

Do not send a generic full-service brochure if a specific next step was agreed.

---

# No-Response Handling

The goal is not endless chasing.

## After inbound inquiry with no response to Koinonia

Working pattern:

- initial response
- one useful follow-up in approximately 2–3 business days
- one close-the-loop follow-up around 7–10 days if appropriate
- then move to Hold / No Response

Urgent/time-sensitive service inquiries may require a shorter timeline because the opportunity expires.

Do not continue sending repeated messages after the need is clearly stale.

## After consultation / proposal with no response

Working pattern:

- confirm they received the recommendation
- follow up once around the decision timing discussed
- close the loop if no response

If they said "circle back in October," schedule October rather than sending generic weekly follow-ups.

---

# Lost / Hold Reasons

Use structured reasons where possible:

- not now / timing
- no current need
- existing support sufficient
- price/value mismatch
- service/scope mismatch
- geographic/licensing fit
- chose another provider
- no response
- referral path not desired
- do not contact
- other

Preserve exact explanation when useful.

Lost is learning data, not merely failure.

---

# Nurture Handoff

A person can move to nurture only when the Email Marketing Operations Playbook supports that status.

Do not use nurture as a dumping ground for every unresponsive lead.

Appropriate examples:

- explicit interest but timing later
- permission-based subscriber
- past client/prospect with valid ongoing relationship basis

Inappropriate:

- person asked to stop
- cold prospect never replied and has no nurture basis
- invalid/bounced contact

---

# Next-Action Rule

Every active relationship must have exactly one current next action.

Examples:

- call Tuesday
- send showing quote today
- confirm open-house details Friday
- follow up October 1
- wait for signed agreement
- send referral instructions

And one next-action date.

Avoid multiple competing tasks that make ownership unclear.

---

# Ownership Rule

One person should own the relationship at a time.

If marketing generates the lead but sales/service takes over, record the handoff.

Avoid situations where:

- two Koinonia people contact the same Realtor independently
- automated email continues after personal conversation
- brokerage and agent outreach collide without awareness

---

# Lead Quality Definition

A qualified lead is not simply someone who submitted a form.

Working definition:

A qualified lead is a Colorado real estate professional or relevant brokerage/professional partner with a plausible Koinonia fit and a real operational pressure, timing, or relationship opportunity worth pursuing.

A meaningful conversation is stronger:

The person has actually described context/pressure or engaged in diagnosis.

Paid-media reporting should distinguish raw leads from qualified leads and meaningful conversations.

---

# Consultation Quality Review

After each consultation, capture:

- pressure category
- exact problem language
- consequence
- urgency
- existing support
- desired outcome
- recommended service
- recommendation accepted / declined / pending
- objection
- next action
- next-action date

For the first 50 meaningful relationships, this is core marketing learning data.

---

# Service-Boundary Guardrails

Do not:

- give legal advice beyond appropriate licensed scope
- imply Koinonia replaces broker supervision
- imply Koinonia makes Realtor/client decisions
- expose client confidential information in marketing systems
- convert support to referral without explicit Realtor choice
- promise service availability before confirming capacity

When uncertain, clarify scope before accepting the engagement.

Until then, this playbook remains a readiness standard only.
