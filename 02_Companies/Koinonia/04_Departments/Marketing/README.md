# Marketing & Growth Engine

## Canonical Role

This file is the canonical operating plan for Koinonia marketing and growth.

It expands the existing Marketing department rather than creating a parallel marketing plan.

Use this document to coordinate channels, campaigns, content, measurement, and launch sequencing. Specialized execution documents may exist for individual channels, but they must support this plan rather than compete with it.

Core repository rule: recover before reinventing; reuse before replacing; extend before creating.

## Mission

Attract the right real estate professionals, build trust before the first conversation, convert that trust into durable client relationships, and measure marketing by long-term relationship value rather than vanity metrics.

## Primary Audience

Primary audience:

- Colorado real estate professionals who need reliable operational support
- Solo agents and growing agents who are becoming overloaded by transaction and administrative work
- Teams and brokerages that need scalable operational capacity
- Agents who value responsiveness, professionalism, process control, and client experience

Secondary audiences may be developed as the business grows, but messaging should not become so broad that Koinonia loses its real-estate-operations positioning.

# 3. Social Media

## Objective

Build recognizable trust with real estate professionals before they need Koinonia, demonstrate operational expertise, create repeated brand exposure, and move qualified prospects toward the website or a conversation.

Social media is not a separate brand. It is a distribution layer for the same Koinonia operating story.

Before creating any new account, manually verify whether an account already exists and whether Koinonia controls it.

### No-Duplicate Rule

Do not create a second account merely because login credentials are temporarily unknown.

Sequence:

1. Search each platform for the brand name and likely handles.
2. Determine whether an existing profile is legitimate and controlled by Koinonia.
3. Recover credentials/admin access when possible.
4. Record the exact public URL and handle in this document.
5. Only create a new profile when the search confirms that no appropriate existing account exists.

## Platform Priority

Initial priority:

1. Facebook + Instagram as a connected Meta foundation
2. LinkedIn for professional credibility and Realtor/brokerage relationship building
3. TikTok for short-form reach and educational visibility
4. YouTube when enough video content exists to justify a maintained channel

This priority may change based on measured lead quality, but new platforms should not be added merely to increase channel count.

Every public profile should use, where platform limitations allow:

## Content Pillars

Organic social content should be generated from a small repeatable set of pillars rather than random posting.

### Pillar A — Realtor Education

Help agents understand operational problems before selling Koinonia as the solution.

Examples:

- Common transaction-management mistakes
- What agents should prepare before going under contract
- How strong transaction systems protect client experience
- Showing-coverage planning
- Contract/document workflow tips
- Process and deadline organization

### Pillar B — Operational Relief

Show what becomes possible when an agent stops carrying every operational task personally.

Examples:

- Signs an agent has outgrown doing everything alone
- What should stay on the agent's plate versus an operations partner's plate
- How delegation creates more client-facing time
- Why operational consistency matters during growth

### Pillar C — Process & Expertise

Demonstrate that Koinonia is a real operating business with repeatable systems.

Examples:

- Behind-the-scenes workflow explanations
- Checklists and process walkthroughs
- How Koinonia prepares for a transaction handoff
- How licensed showing coverage fits into an agent's business
- How communication is organized

Never expose client-confidential information in behind-the-scenes content.

### Pillar D — Service Clarity

Explain each approved service clearly and concretely.

Use the canonical service objects as the source of truth.

### Pillar E — Trust & Human Connection

Allow prospects to understand who they are working with and how Koinonia approaches service.

Examples:

- Founder/leadership perspective
- Values
- Why Koinonia exists
- Professional standards
- Lessons from real estate operations

### Pillar F — Proof

As proof becomes available, share truthful evidence of outcomes.

Examples:

- Testimonials with permission
- Process improvements
- Service milestones
- Case studies without exposing confidential information
- Brokerage or agent relationships when permission exists

Do not fabricate testimonials, reviews, performance claims, client counts, or results.

## Content Repurposing Engine

Create once; adapt intentionally.

A strong source idea should be converted into multiple channel-native assets where useful:

1. One core idea or educational topic
2. Short-form video
3. Facebook/Instagram post or carousel
4. LinkedIn post
5. FAQ or website expansion when the topic has search value
6. Email tip or nurture content

Do not publish identical copy everywhere without adapting it to the platform and audience behavior.

## Initial Organic Cadence

Start with a sustainable baseline rather than an aggressive schedule that cannot be maintained.

Recommended launch baseline:

- 3 primary social posts per week
- 2 short-form videos per week within that content mix
- Regular Stories/temporary updates when there is genuine activity worth showing
- 1 professional LinkedIn post per week minimum

Cadence should be adjusted after 30-60 days based on production capacity, lead quality, and engagement patterns.

Consistency is more important than posting volume.

## Social Calls to Action

Rotate calls to action according to content intent.

Examples:

- Learn more about the service
- Visit the relevant service page
- Schedule a consultation
- Send a direct message with a specific question
- Save/share an educational post
- Follow for practical real estate operations guidance

Not every post needs a sales CTA.

# 4. Educational Content

Educational content is the bridge between expertise and trust.

The content engine should prioritize real questions agents ask, recurring operational pain points, and misconceptions that Koinonia can clarify.

Useful formats:

- Short-form video
- FAQ answers
- Checklists
- Short articles
- Carousels
- Email education
- Downloadable resources when a genuine lead-generation use case exists

Avoid creating large amounts of generic content merely for volume.

Each substantial topic should be evaluated for reuse across social, website, email, and brokerage outreach.

Objectives:

# 7. Email Marketing

Email supports trust and follow-up after a prospect has a legitimate relationship or permission basis for contact.

Primary uses:

- Consultation follow-up
- Lead nurture
- Educational updates
- Service announcements
- Relationship maintenance
- Re-engagement of legitimate prior contacts

Email should not become an indiscriminate purchased-list or mass-spam channel.

Core future sequences:

1. New inquiry acknowledgment/follow-up
2. Consultation follow-up
3. Educational nurture
4. Existing-client relationship communication
5. Re-engagement where appropriate

Email copy should point prospects toward the next logical action rather than repeatedly pushing a generic sales message.

---

# 8. Paid Acquisition

Paid marketing is an amplification layer, not the first foundation.

Do not scale paid traffic until:

- Website conversion paths are working
- Core tracking is reliable
- Organic messaging has produced useful learning
- Social profiles and brand identity are credible
- Follow-up processes are ready

Potential paid sequence:

1. Meta retargeting and carefully controlled audience tests
2. Meta lead/conversion campaigns when creative and follow-up are ready
3. Google Search campaigns for high-intent service demand once conversion measurement is reliable
4. Additional paid channels only when evidence supports them

Every paid campaign needs:

- Defined audience
- Defined offer/message
- Defined landing destination
- Defined conversion event
- Defined budget ceiling
- Defined test period
- Defined success/failure criteria

Do not optimize to cheap clicks if those clicks do not become qualified conversations or clients.

---

# 9. Conversion & Follow-up

Marketing is incomplete if leads are generated but not handled consistently.

Primary conversion path:

Discovery -> Trust -> Website/Profile Review -> Inquiry or Consultation -> Follow-up -> Qualified Relationship -> Client

All channels should make the next step clear.

Minimum conversion signals to preserve:

- Contact form submissions
- Consultation bookings
- Calls
- Texts where supported
- Direct-message inquiries
- Referral source
- Lead source/channel

As Reynalds OS marketing/CRM capabilities mature, these should become shared objects/events rather than disconnected channel records.

---

# 10. Measurement

Marketing should be judged by business outcomes.

## Primary Metrics

Track:

- Qualified leads by source
- Consultation requests by source
- Consultation-to-client conversion
- Client acquisition by source
- Referral source
- Cost per qualified lead for paid channels
- Cost per acquired client for paid channels
- Website conversion rate
- Google Business Profile calls/clicks/actions
- Search Console queries and impressions
- Email response/conversion behavior
- Social content that contributes to inquiries or qualified conversations
- Long-term relationship value

## Secondary Metrics

Useful but not decisive on their own:

- Reach
- Impressions
- Followers
- Likes
- Comments
- Video views
- Email opens

Do not confuse audience activity with business performance.

Actions:

Goal: improve the path from attention to consultation and client.

Actions:

- Review website conversion performance
- Improve calls to action where evidence supports it
- Tighten consultation follow-up
- Identify highest-quality lead sources

## Phase 5 — Paid Acquisition Tests

Goal: amplify messages and audiences already demonstrating organic or relationship-market traction.

Actions:

- Launch small, controlled tests
- Measure qualified leads and clients, not just traffic
- Stop weak campaigns quickly
- Scale only repeatable winners
