# Koinonia Transactions — Marketing Dashboard & Weekly Review Playbook

Status: Build-Mode Operating Playbook  
Business: Koinonia Transactions  
Owner: Marketing + Sales  
Effective Date: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

Define exactly how Koinonia measures marketing performance, reviews what is working, identifies operational breakdowns, and decides what to change next.

This playbook governs:

**Source -> Prospect -> Qualified Lead -> Meaningful Conversation -> Consultation -> First Paid Engagement -> Repeat / Cross-Service -> Advocate / Referral Source**

This document is a build-stage system definition. It does not authorize live campaign changes.

Until activation is explicitly approved:

- do not increase or decrease live ad budgets
- do not pause live campaigns
- do not change live audiences/creative
- do not publish additional content because a dashboard says to
- do not alter outreach cadence

---

# Core Measurement Principle

Koinonia should not optimize for activity.

Koinonia should optimize for:

**Useful professional relationships that become paid engagements and repeat business.**

The reporting hierarchy is:

1. business outcomes
2. relationship quality
3. channel/conversion performance
4. diagnostic platform metrics

Do not reverse that order.

A high-view post with no qualified Realtor response is not automatically better than a low-view post that creates a paid engagement.

---

# Funnel Definitions

Use consistent definitions across every channel.

## Prospect

A researched or known person/account that may fit Koinonia but has not necessarily engaged.

## Lead

A person who has actively entered Koinonia's marketing/sales process through a form, DM, reply, introduction, event interaction, or approved outreach response.

## Qualified Lead

A Colorado real estate professional, brokerage leader, or relevant professional partner with a plausible Koinonia fit and a real pressure, timing, or relationship opportunity worth pursuing.

## Meaningful Conversation

The person has actually described business context, pressure, objection, timing, or desired outcome.

A generic "thanks" response does not automatically count.

## Consultation

A deliberate diagnostic conversation about fit and next steps.

## Recommended Engagement

Koinonia has diagnosed the situation and recommended the smallest sufficient engagement.

## First Paid Engagement

The person purchases/commits to the first paid Koinonia service.

## Repeat Engagement

The same relationship purchases again.

## Cross-Service Engagement

The relationship uses another Koinonia service lane.

## Advocate / Referral Source

The person actively introduces, recommends, or refers other professional relationships to Koinonia.

# Dashboard Views

The reporting system should support six primary views.

## 1. Executive Marketing Scorecard

Weekly summary of the acquisition engine.

Required metrics:

- new leads
- qualified leads
- meaningful conversations
- consultations
- recommended engagements
- first paid engagements
- repeat engagements
- cross-service engagements
- attributed revenue where reliable
- open next actions overdue

The scorecard should also show the prior comparable period when useful.

Do not require perfect attribution before using the scorecard.

---

## 2. Source-to-Outcome View

Group by source/channel.

Examples:

- Facebook organic
- Instagram organic
- TikTok organic
- Meta paid
- email outbound
- email nurture
- website organic/direct
- brokerage relationship
- Realtor event/networking
- direct introduction
- professional partner
- existing client/referral

For each source track:

- leads
- qualified leads
- meaningful conversations
- consultations
- first paid engagements
- first revenue
- repeat/cross-service outcomes later

Core ratios:

### Qualification rate

Qualified leads / leads

### Conversation rate

Meaningful conversations / leads

### Consultation rate

Consultations / qualified leads

### First-paid conversion rate

First paid engagements / qualified leads

### Conversation-to-paid rate

First paid engagements / meaningful conversations

Do not compare tiny sample sizes as if they are statistically stable.

# Paid Media Quality View

For Meta and later TikTok paid, track:

- spend
- leads
- qualified leads
- meaningful conversations
- consultations
- first paid engagements
- attributed revenue where reliable
- cost per lead
- cost per qualified lead
- cost per meaningful conversation
- cost per consultation
- cost per first paid engagement

Platform diagnostic metrics may include:

- impressions
- CPM
- CTR
- frequency
- form completion rate
- placement breakdown

Primary decision rule:

**Qualified-conversation economics outrank cheapest CPL.**

A creative with a higher CPL can still be stronger if lead quality is materially better.

---

# Content Learning View

Track each content ID.

Required fields:

- content ID
- channel
- format
- content job
- pressure category
- service lane
- publish date
- views/reach/impressions
- profile visits where available
- clicks
- saves/shares
- comments/questions
- DMs
- qualified conversations attributed
- consultations attributed
- paid engagements attributed where reasonably known
- exact language/themes learned
- keep / remake / retire decision

Do not judge content solely by views or likes.

## Content Outcome Classes

### Business Winner

Generated meaningful business conversations, consultations, or paid engagements.

### Learning Winner

Generated useful pressure language, objections, or questions even if no immediate sale.

### Reach Winner

Generated strong distribution but limited business signal.

### Weak / Confusing

Low strategic value, poor-fit engagement, or unclear messaging.

A Reach Winner should not automatically become paid creative.

## Personal outbound

- sent
- meaningful replies
- qualified conversations
- consultations
- first paid engagements
- opt-out/stop requests

## Warm follow-up

- follow-ups sent
- next-step completion
- consultations/service starts

## Nurture

- delivered
- bounced

- complaints where exposed
- clicks
- replies
- consultations
- first paid engagements

---

# Brokerage Relationship View

Track at the account level.

Required metrics:

- qualified brokerage accounts
- leader conversations
- presentations requested/accepted
- sessions completed
- estimated attendance
- individual agent relationships created
- consultation requests from brokerage channel
- first paid engagements
- repeat engagements
- introductions to other offices/teams

Also capture:

- common leader questions
- existing support systems
- recurring agent pressure themes
- objections to outside support

Do not measure brokerage success by emails sent to offices.

---

# Lead Response Operations View

Marketing performance is weak if Koinonia creates demand but fails to handle it.

Track:

- new inquiries
- same-business-day response rate
- urgent inquiry response rate
- leads without Relationship record
- active Relationships without next action
- overdue next actions
- consultations missing follow-up
- proposals/recommendations pending beyond planned decision date

These are operating-health metrics, not vanity metrics.

# First-50 Relationship Review

The first 50 meaningful relationships are a structured learning sample.

Do not wait until relationship 50 to review.

Review checkpoints:

- first 10
- first 25
- first 50

## First 10 — Language / Problem Learning

Review:

- pressure categories
- exact problem language
- confusion about services
- first objections
- which question earns the best diagnosis

Primary question:

**Are we describing the problem the way Realtors actually describe it?**

## First 25 — Source / Entry-Service Learning

Review:

- source mix
- meaningful-conversation rate
- common first service
- objections
- existing-support patterns
- warm vs cold differences
- brokerage vs individual differences

Primary question:

**Which relationships and pressure categories most naturally produce a first engagement?**

## First 50 — Economic / Relationship Learning

Review:

- first-paid conversion
- repeat engagements
- cross-service adoption
- source-to-value
- brokerage introductions
- paid channel economics
- service delivery capacity implications

Primary question:

**Where should Koinonia intentionally invest more attention next?**

# Weekly Review Cadence

Recommended review rhythm after activation:

**Once per week, 30–45 minutes.**

Use one consistent day/time when practical.

Do not continuously redesign marketing based on daily noise.

---

# Weekly Review Agenda

## 1. Pipeline Health — 5 minutes

Review:

- new qualified leads
- meaningful conversations
- consultations
- paid engagements
- overdue next actions

Immediate operational failures come first.

Example:

If five leads arrived but three were never contacted, do not spend the meeting debating ad creative.

## 2. Source Quality — 5–10 minutes

Ask:

- which sources created real conversations?
- which sources created low-fit leads?
- did any source produce a first paid engagement?

## 3. Prospect Language / Pressure — 5 minutes

Review:

- exact language
- pressure-category changes
- objections
- new scenarios

This should feed content and sales language.

## 4. Content — 5 minutes

Identify:

- one content winner
- one learning insight
- one weak/confusing asset if present

Avoid overreacting to every post.

## 5. Paid Media — 5 minutes when active

Review:

- spend vs approved plan
- qualified lead quality
- meaningful conversations
- campaign operational issues

Use green/yellow/red logic.

## 6. Brokerage / Relationship Marketing — 5 minutes

Review:

- leader conversations
- sessions
- agent follow-ups
- account next actions

## 7. Decisions — 5 minutes

Finish with no more than three intentional changes for the next cycle unless a major operational failure requires more.

---

# Weekly Decision Categories

Every proposed change should be labeled.

## Keep

Evidence supports continuing.

## Improve

Core strategy remains valid; one part needs adjustment.

## Test

A controlled new variable is worth learning from.

## Stop / Hold

Evidence or operational reality says continuing is not useful now.

Do not label everything "optimize."

---

# Change-Control Rule

Prefer one meaningful variable at a time when practical.

Examples:

Good:

- change the Meta creative hook while keeping form/audience stable
- change outbound opening question while keeping prospect segment stable

Poor:

- change audience, budget, creative, landing page, form, and follow-up on the same day

When multiple changes are necessary for a broken system, document that the comparison is no longer clean.

# Month 2 Change-Control Rule

At the Month 1 retrospective, choose a maximum of **three major Month 2 changes**.

Examples:

- increase emphasis on showing-conflict content
- change Meta lead-form question
- focus brokerage outreach on team leaders rather than large offices

Do not redesign the entire marketing engine after one month.

Keep the stable core and change the areas where evidence is strongest.

# Dashboard Data-Quality Rules

A dashboard is only useful when definitions are trustworthy.

## Source preservation

Preserve original first-touch source when known.

Later interactions can be recorded without overwriting first-touch history.

## Campaign/content IDs

Use stable IDs.

Do not rename past campaigns/content in ways that break historical grouping.

## Manual attribution

When a Realtor says:

"I saw your TikTok about two showings at once"

record that content attribution even if the final website session was direct traffic.

## Unknown attribution

Use Unknown / Direct when genuinely unknown.

Do not fabricate attribution for completeness.

## Duplicates

One person = one Relationship record whenever possible.

Do not inflate lead counts by counting the same person separately across website, email, and social.

---

# Revenue Attribution Standard

Use conservative attribution.

## Direct attributed revenue

Reasonably tied to a known source/campaign/relationship.

## Influenced revenue

Marketing clearly contributed, but multiple relationship touches were involved.

## Unknown / organic relationship value

Do not force credit to a campaign.

Avoid arguing over perfect attribution at low volume.

Early-stage Koinonia benefits more from clean relationship learning than overly complex multi-touch models.

---

# Cost Calculations

## Cost per Lead

Paid spend / leads

## Cost per Qualified Lead

Paid spend / qualified leads

## Cost per Meaningful Conversation

Paid spend / meaningful conversations

## Cost per Consultation

Paid spend / consultations

## Cost per First Paid Engagement

Paid spend / first paid engagements

If denominator = 0, report no conversion / not yet measurable rather than inventing a cost.

---

# Green / Yellow / Red Weekly Health

## Green

- leads handled promptly
- qualified conversations occurring
- source data clean enough
- no major compliance/technical issue
- spend within plan

## Yellow

- lead quality mixed
- response delay
- incomplete attribution
- content confusion
- one channel underperforming but still learning

Action:

Diagnose and make a limited change.

## Red

- leads not being handled
- broken conversion path
- privacy/compliance issue
- uncontrolled spend
- repeated poor-fit demand
- service capacity cannot support generated demand

Action:

Pause the affected implementation area and fix the operating problem.

# Marketing Learning Log

Every weekly review should produce a short learning record.

Required fields:

- week/date range
- what happened
- what we learned
- supporting evidence
- what stays the same
- what changes
- owner
- review date for the change

This prevents Koinonia from repeatedly relearning the same lesson.

# Build-Mode Activation Gate

Reporting/review execution becomes live only when:

1. Koinonia has entered implementation mode.
2. source taxonomy is being captured consistently.
3. Relationship lifecycle definitions are in use.
4. content IDs are in use.
5. paid campaign IDs are in use.
6. response/next-action data is available.
7. someone owns the weekly review.
8. the user explicitly approves implementation.

Until then, the dashboard remains a reporting specification.
