# Koinonia Sellable Service Architecture — 2026-09-02

Status: Working Recommendation / Owner Review Required  
Owner: Koinonia  
Purpose: Define what Koinonia sells to Realtors, how the offering is organized publicly, how clients can buy support, and how the website should present the breadth of service without creating a separate service box for every task.

This document is a product and sales architecture. It is **not** an infrastructure blueprint and does not authorize unreviewed public claims, pricing, licensed activity, vendor representations, or regulated work.

---

# 1. Executive Recommendation

Koinonia should keep **five public capability umbrellas**, with slightly simplified client-facing names:

1. **Transactions & Contracts**
2. **Listing & Seller Support**
3. **Licensed Field Coverage**
4. **Marketing & Growth**
5. **CRM & Business Operations**

These five categories are broad enough to represent the intended one-stop model without becoming vague, and distinct enough that a Realtor can quickly understand where a need belongs.

The current internal/working labels may remain more detailed where useful, but the website should prefer language Realtors immediately recognize.

## Why five works

Each category answers a different practical Realtor question:

- **Transactions & Contracts:** “Can you help me get the paperwork and deal to closing?”
- **Listing & Seller Support:** “Can you take the operational work around this listing off my plate?”
- **Licensed Field Coverage:** “Can you cover the property/client task when I cannot physically be there?”
- **Marketing & Growth:** “Can you keep my listings, brand, database, and campaigns visible and moving?”
- **CRM & Business Operations:** “Can you help run the business behind my client work?”

A sixth or seventh foundational category is not recommended at this stage.

Open houses, vacation coverage, database nurture, listing launch, and similar needs should be placed inside these umbrellas or sold as integrated solutions rather than promoted as separate foundational categories.

---

# 2. The Core Product Position

Koinonia is not selling a menu of unrelated tasks.

Koinonia is selling **managed real-estate operational capacity**.

The client proposition is:

> **Bring Koinonia the need. We help get it handled.**

Primary message:

> **You focus on your clients. We’ll keep the business running.**

Supporting message:

> **One relationship. More of your business covered.**

The customer should understand that Koinonia can perform work directly, provide approved licensed coverage, or coordinate an approved specialist while remaining the accountable support relationship.

The Realtor should not need to understand Koinonia’s internal fulfillment architecture before asking for help.

# 3. Separate “What We Do” From “How You Buy It”

This distinction is essential.

## What Koinonia does

The five capability umbrellas define **what kinds of work Koinonia can support**.

## How a Realtor buys

Koinonia should give Realtors three simple buying modes:

### A. Get Help With One Need

For a specific request, assignment, project, or immediate problem.

Examples:

- prepare a contract/document request;
- coordinate a showing;
- host an open house;
- provide inspection/vendor access;
- create a listing flyer;
- clean up a CRM;
- build a campaign;
- coordinate a photographer;
- organize a defined business task.

Commercial unit may be per request, per assignment, per project, time block, or quoted scope depending on the work.

### B. Hand Us the File / Property

For work that has a natural lifecycle and should be managed as one engagement rather than many individual tasks.

Examples:

- Transaction Management;
- Listing Launch;
- Full Listing Management;
- Hand Us the Listing — listing through accepted offer / close depending package;
- defined campaign or seller lifecycle engagements.

Commercial unit is generally per transaction, per listing, per lifecycle package, or another defined engagement price.

### C. Build an Ongoing Partnership

For Realtors who want Koinonia embedded in the recurring operation of the business.

Examples:

- CRM and pipeline management;
- client follow-up;
- recurring marketing;
- database nurture;
- business operations;
- listing/transaction coordination allowances;
- field-coverage allowances;
- vacation/overflow support;
- priority service and recurring planning.

Commercial unit is generally monthly recurring support with defined scope, capacity, allowances, and specialist costs.

## Website implication

The website should **not** make a Realtor choose between 40 task products before understanding Koinonia.

A strong site flow is:

1. What Koinonia can cover — five umbrellas.
2. How to work with us — one need / file or property / ongoing partnership.
3. Popular integrated solutions.
4. Detailed examples under each umbrella.
5. Consultation / request support CTA.

# 4. Service Umbrella 1 — Transactions & Contracts

## Client-facing promise

> **From paperwork and contract preparation through contract-to-close coordination, Koinonia helps keep the deal organized, visible, and moving.**

## Customer problem

Transactions create deadline pressure, document volume, communication demands, and repetitive administrative work. Realtors lose selling time when every milestone depends on them personally remembering, chasing, preparing, and organizing the file.

## Major capability groups

### Contract & document support

- offer preparation from Realtor-provided terms;
- counterproposal preparation;
- amendments;
- addenda;
- notices;
- extension documents;
- inspection/repair documentation from Realtor instruction;
- signature-package preparation;
- missing-information review;
- document revision/version organization;
- executed-document follow-up.

### Transaction intake & file setup

- executed contract intake;
- file setup;
- participant/contact setup;
- critical-date extraction;
- brokerage checklist setup;
- missing-document identification;
- earnest-money tracking setup;
- transaction document organization.

### Contract-to-close management

- deadline tracking;
- lender coordination;
- title/closing-company coordination;
- inspection scheduling and tracking;
- appraisal tracking;
- due-diligence/HOA document follow-up;
- repair/vendor coordination;
- amendment/addendum follow-up;
- milestone communication;
- exception/escalation tracking;
- closing scheduling;
- closing preparation;
- final-walkthrough scheduling;
- file closeout and archive.

### Communication & visibility

- Realtor status summaries;
- participant follow-up;
- missing-item reminders;
- milestone confirmations;
- closing reminders;
- client update drafting where appropriate.

## Common Realtor scenarios

- “I have an offer to write and I’m driving between appointments.”
- “We just went under contract — take this file from here.”
- “I have six active files and I’m tired of chasing title, lender, and signatures.”
- “The inspection deadline is coming up; help keep every document and next step organized.”
- “I want a dependable process instead of reinventing my closing checklist every transaction.”

## Fulfillment model

Primarily **Koinonia Direct**, with Agent/Broker approval and judgment gates where required.

## What remains with the Realtor / brokerage

- client advice;
- negotiation strategy and decisions;
- legal interpretation;
- business terms not supplied by the Realtor;
- final professional approval;
- brokerage supervision and compliance responsibility.

## Purchasing model

Recommended sellable forms:

1. **Transaction Management** — per file.
2. **Contract / Document Support** — capability remains important, but standalone pricing must complete Colorado brokerage/legal review before final website publication.
3. **Pay-at-Closing Transaction Management** — retain as a payment option/package if unit economics and compliance remain approved.
4. Included or discounted usage inside an ongoing Koinonia partnership where appropriate.

## Website presentation

Do not create separate top-level cards for “contract drafting,” “deadline tracking,” “closing coordination,” etc.

One Transactions & Contracts card should communicate the breadth, then reveal examples in an expanded section.

Suggested card bullets:

- Contract and document preparation support
- Transaction setup and deadline management
- Lender, title, inspection and closing coordination
- File completion and post-close organization

# 6. Service Umbrella 3 — Licensed Field Coverage

## Client-facing promise

> **When you cannot physically be there, Koinonia helps keep your clients, properties, and appointments covered.**

## Customer problem

Realtors lose time, opportunities, family time, and client responsiveness when every property-access event requires them personally.

This category is a major Koinonia differentiator because remote administrative/marketing providers usually cannot solve in-person licensed field needs.

## Major capability groups

### Buyer / showing coverage

- standard showings;
- same-day requests subject to availability;
- evenings/weekends;
- multi-property tours where approved;
- overlapping-client conflicts;
- distance conflicts;
- vacation/overflow showing coverage;
- appointment coordination;
- completion and feedback.

### Open houses

- licensed host;
- setup and teardown;
- signs/materials;
- visitor registration process;
- Realtor-approved property information;
- lead capture/handoff workflow;
- completion/feedback report;
- marketing coordination through Marketing & Growth.

### Inspection / appraisal / vendor access

- inspection access;
- appraisal access;
- photographer access;
- stager access;
- contractor/vendor access;
- repair-completion access;
- floor-plan/media vendor access.

### Property tasks

- authorized property walk-through / information capture;
- property photo/documentation visit;
- vacancy/property check;
- lockbox pickup/drop-off/install/removal;
- sign placement/pickup where permitted;
- key pickup/drop-off;
- property securement check;
- approved visual status checks.

## Common Realtor scenarios

- “Two buyers need me at the same time.”
- “I’m in Colorado Springs and another client wants to see a house in Denver tonight.”
- “I need somebody to let the inspector in and remain at the property.”
- “I want an open house Saturday but I’m already booked.”
- “The photographer needs access while I’m at a closing.”
- “I’m leaving town for five days and don’t want my clients to lose momentum.”

## Fulfillment model

Primarily **Licensed Coverage**, with internal Koinonia coordination and clear written authority/instructions.

Some supporting tasks may be administrative or specialist-managed, but the public promise should emphasize dependable professional field coverage rather than expose licensing complexity to the client.

## Gated capabilities

Do not include these in the standard public promise until separately approved:

- final-walkthrough representation/coverage;
- physical closing attendance as a generic service;
- closing-document review responsibility;
- dispute handling;
- any representation duty requiring a brokerage relationship not already established.

## Purchasing model

Recommended sellable forms:

1. **Per Assignment** — showing/access/property task.
2. **Open House Coverage** — event-based package.
3. **Multi-stop / extended assignment** — quoted based on duration, distance and complexity.
4. **Vacation / Overflow Coverage** — bundled solution.
5. Defined field allowances or preferred rates inside an ongoing partnership.

## Website presentation

Suggested card bullets:

- Buyer showings and schedule-conflict coverage
- Professional open-house hosting
- Inspection, appraisal and vendor access
- Lockbox, property-check and approved field tasks

Important: Professional Open House Coverage belongs here and should **not** remain a sixth foundational service category.

# 11. What Should Change From the Current Public Model

## Current: Transaction Support

**Keep**, but place beneath Transactions & Contracts.

Public language should emphasize managed contract-to-close operations rather than generic paperwork.

## Current: Contract & Document Support

**Keep as a capability/product**, but do not necessarily keep it as a foundational website card.

Important pricing gate: Colorado Rule 6.14 prohibits a broker or broker’s designee performing brokerage services from charging a separate fee for preparation of legal documents. The existing standalone pricing model must be reviewed before the rewritten site republishes a separate contract-preparation price.

This is a pricing/legal architecture issue, not a reason to remove contract support from Koinonia’s capability model.

## Current: Licensed Showing Coverage

**Expand to Licensed Field Coverage.**

Showings remain a core capability, but the public category should visibly include open houses, inspection/appraisal/vendor access, property checks, and approved property tasks.

## Current: Professional Open House Coverage

**Move under Licensed Field Coverage** and also allow it to appear inside listing packages.

It should not be a foundational sixth category.

## Current: Monthly Operations Partnership

**Reposition from a service category into a buying model/recurring product.**

The monthly plan can draw work from CRM & Business Operations and, depending tier/package, Marketing & Growth and other categories.

## Current: Business Operations Support

**Expand/relabel publicly as CRM & Business Operations.**

The current certified scope is a useful foundation, but the sales presentation should explicitly surface CRM, lead/client follow-up, past-client systems, workflow and recurring business management.

## Missing today: Listing & Seller Support

**Add as a foundational category.**

This closes the major gap before a transaction exists.

## Missing today: Marketing & Growth

**Add as a foundational category.**

This is part of the approved one-stop vision and should not be hidden inside generic admin support.

# 17. Important Regulatory / Commercial Gates Before Final Website Relaunch

## Contract preparation pricing

Colorado Rule 6.14 currently states that if a broker or broker’s designee prepares legal documents while performing real-estate brokerage services, a separate legal-document preparation fee may not be charged.

Therefore:

- retain contract/document preparation as a capability;
- do not automatically carry the current standalone $75 public price into the new site;
- complete brokerage/legal review of the compensation structure first.

## Licensed / brokerage-sensitive work

Before final public claims, confirm the operating model for:

- licensed field assignments;
- open-house coverage;
- any contract preparation involving licensed brokerage duties;
- final walkthroughs;
- closing attendance;
- communications that cross from administration into brokerage representation.

## Marketing

Confirm:

- brokerage advertising approval requirements;
- brand/disclaimer standards;
- platform credentials/access;
- client approval authority;
- ad-spend authorization;
- specialist/vendor disclosure;
- ownership/licensing of creative assets where relevant.

## Specialist/vendor work

Do not imply photographers, videographers, printers, stagers, contractors, or other third parties are Koinonia employees or licensees unless they actually are.
