# 03 — Owner & Prospect Content Playbook

Research date: 2026-11-17 · Status: research input; all published copy requires owner approval

What to show each audience, in what order, based on what owners say they value and what top-converting sites display.

## 1. What owners actually care about (and why they leave)

From Buildium's 2026 survey of 300 rental owners ([Buildium](https://www.buildium.com/blog/how-rental-owners-evaluate-a-property-managers-performance/)):

| Finding | Number | What the site must show |
|---|---|---|
| Owners who rate their manager's value "excellent" | 23% | Room to win on clarity and service |
| Most-valued services: tenant management / maintenance / customer service / responsiveness | 57% / 43% / 34% / 31% | Lead with screening, maintenance process and response standard — not features lists |
| Maintenance is the top stressor | 46% | Show the repair approval process, $250 threshold and vendor standards |
| Owners who don't live near the property | 55% | Speak to remote/relocating owners; photo-documented inspections |
| Judge managers by response time / maintenance turnaround / occupancy / time to lease | 43% / 31% / 30% / 25% | Publish response standard now; publish metrics once real data exists |
| Switch managers because of poor communication / service decline / lack of transparency | 57% / 54% / 34% | "Switching to Koinonia" page; no termination penalty; full fee table |
| Expect same-day / next-business-day response | 43% / 42% | Commit to a written response standard |
| Want consultation on tenant and rent decisions | 50% | Explain the "Managed With You" (8%) plan as a feature, not a discount |
| Want to approve large repairs | 84% | State the approval threshold in plain words |

## 2. Owner page structure (`/owners` and every city page)

Order is based on top competitor patterns (file 01) and ClearLead's guidance to put a free rental analysis above the fold, keep an Owners link on every page, and ask for address + unit count + contact ([ClearLead Digital](https://www.clearleaddigital.com/blog/increase-property-management-leads)).

1. **Hero:** location-specific H1, one-sentence promise, address field → rental analysis (RentCast estimate), phone/text link. Secondary CTA: "Compare plans."
2. **Trust strip (honest version):** "Colorado-licensed oversight" (only once true), "Every fee published," "No termination penalty," "Response within one business day" (once approved). No review stars until real reviews exist.
3. **The two plans:** Managed for You (10%) vs Managed With You (8%), each with who it's for.
4. **What's included at no charge:** setup, vacancy months, initial property assessment, move-in and move-out inspections, owner portal, monthly statements.
5. **How it works (3–4 steps):** analysis → agreement and onboarding → marketing and screening → ongoing management. Colorado RPM's Castle Rock page uses a short three-step process ([Colorado RPM](https://www.coloradorpm.com/castle-rock-property-management/)).
6. **Maintenance and approvals:** $250 approval threshold, owner response expectation, vendor standards (link `/standards`), 10% contractor markup disclosed.
7. **Screening standard:** written criteria applied equally, Colorado-compliant application fee and portable screening reports (file 05).
8. **Proof:** standards now, metrics later (section 4).
9. **FAQ** (with FAQPage schema).
10. **Closing CTA:** rental analysis + "Talk to a person."

### 3b. Market context (shown honestly, not as an attack)

- National average management fee 8.49% of rent; 70.6% of companies bundle placement; average renewal fee $231.77; average setup fee $185.24 ([iPropertyManagement](https://ipropertymanagement.com/research/average-property-management-fees)).
- Denver-area ranges: 8–12% management, $200–$500 renewal, 10–15% maintenance markup, $200–$500 setup ([RentPro](https://renpro.com/property-management-cost-denver-co/)).
- Denver first-year cost for a $3,000/month home ranges $1,647–$6,240, median $4,719 across 26 firms ([iPropertyManagement](https://ipropertymanagement.com/answers/what-does-a-property-manager-cost-in-denver)).
- Local direct competitor: Bergan's Parker page advertises 10% management, 50% placement, free renewals and no upfront fees ([Bergan & Co](https://www.berganco.com/areas-we-serve/parker-colorado)).

### 3c. All-in first-year example (recommended format)

Assumptions: $2,800/month rent (near Parker's 3BR median of $2,800 per [TurboTenant](https://www.turbotenant.com/property-management/services/colorado/parker/)), one placement, 12 months rented, no mid-lease inspection, repairs excluded.

| Line | Managed for You (10%) | Managed With You (8%) |
|---|---|---|
| Management (12 × rent × rate) | $3,360 | $2,688 |
| Placement (50% of one month) | $1,400 | $1,400 |
| Setup | $0 | $0 |
| **First-year total (before repairs)** | **$4,760** | **$4,088** |
| Year 2 if tenant renews ($199 renewal) | $3,559 | $2,887 |

Scaling the Denver median ($4,719 at $3,000 rent) to $2,800 rent gives about $4,400, so the 10% plan's first year is slightly above the median and the 8% plan slightly below — the message is "transparent and fair," not "cheapest." Include a calculator on the page so owners can enter their own rent. Under HB25-1090, any advertised price must disclose the total price the person may pay (file 05).

## 4. Proof strategy for a new company

Koinonia has no track record yet. Top sites use dated metrics (Grace shows a dated metrics block with definitions — [Grace Property Management](https://www.rentgrace.com/Castle-Rock-property-management); Colorado RPM shows 34 days on market, 9 showings, 3.44-hour email response and 80% renewal — [Colorado RPM](https://www.coloradorpm.com/castle-rock-property-management/)). Koinonia should:

1. **Launch with standards, not stats:** published response standard, inspection schedule, repair-approval rules, payout date, screening criteria, vendor standards.
2. **Track from day one:** days on market, showings per lease, first-response time, maintenance first-response and completion time, renewal rate, owner retention.
3. **Publish metrics after enough data** (suggest ≥10 leases or 6 months), dated, with definitions — following Grace's format.
4. **Founder/team story with real photos**, local roots, and who answers the phone.
5. **Reviews:** request after move-in, first statement and each completed repair (owners) and after move-in (residents). Display only verified reviews.
6. **Transaction track record** from Koinonia's sales side may be used only if accurate and attributed to the right licensed entity.

## 6. Prospect (renter) content

Renters are a secondary audience but drive vacancy speed (and owner outcomes):
- `/rentals`: listings with photos, rent, total monthly cost disclosure, availability date, pet policy, apply link.
- `/apply`: written screening criteria, application fee equal to actual cost, portable screening report acceptance (file 05).
- `/portals` and `/maintenance`: clearly labeled so tenants searching "Koinonia portal" don't land on owner pages.
- Equal Housing Opportunity logo and statement on every page.

## 7. Sales-line content (equal prominence)

- `/real-estate` landing with Buy, Sell and Investors paths.
- **Investor path** bridges both lines: buy a rental → we lease and manage it → rent-or-sell later. Castle Rock median sold price was $715,000 with 57 median days on market ([Realtor.com](https://www.realtor.com/local/market/colorado/douglas-county/castle-rock)); rent levels around $2,500–$3,000 (file 02) mean many homes are cash-flow-negative at current prices — the content should be honest about that.
- Every page names the licensed brokerage of record (file 05).
