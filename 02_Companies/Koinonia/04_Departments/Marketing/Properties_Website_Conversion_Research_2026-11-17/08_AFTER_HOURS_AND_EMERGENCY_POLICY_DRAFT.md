# 08 — After-Hours and Emergency Policy (Draft)

Status: **draft for owner review** · not legally reviewed · not published. Have the managing broker and a Colorado landlord-tenant attorney review it before it goes into leases, management agreements or the website.

Business hours (owner-approved 2026-11-17): **Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. Mountain Time**, excluding posted holidays.

## 1. Why the policy has to cover weekends

Colorado's warranty-of-habitability law runs on calendar hours, not business days. After a landlord gets written or electronic notice of a habitability condition ([C.R.S. 38-12-503](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-38/tenants-and-landlords/article-12/part-5/section-38-12-503/)):

| Requirement | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Contact the tenant with the plan, including when work will start and finish | **24 hours** (72 hours if the home can't be reached because of an environmental public health event) |
| Start remedial action — condition materially interferes with life, health or safety | **24 hours** |
| Start remedial action — home otherwise uninhabitable | **72 hours** |
| Mold/dampness: containment, stop the water source, HEPA filtration | **72 hours** |
| Presumed failure if the condition still exists | 7 calendar days (life/health/safety) · 14 calendar days (other uninhabitable conditions) |
| Missing the 24-hour contact deadline | Itself creates a presumption of failure |

For life, health or safety conditions, the tenant can also request a comparable unit or hotel room under the same statute. Heat loss is treated as an emergency with a 24-hour start clock ([Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/local-news/colorado-colder-months-are-here-what-are-renters-right-when-it-comes-to-staying-warm)).

**So "next business day" can't apply to habitability notices.** A notice sent at 5:30 p.m. Friday has a 24-hour contact deadline of 5:30 p.m. Saturday. The policy therefore puts every request into one of three tiers, and only the routine tier waits for business hours.

## 2. Request tiers

| Tier | Examples | Koinonia response | Work starts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Emergency** (24/7) | Fire or smoke damage (after 911); gas smell or CO alarm (after leaving and calling 911/the gas utility); active water leak or flooding that the shutoff doesn't stop; burst or frozen pipes; sewage backup; no heat in cold weather; no water to the home; electrical sparking, burning smell or total loss of power that isn't a utility outage; break-in, or an exterior door/window that won't lock | On-call person calls back **within 1 hour**, 24/7 | Vendor dispatched to make it safe **as soon as possible, never later than 24 hours** |
| **2 — Urgent habitability** (7 days a week) | No hot water; one of several toilets not working; refrigerator or stove out; active mold or dampness; pest infestation; broken window that still locks; smoke/CO detector chirping or missing | Written reply with the plan **within 24 hours**, including weekends and holidays | Within **24 hours** if it affects life, health or safety; otherwise within **72 hours** |
| **3 — Routine** | Dripping faucet, appliance noise, cosmetic damage, garage door remote, landscaping, questions | Auto-acknowledged right away; **next steps within 1 hour** of opening on the next business day | Same or next business day (owner-approved standard) |

When unsure, staff treat it as the higher tier. Air conditioning failure is Tier 2 by default and Tier 1 during extreme heat or when the resident reports a medical need.

## 3. How residents report

1. **Life-threatening: call 911 first.** For gas smell, leave the home, then call the gas utility's emergency line (listed on the move-in sheet and resident portal).
2. **Tier 1 after hours: call the after-hours emergency line.** It reaches a live person or answering service 24/7. Text or portal messages alone are not enough for emergencies, and residents are told this at move-in.
3. **Everything else:** resident portal (`/maintenance`) or email, with photos. Each submission gets an automatic receipt with a reference number and the time it was logged — this timestamp starts the legal clock.

Resident portal and phone menu should be accessible (relay service, TTY) and available in the languages Koinonia can support. No fees are charged to residents for using the emergency line (fee restrictions under HB25-1090 — see file 05).

## 4. What residents should do first (move-in sheet)

- Know where the water main shutoff, breaker panel and gas shutoff are (photos in the move-in packet).
- Water leak: turn off the fixture valve or main, move belongings, then call.
- Power loss: check the breaker panel and the utility outage map first.
- No heat: check the thermostat batteries and the furnace filter/switch, then call.
- Freeze season: keep heat at or above 55°F and cabinet doors open during cold snaps (local guidance; confirm wording).

## 5. On-call procedure (internal)

| Step | Standard |
|---|---|
| Coverage | One named on-call person every evening, weekend and holiday, with a named backup; rotation posted weekly |
| Answering | After-hours line → answering service or call forwarding → on-call phone; voicemail-only is not acceptable for Tier 1 |
| Callback | Tier 1 within 1 hour; Tier 2 written plan within 24 hours |
| Dispatch | Make-safe first (stop the water, secure the door, restore heat); full repair can follow during business hours if the make-safe step resolves the risk |
| Owner notice | Tier 1: text/call the owner as soon as practical; always a written summary by 9 a.m. the next business day. Tier 2: included in the next-business-day update |
| Documentation | Timestamp of notice, tenant contact, dispatch, vendor arrival, photos before/after, invoice — all in the work order |
| Escalation | If the on-call person can't reach a vendor within 1 hour on a Tier 1 issue, escalate to the backup and the broker |
| Lodging | For life/health/safety conditions, offer a comparable unit or hotel when the resident asks, as the statute requires; record the request |

## 6. Owner authority and costs

- **Routine work** keeps the owner-approved $250 approval threshold.
- **Emergencies:** the management agreement should authorize Koinonia to spend what is needed to make the home safe and meet legal deadlines without waiting for owner approval, with a stated cap (proposed: **$1,000** per incident) above which Koinonia will try to reach the owner but may still act when the law or safety requires it. This matches the pricing memo rule that no approval threshold may delay emergency, safety or statutory responses.
- The 10% contractor markup applies to Koinonia-coordinated emergency work under both plans.
- On the 8% owner-involved plan, Koinonia still handles Tier 1 and Tier 2 issues after hours; the owner's vendor may be used only if they can meet the deadlines.
- Damage caused by a resident or guest may be charged back only as the lease and Colorado law allow.
- After-hours vendor premiums are passed through at cost (plus the disclosed markup) and shown on the owner statement.

## 7. Owners and prospects after hours

- **Owner messages:** auto-acknowledged; reply with next steps within 1 hour of opening on the next business day (by 9:00 a.m.). Tier 1 issues at an owner's property are reported right away as above.
- **New owner and leasing inquiries:** automatic text/email within 5 minutes (file 04 §3); a person follows up within 1 hour of opening on the next business day.

## 8. Holidays (proposed)

New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day and the day after, Christmas Eve afternoon, Christmas Day. Tiers 1 and 2 run the same on holidays.

## 9. Public website wording (draft)

> **Office hours:** Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Mountain Time. During office hours we respond with next steps within one hour and start on the issue the same or next business day.
>
> **After hours, weekends and holidays:** For fire, a gas smell or anything life-threatening, call 911 first. For emergencies like flooding, no heat, sewage backup or a home that can't be secured, call our 24/7 emergency line at [number] — a person will call you back within one hour. Urgent repairs reported through the resident portal are answered within 24 hours, every day of the week. Routine requests are handled the next business day.
