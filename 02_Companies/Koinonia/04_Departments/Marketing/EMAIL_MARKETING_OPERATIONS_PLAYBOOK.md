# Koinonia Transactions — Email Marketing Operations Playbook

Status: Build-Mode Operating Playbook  
Business: Koinonia Transactions  
Owner: Marketing + Sales  
Effective Date: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

Define how Koinonia uses email across personal outreach, warm relationship follow-up, brokerage outreach, and permission-based nurture without confusing those modes or creating unmanaged bulk-email risk.

This playbook is a build-stage system definition. It does not authorize sending any marketing email.

Until activation is explicitly approved:

- do not send cold prospecting emails
- do not send warm follow-up campaigns
- do not enroll contacts in nurture
- do not upload public prospect research into a bulk email platform
- do not launch newsletters or broadcasts
- do not turn draft email sequences into live automations

---

# Core Email Principle

Koinonia should use email to continue a relevant professional relationship, not to spray the full service menu at a list.

Use:

**Context -> Pressure -> Useful Question -> Relevant Next Step**

Avoid:

**Generic intro -> service dump -> repeated sales follow-up**

The goal of the first email is usually not to close a sale.

The goal is to earn the next useful conversation.

---

# Email Modes

Koinonia has four distinct email modes.

## Mode 1 — Personal One-to-One Outbound

Use for:

- a specific Realtor
- a specific brokerage leader
- a professional partner
- a researched prospect with a legitimate business contact path

Characteristics:

- manually reviewed
- individualized context
- sent as one-to-one professional correspondence
- low volume during the first learning cycle
- no fake personalization
- not automatically enrolled into a newsletter

Primary objective:

Discover whether there is a real operational pressure worth discussing.

## Mode 2 — Warm Relationship Follow-Up

Use after:

- meeting someone
- a brokerage presentation
- an event conversation
- a referral/introduction
- a meaningful social interaction
- a consultation
- prior Koinonia service

Characteristics:

- references real prior context
- follows up promptly
- recommends only the smallest relevant next step

Primary objective:

Continue the relationship while the context is still useful.

## Mode 3 — Permission-Based Nurture / Subscription Email

Use for contacts who have a legitimate basis to receive ongoing marketing/educational email and whose consent/relationship status is handled appropriately.

Examples:

- opted-in website subscriber
- explicit request for updates/resources
- client/prospect relationship where ongoing marketing is appropriate and opt-out is provided
- brokerage audience that has been properly enrolled through an approved process

Characteristics:

- sent through a compliant campaign platform
- unsubscribe/suppression supported
- sender authentication configured
- list hygiene maintained
- useful educational cadence

Primary objective:

Stay useful and relevant until timing changes.

## Mode 4 — Transactional / Relationship-Service Email

Examples:

- consultation confirmation
- active service communication
- scheduling information
- invoices/receipts
- requested documents or service updates

These messages are not governed like a newsletter simply because they are email.

Do not mix unnecessary promotional material into service-critical transactional messages.

---

# Decision Tree: Which Email Mode?

## Did the person request a service or consultation?

Use transactional/relationship-service email first.

If useful marketing follow-up is later appropriate, classify separately.

## Did Koinonia recently meet or speak with the person?

Use warm relationship follow-up.

## Is this a specific researched professional prospect with no prior relationship?

Use one-to-one outbound only after activation and batch approval.

Do not automatically add them to nurture.

## Has the person explicitly opted into ongoing updates or otherwise been validly enrolled in a compliant nurture process?

Use permission-based nurture.

## Is the basis unclear?

Do not enroll them in ongoing marketing until the basis is clarified.

# List / Contact Source Rules

A contact source must be known before email activation.

Approved source categories include:

- website consultation
- website opt-in/subscription
- existing client
- prior prospect relationship
- brokerage meeting/event
- Realtor networking/event
- direct introduction
- professional partner introduction
- social DM/comment relationship
- public professional prospect research
- manual business-card capture

For each email recipient, Koinonia should be able to answer:

1. Who is this person?
2. Why do we have their email address?
3. What relationship or context exists?
4. Which email mode applies?
5. What should happen if they do not want more marketing email?

Do not use:

- purchased giant cold lists
- harvested personal addresses with no legitimate business context
- scraped social follower emails
- unknown-source spreadsheet imports

# Personal One-to-One Outbound Rules

During implementation, outbound should remain controlled and manually reviewed during the first learning cycle.

## Required before first send

- prospect qualifies under Prospecting & Lead-Sourcing Playbook
- professional contact route is legitimate
- personalization basis is real
- first-message purpose is clear
- sender identity is accurate
- subject is not deceptive
- postal/contact compliance requirements are handled where applicable
- suppression/do-not-contact status checked
- Relationship capture process ready

## First-email structure

1. brief real context
2. one sentence explaining Koinonia's category
3. one useful question about operational pressure
4. no full pricing/service dump
5. no aggressive scheduling demand

Core diagnostic opener:

**What part of the business keeps falling back on you even when you're busy with clients?**

## Initial working sequence

### Day 0 — Intro / Question

Goal:

Earn a response about workload/pressure.

### Day 4 — Useful Scenario

Goal:

Show Koinonia understands multiple forms of operational pressure.

### Day 10 — Smallest Entry Point

Goal:

Show the person does not need to buy a giant package.

Use the principle:

**The first engagement should earn the second engagement.**

### Day 21 — Close the Loop

Goal:

Stop active inbox chasing respectfully.

After this:

- stop the active cold sequence
- use long-term nurture only if an appropriate nurture basis exists
- otherwise close/no-response

Do not create infinite follow-up automation.

---

# Warm Follow-Up Rules

Warm follow-up should usually occur while the interaction is still fresh.

Recommended target:

- same day when practical
- within 24 hours for normal professional follow-up

Structure:

1. reference where/how you met
2. reference the exact issue they mentioned
3. reflect the pressure in their language
4. recommend the smallest sufficient next step
5. give one concrete next action

Formula:

**You mentioned [problem]. Based on that, I would start with [service] so we can [specific desired outcome].**

Only attach or link collateral when useful.

Do not attach the full marketing library by default.

---

# Brokerage Email Rules

Treat a brokerage/team as an account.

Do not send duplicate generic emails to many agents and leaders in the same office at the same time.

Primary first ask:

Offer a practical 15–20 minute agent/team session on protecting client service when operational workload and transaction volume collide.

Brokerage email should emphasize:

- agent education
- defined boundaries
- optional support resource
- no attempt to replace broker supervision
- no assumption that every agent needs the same service

Follow-up should occur at the account level before individual agent follow-up is activated.

---

# Nurture / Subscription Strategy

Koinonia should not publish a newsletter merely to maintain a schedule.

Initial working cadence:

- approximately 1–2 useful nurture emails per month

Possible themes:

- capacity questions
- transaction organization lessons
- showing/open-house planning
- CRM/follow-up operations
- service-boundary education
- real operational scenarios

Every nurture email should answer:

**Why is this useful to a Colorado Realtor even if they do not buy today?**

Avoid:

- weekly promotional blasts without value
- constant pricing reminders
- every email containing every service
- referral-option promotion in every send

---

# Subscription / Suppression Status Model

Every person in a campaign platform should have a clear status.

## Subscribed / Marketable

Appropriate for ongoing nurture under the approved consent/relationship process.

## Transactional Only

Can receive service/relationship messages but should not receive ongoing marketing unless separately eligible.

## One-to-One Prospecting Only

May be used for individually reviewed professional outreach if approved, but is not enrolled in bulk nurture.

## Unsubscribed / Suppressed

Do not send further marketing email.

## Do Not Contact

Broader stop flag where appropriate.

## Bounced / Invalid

Do not keep retrying automatically.

## Complaint / Spam Report

Suppress promptly and investigate the source/process.

---

# Unsubscribe / Opt-Out Workflow

For commercial marketing messages, Koinonia must provide a clear way to stop future marketing email and honor valid opt-out requests promptly.

Operational standard:

- unsubscribe request immediately updates suppression status in the sending platform
- manual requests such as "please remove me" are also recorded
- suppression is checked before every future campaign
- suppressed addresses are not re-added through a later spreadsheet import
- suppression data is preserved even if the active marketing record is otherwise archived

Do not require a person to explain why they are unsubscribing.

Do not make unsubscribe depend on a login.

FTC compliance requires honoring opt-out requests within 10 business days; Koinonia should operationally process them much faster whenever possible.

---

# CAN-SPAM Operational Checklist

Before commercial email activation, verify:

- From/To/Reply-To/routing information is accurate
- sender identity is not misleading
- subject line accurately reflects the content
- promotional/commercial nature is not deceptively disguised
- valid physical postal address is present where required
- clear opt-out method is present in applicable marketing messages
- opt-out mechanism remains functional as required
- valid opt-outs are honored within 10 business days at the latest
- vendors/platforms sending on Koinonia's behalf are monitored

Koinonia remains responsible for compliant practices even when a third-party platform is used.

# Sending Infrastructure Requirements

## One-to-One Personal Email

May use the approved professional mailbox when implementation begins, subject to normal sending limits and domain authentication.

Do not turn the personal mailbox into a high-volume cold-email engine.

## Nurture / Broadcast Email

Use a dedicated campaign platform that supports at minimum:

- authenticated custom sending domain
- SPF/DKIM support
- DMARC-compatible configuration
- unsubscribe links
- suppression management
- one-click unsubscribe support where required by mailbox-provider rules
- bounce handling
- complaint/spam handling
- segmentation
- campaign reporting
- export/access to contact consent/source data

Google currently requires SPF or DKIM for all senders to Gmail accounts and stronger authentication for high-volume senders; bulk marketing traffic also has unsubscribe requirements. Koinonia should configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC before scaling even if initial volume is far below bulk thresholds.

---

# Sending Platform Selection Criteria

Do not choose a platform solely because it is cheap or has the largest template library.

Score candidates on:

## 1. Compliance Operations

- unsubscribe/suppression
- consent/source fields
- bounce/complaint handling
- auditability

## 2. Deliverability

- authenticated domains
- SPF/DKIM/DMARC support
- sender reputation controls
- clear sending-limit guidance

## 3. CRM / Relationship Integration

- export/API/webhook options
- source/campaign fields
- ability to sync unsubscribes safely

## 4. Segmentation

Must support distinctions such as:

- Realtors
- brokerage leaders
- clients
- prior clients
- nurture subscribers
- referral/professional partners

## 5. Reporting

At minimum:

- delivered
- bounced
- unsubscribed
- complaints where exposed
- clicks
- replies where integration allows

Open rate should be treated cautiously because privacy features can make it noisy.

## 6. Operational Simplicity

The platform should be manageable without creating a separate marketing technology project.

# Domain / Sender Standard

Preferred principle:

Use a real Koinonia-controlled domain identity for marketing and relationship email.

Do not send marketing from:

- a deceptive lookalike domain
- unrelated personal domain
- no-reply address when a reply would be useful

Different email categories may use different sender addresses later, but the sender identity should remain recognizable.

Potential future structure:

- relationship/outreach: human sender address
- nurture: recognizable Koinonia marketing or team sender
- transactional system: system/notification sender

Do not finalize sender addresses until DNS/authentication and operational ownership are confirmed.

# Email Content QA Checklist

Before any marketing email is approved for sending:

## Audience

- correct segment
- correct email mode
- no suppressed recipients
- source/basis known

## Sender

- From name correct
- From address correct
- Reply-To correct
- signature correct

## Subject

- accurate
- not deceptive
- not fake urgency

## Body

- one primary job
- useful/relevant
- service names current
- pricing current if included
- Referral Partner path clearly separate when mentioned
- no unsupported promises
- no confidential information

## Links

- correct destination
- HTTPS
- UTM naming correct when appropriate
- consultation/privacy links live when relied upon

## Compliance Footer

For nurture/broadcast commercial messages:

- postal address present where required
- unsubscribe present
- business identity clear

## Technical

- test email rendered on desktop/mobile
- no broken personalization tokens
- images have appropriate alt text where useful
- plain-text version acceptable

## Final Approval

- sender list count reviewed
- scheduled date/time reviewed
- campaign ID recorded
- activation approval in effect

During build mode, stop at **Approved — Not Sent**.

# UTM Standard for Email

Use lowercase.

## Source

`email`

## Medium

Use:

- `personal_outbound`
- `warm_followup`
- `nurture`
- `brokerage_outreach`

## Campaign

Use a stable campaign identifier.

Examples:

- `aug2026_launch`
- `capacity_nurture`
- `brokerage_agent_session`

## Content

Identify the message/version/CTA.

Examples:

- `e1_initial_question`
- `e3_smallest_entry`
- `n1_capacity_question`
- `b1_agent_session`

Do not add unnecessary UTM parameters to ordinary service/transactional links where marketing attribution is not relevant.

# Reply Handling

Replies outrank sequence completion.

If someone replies:

- stop automated/templated follow-up for that person
- read the actual response
- classify the pressure/objection/context
- respond personally
- update/create the Relationship record
- assign next action/date

Do not let a sequence send Day 10 automatically after a meaningful Day 4 reply.

---

# Response Categories

## Interested / describes pressure

Move into Lead Response & Consultation workflow.

## Not now

Record timing/context.

Use nurture later only if appropriate.

## Already have support

Respect it.

Ask no aggressive follow-up.

Koinonia may still be relevant for narrow overflow such as showings/open houses if the person identifies that need.

## No need

Close respectfully.

## Unsubscribe / stop

Suppress immediately.

## Referral-path interest

Clarify whether they want to keep the client and need capacity help or explicitly refer the client.

## Out-of-office

Do not treat as engagement.

Adjust timing if appropriate.

---

# Bounce / Complaint Handling

## Hard bounce

- mark invalid/bounced
- suppress from future campaigns
- do not repeatedly retry

## Soft bounce

- allow platform-standard limited retry
- investigate recurring failures

## Spam complaint

- suppress
- investigate source and message type
- review whether enrollment was appropriate

## High complaint/unsubscribe pattern

Pause the campaign/list source and diagnose before scaling.

Do not treat complaints as merely a deliverability problem; they may indicate a targeting or permission problem.

# Reporting Hierarchy

Email is measured by business value, not just opens.

## Primary

- meaningful replies
- qualified conversations
- consultation requests
- service requests
- first paid engagements
- repeat/cross-service outcomes later

## Secondary

- clicks
- unsubscribe rate
- bounce rate
- complaint rate
- reply rate

## Diagnostic

- delivery rate
- open rate
- click-to-open type metrics

Open rate should not be treated as ground truth because mailbox privacy features can distort it.

---

# First-50 Email Learning Rule

For the first meaningful relationship cycle:

Do not optimize for maximum sends.

Learn:

- which first questions earn real replies
- which pressure categories appear most often
- which personalization actually matters
- which objections recur
- which contact sources produce useful conversations
- which service becomes the first engagement

A five-email batch that creates two real conversations may be more valuable than a 500-recipient send that creates none.

---

# Email Automation Boundary

Automation may later help with:

- nurture scheduling
- unsubscribe processing
- bounce handling
- campaign reporting
- reminders to follow up

Automation should not replace:

- diagnosis
- personalized warm responses
- interpretation of objections
- service recommendation
- important relationship judgment

No autonomous cold-email blasting is part of the initial Koinonia marketing system.

---

# Build-Mode Activation Gate

Email remains inactive until all are true:

1. Koinonia has entered implementation mode.
2. Email modes/segments are approved.
3. sender domain/address is approved.
4. SPF/DKIM/DMARC readiness is verified for scaled sends.
5. nurture/broadcast platform is selected and configured.
6. physical postal address/footer requirement is resolved.
7. unsubscribe/suppression workflow is tested.
8. source/permission fields exist.
9. first outbound/nurture batch is reviewed.
10. Relationship capture and reply handling are ready.
11. website/privacy destinations relied upon by email are live.
12. the user explicitly approves the email activation phase.

Until then, drafts remain **Approved — Not Sent**.
