# Koinonia Transactions — Prospecting & Lead-Sourcing Playbook

Status: Build-Mode Operating Playbook  
Business: Koinonia Transactions  
Owner: Marketing + Sales  
Effective Date: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

Define how Koinonia identifies, qualifies, prioritizes, records, and eventually activates prospective Realtor and brokerage relationships.

This playbook is a **build-stage system definition**. It does not authorize outreach.

Current rule:

**Build the marketing machine first. Implement it only after the system is complete and activation is explicitly approved.**

Until activation:

- do not send prospecting emails
- do not send cold DMs
- do not send promotional texts
- do not launch ads
- do not publish social posts
- do not treat researched names as active sales targets

Research may be used to validate the system, but research is not outreach.

---

# Core Prospecting Principle

Koinonia should not ask:

**Who can we sell every service to?**

Koinonia should ask:

**Which Colorado Realtor or brokerage is likely to experience an operational pressure that Koinonia can solve with the smallest sufficient engagement?**

Prospecting exists to identify likely pressure and create a reason for a useful conversation later.

The order is:

**Find -> Qualify -> Understand -> Prioritize -> Prepare -> Approve -> Contact -> Diagnose -> Recommend**

Do not skip from Find directly to Contact during build mode.

# Prospect Types

## 1. Warm Realtor Relationship

A Realtor Jeremiah or Koinonia already knows through:

- past professional interaction
- brokerage relationship
- event/networking connection
- previous real estate work
- lender/title/professional introduction
- prior Koinonia interaction
- personal introduction with legitimate business context

Warm does not mean the person has expressed interest.

Warm means the relationship already exists.

## 2. Engaged Inbound Prospect

A Realtor who initiates or meaningfully engages through:

- website form
- consultation request
- Facebook comment/DM
- Instagram comment/DM
- TikTok comment/DM
- response to educational content
- event interaction
- referral from another professional

This person is no longer pure prospect research. They belong in the active Relationship system.

## 3. Public Professional Prospect

A Colorado real estate professional identified through legitimate public professional information such as:

- brokerage roster
- agent website
- professional directory
- Realtor association directory where permitted
- public social business profile
- professional event roster
- publicly advertised open house/listing activity

Public professional information may support research and later controlled one-to-one business outreach.

It does not automatically authorize bulk email, automated text, or mass list enrollment.

## 4. Brokerage / Team Account

An organization-level relationship such as:

- brokerage office
- real estate team
- office manager
- managing/employing broker
- team leader
- productivity/agent-development leader

The account may contain multiple individual relationships.

Do not treat every agent in a brokerage as a separate cold target merely because one brokerage was identified.

## 5. Professional Referral Source

Potential relationship sources include:

- lenders
- title professionals
- inspectors
- photographers
- attorneys where appropriate
- brokerage leaders
- existing Realtor clients
- other real estate professionals

These relationships may produce introductions rather than direct service purchases.

## 6. Re-Engagement Prospect

A person who previously:

- asked about Koinonia
- used one service
- attended a presentation
- downloaded/received material
- said "not now"
- had a consultation but did not purchase

Re-engagement is not the same as cold prospecting.

Preserve the original context and reason the relationship did not advance.

# Lead-Sourcing Channels

## A. Existing Network Mining

Purpose:

Find people where a real relationship already exists.

Sources to review during implementation:

- Google Contacts
- Gmail relationship history
- prior transaction contacts where appropriate
- brokerage/event contacts
- business-card records
- referral partners
- existing Koinonia Relationships

Build-mode task:

Define the search terms and data fields.

Implementation task later:

Actually identify and classify the people.

## B. Brokerage Relationship Mapping

Purpose:

Identify offices or teams where one useful relationship can create repeated agent exposure.

Research fields:

- brokerage/team name
- market/location
- office size where reasonably public
- leadership role
- public contact path
- meeting/event structure if public
- apparent agent-development culture
- current support resources where public
- likely Koinonia education angle

Do not assume a brokerage needs outside support simply because it has many agents.

## C. Agent Public-Profile Research

Purpose:

Identify individual Realtors whose visible business model suggests one or more possible capacity pressures.

Useful public signals may include:

- active listings
- frequent open houses
- wide geographic service area
- team activity
- buyer-heavy business
- listing-heavy business
- visible business growth
- repeated educational/marketing activity
- solo practice with meaningful production

These are **signals**, not facts about workload.

Never say:

"I saw you are overwhelmed."

Instead later ask:

"What part of the business keeps falling back on you when client activity gets busy?"

## D. Social Engagement Sourcing

Purpose:

Turn meaningful content engagement into relationship opportunities.

A social interaction becomes a prospect signal when someone:

- asks how a service works
- describes a workload problem
- asks about price/availability
- sends a DM
- comments with a real operational scenario
- visits/contact-converts from an attributable post

Do not treat likes/follows alone as permission for direct sales outreach.

## E. Event / Networking Sourcing

Potential sources:

- Realtor association events
- brokerage events
- industry education
- open houses
- vendor events
- community professional events

Capture:

- event
- date
- person
- brokerage
- actual conversation
- exact problem language
- material given
- next step if any

## F. Professional Partner Sourcing

Build relationships with professionals who regularly see Realtor capacity problems.

The best partner message is not:

"Send us leads."

It is:

**When one of your Realtor relationships needs dependable operational capacity, Koinonia can be a useful resource without replacing the Realtor-client relationship.**

## G. Paid Lead Sources

Paid sources are activated only after marketing infrastructure is approved.

Planned sequence:

1. Meta lead generation
2. TikTok organic learning
3. TikTok paid amplification when justified
4. retargeting once sufficient traffic exists

Paid leads enter the same Relationship system as every other source.

Do not create a separate "ad leads" sales process.

# Prospect Qualification Framework

A prospect does not need to meet every criterion.

The purpose is to prioritize likely-fit relationships, not exclude everyone who is unfamiliar.

## Fit Dimension 1 — Geography

### Strong fit

- Colorado Realtor
- primary service activity inside Koinonia's practical licensed/service footprint

### Lower fit

- mostly outside Colorado
- geography makes showing/open-house services impractical

Transaction/document/operations support may still be relevant where scope/licensing permits, but Koinonia should not obscure geographic limitations.

## Fit Dimension 2 — Role

### Strong fit

- active Realtor
- team lead
- producing broker
- brokerage/office leader

### Secondary fit

- industry professional capable of meaningful introductions

### Weak fit

- consumer with no professional real-estate relationship to the service offer

## Fit Dimension 3 — Likely Operational Pressure

Potential signals:

- transaction/file capacity
- contract/document workload
- showing/schedule conflicts
- open-house/listing capacity
- CRM/follow-up/business organization
- referral/no-capacity opportunity
- brokerage/team operations

Do not classify a public signal as confirmed pressure until the prospect says it or behavior clearly establishes it.

Use:

**Likely Pressure — Unconfirmed**

until diagnosis occurs.

## Fit Dimension 4 — Smallest Useful Entry Point

Strong prospects have at least one plausible low-friction starting point such as:

- one showing
- one document assignment
- one open house
- one transaction
- one operations consultation

If the only imaginable sale requires replacing an existing internal team or changing the prospect's entire business, the relationship is lower priority.

## Fit Dimension 5 — Relationship Accessibility

Higher priority when there is:

- prior relationship
- mutual introduction
- shared event
- public professional email
- brokerage-level introduction
- meaningful social engagement

Lower priority when the only route is intrusive or uncertain.

## Fit Dimension 6 — Strategic Learning Value

During the first 50 meaningful relationships, prioritize prospects that help Koinonia learn across different categories.

Example:

Do not research 50 identical solo buyer agents if Koinonia has learned nothing yet about listing agents, team leaders, open-house pressure, or monthly operations needs.

# Prospect Score

Use a simple score only as a sorting aid.

Do not let scoring replace human judgment.

Score each dimension 0–2.

## Geography

- 2 = strong Colorado/local fit
- 1 = Colorado but service-distance limitations
- 0 = outside practical market

## Professional Role

- 2 = Realtor/team/brokerage decision-maker
- 1 = adjacent professional/referral source
- 0 = unclear/non-fit

## Pressure Signal

- 2 = clear likely pressure signal or inbound self-identification
- 1 = plausible but generic
- 0 = no relevant signal

## Entry-Service Fit

- 2 = obvious small entry point
- 1 = possible but unclear
- 0 = only broad/complex engagement imaginable

## Relationship Access

- 2 = warm, introduced, engaged, or clear business contact route
- 1 = public professional cold route
- 0 = intrusive/unclear route

## Learning Value

- 2 = helps test an important Koinonia hypothesis/category
- 1 = useful but repetitive
- 0 = low learning value

Maximum = 12.

Suggested planning tiers:

- 10–12 = Priority A research prospect
- 7–9 = Priority B research prospect
- 4–6 = Priority C / hold
- 0–3 = do not prioritize

The score does **not** authorize contact.

# Prospect Status Model

Use a pre-outreach planning state separate from the active sales lifecycle.

## Research — Unreviewed

A prospect has been identified but not evaluated.

## Research — Qualified

The prospect meets fit criteria and has a legitimate future contact path.

## Research — Hold

Potential fit, but not enough reason to prioritize now.

## Research — Do Not Contact

Examples:

- explicit opt-out known
- inappropriate contact path
- clear non-fit
- compliance concern
- duplicate relationship

## Ready for Activation Review

The prospect has:

- fit
- reason for contact
- approved channel
- personalization basis
- planned first message

This still does not send anything.

## Approved for Outreach

Used only after Koinonia intentionally enters implementation mode and the specific outreach batch is approved.

Once actual contact occurs, the person becomes an active Relationship and uses normal lifecycle/source rules.

---

# Activation Gate

No prospect moves from research into outreach until all of these are true:

1. Koinonia has intentionally entered marketing implementation mode.
2. The relevant outreach channel is operationally ready.
3. The first-message template is approved.
4. The person has a legitimate professional contact path.
5. Personalization is based on real information, not invented assumptions.
6. Contact frequency and opt-out handling are defined.
7. A Relationship record or immediate capture process exists.
8. Every contacted person can receive a next action/date.
9. The batch size is intentionally limited for learning.
10. The user has approved activation of that outreach phase.

If any item is false, remain in research/build mode.

# Personalization Standard

Personalization should answer:

**Why this person, why this message, why now?**

Acceptable personalization:

- real prior conversation
- shared event
- public professional role
- public service area
- public team/listing business model
- a post they made that genuinely relates to Koinonia's category

Unacceptable personalization:

- invented workload assumptions
- implying access to private transaction data
- fake familiarity
- generic mail-merge details presented as insight

Bad:

"I know you're drowning in transactions."

Better:

"I work with Colorado Realtors on the operational work that tends to compete with client time. I'm curious which part of the business keeps falling back on you when activity gets busy."

# Lead-Sourcing Research Rhythm — Future

Once implementation begins, a sustainable prospecting rhythm can be:

### Weekly research

- add a small number of qualified prospects
- review current Relationship records first
- prefer introductions/warm paths where available
- identify brokerage/account opportunities

### Weekly activation

- limited manually reviewed outreach batch
- replies take priority over new cold sends
- no endless sequence expansion because a quota exists

### Weekly learning

Review:

- source
- reply rate
- meaningful conversation rate
- pressure discovered
- exact language
- consultation/service request
- first paid engagement
- objection
- lost/no-response reason

Do not optimize solely for number of emails sent.

---

# First-50 Relationship Rule

For the first 50 meaningful relationships:

Prioritize:

- clean source capture
- real pressure language
- small controlled outreach batches
- understanding objections
- understanding existing support systems
- first-service preference
- repeat/cross-service behavior

Do not prioritize:

- huge scraped lists
- maximum automation
- vanity lead volume
- bulk cold email scale
- complicated lead scoring models

The first 50 relationships are a learning system.

---

# Research Ethics / Data-Minimization Rule

Only collect information useful for a legitimate professional relationship.

Prefer:

- professional role
- business contact information
- public business context
- service/market fit

Avoid collecting unrelated personal information simply because it is publicly discoverable.

Koinonia's prospecting system should know enough to start a relevant professional conversation—not build a dossier.
