# Koinonia Minimum Product Set — 2026-09-03

Status: Working Recommendation / Owner Review Required  
Owner: Koinonia  
Purpose: Reduce the broad Koinonia capability model into the smallest practical set of named products a Realtor can understand and buy without losing the breadth of the white-glove one-stop model.

This document sits beneath the five public capability umbrellas and above final pricing. It does not replace canonical service objects or pricing rules until approved.

---

# 1. Core Decision

Koinonia should **not** create a separate named product for every service capability.

The five public umbrellas explain **what Koinonia can handle**:

1. Transactions & Contracts
2. Listing & Seller Support
3. Licensed Field Coverage
4. Marketing & Growth
5. CRM & Business Operations

But the actual product shelf should stay much smaller.

## Recommended named product set

1. **Transaction Management**
2. **Hand Us the Listing**
3. **Licensed Field Coverage**
4. **Marketing Management**
5. **Koinonia Partnership**

Everything else should normally be one of four things:

- an included capability inside one of these products;
- a type of field assignment;
- a scoped one-time project/request;
- an approved outside expense required to complete the work.

This creates a company that is broad in capability but simple to buy.

---

# 2. Universal Intake — Not a Sixth Product

Koinonia should support one universal fallback:

> **Need something else? Ask Koinonia.**

This is **not** a sixth product.

It is the intake path for legitimate needs that do not fit neatly into a named product at first glance.

Examples:

- CRM cleanup;
- database migration;
- one-off marketing campaign;
- process/SOP project;
- brochure or presentation project;
- property/vendor coordination request;
- unusual business-support request;
- approved contract/document request after compensation/compliance review.

Koinonia scopes the request and either:

1. handles it as a project;
2. routes it into a named product;
3. treats it as an assignment within Licensed Field Coverage;
4. includes it within an existing client partnership;
5. declines or narrows the portion outside Koinonia's approved scope.

The public website should never make the Realtor feel that a need is invalid merely because it is not a named SKU.

# 3. Product 1 — Transaction Management

## Customer promise

> **Send us the executed deal. Koinonia keeps the contract-to-close work organized, tracked, coordinated, and moving.**

## Why this remains a named product

Transaction Management is already understood by the market, has a natural start/end, has established Koinonia operating history, and is easy to price per file.

## Included capability families

- transaction intake and file setup;
- deadline extraction and tracking;
- document organization/follow-up;
- lender/title/closing coordination;
- inspection/appraisal tracking;
- repair/vendor coordination;
- milestone communication;
- closing preparation;
- post-close file completion.

## Contract/document relationship

Contract/document preparation remains a Koinonia capability, but it does **not** need to remain a separate foundational product card.

After compliance/compensation review, contract/document support can be handled through one or more of these paths:

- included where naturally part of an active Transaction Management file;
- included within Koinonia Partnership according to scope;
- handled as a scoped request when lawfully compensable;
- bundled into listing/transaction work where appropriate.

Do not force the entire product architecture to preserve a separate $75 contract-writing SKU merely because the legacy website had one.

## Pricing architecture

Retain the simple per-file model.

Current approved starting point remains:

- $389 prepaid;
- $599 pay at closing.

Do not add micro-fees for internal transaction tasks.

# 4. Product 2 — Hand Us the Listing

## Customer promise

> **You win the listing. Koinonia manages the operational work around it so you can stay focused on the seller, strategy, and getting it sold.**

## Important simplification decision

Do **not** publicly create three separate overlapping listing products called:

- Listing Launch;
- Full Listing Management;
- Hand Us the Listing.

That recreates choice friction and forces the Realtor to understand Koinonia's internal stages.

Use **Hand Us the Listing** as the primary named listing product.

Internally, Koinonia may still track launch-only, active-listing, or full-lifecycle scopes for operations and pricing logic, but the public sales experience should lead with one recognizable outcome.

## Standard Koinonia scope should be designed around

- seller/listing intake;
- property information and listing-document organization;
- launch planning;
- media/vendor coordination;
- sign/lockbox coordination;
- MLS/setup support within approved permissions;
- listing-live QA;
- standard listing marketing execution;
- open-house coordination where requested;
- showing feedback/seller-update support;
- price/status/listing-change administration from Realtor instruction;
- accepted-offer handoff.

## What happens when it goes under contract

Do not invent a dozen separate add-on charges.

Recommended commercial direction:

### Option A — Preferred working model

**Hand Us the Listing fee covers listing-side operational management through accepted offer.**

If the property goes under contract, the file moves automatically into standard Transaction Management at the client's selected transaction pricing rule.

Why this model is strong:

- the listing fee pays for real work even if the property never sells;
- transaction pricing remains consistent across buyer/seller transactions;
- there is one major price at each natural lifecycle stage, not micro-fees;
- the operational handoff is seamless even though accounting recognizes two major service phases.

The website can explain this simply rather than making the Realtor calculate individual component fees.

### Future alternative to test

A single bundled listing-to-close price may later be tested if unit economics support it.

Do not force one all-inclusive price before Koinonia has enough real usage data to understand listing workload and failure-to-close rates.

## Outside expenses

Property-specific costs remain additional when needed and approved:

- photography;
- drone/video;
- 3D/floor plans;
- staging;
- print/postage;
- paid advertising;
- specialty property vendors;
- repair/contractor services.

Koinonia owns coordination when within scope.

Do not create separate Koinonia coordination charges for every vendor inside the listing product.

# 5. Product 3 — Licensed Field Coverage

## Customer promise

> **When you cannot physically be there, Koinonia helps keep the appointment, property, and client need covered.**

## Why this remains one product

Showings, open houses, inspection access, appraisal access, vendor access, lockbox work, property checks, and similar tasks share the same fundamental customer need:

> “I need a trusted person at the property because I cannot be there.”

Do not make each field task a separate product family.

## Assignment types beneath one product

- buyer showing;
- multi-property tour;
- open-house hosting;
- inspection access;
- appraisal access;
- photographer/stager/vendor access;
- repair-completion access;
- approved property check;
- lockbox/key task;
- sign/property task where permitted;
- vacation/overflow field coverage.

## Pricing architecture

Use **per assignment/event** pricing with the price confirmed before booking.

A standard base or starting price can be public.

Final assignment pricing may vary based on:

- distance;
- notice;
- time/day;
- duration;
- number of properties;
- assignment complexity.

This is one area where some variability is legitimate because the direct fulfillment economics materially change.

Do not publish a complex matrix unless actual customer behavior later proves it is necessary.

## Open-house treatment

Open-house hosting is an assignment type under Licensed Field Coverage, not a sixth foundational product.

It can also be included or preferred-priced within Hand Us the Listing or Koinonia Partnership when the commercial design supports it.

# 6. Product 4 — Marketing Management

## Customer promise

> **Koinonia keeps your real-estate marketing moving without making you manage every post, campaign, vendor, and platform yourself.**

## Why marketing deserves one named product

Marketing is a large enough and distinct enough ongoing business need that it should be directly buyable without forcing the Realtor into a full operations partnership.

But Koinonia should not create separate named products for:

- social media;
- newsletter;
- listing flyers;
- database email;
- review campaigns;
- direct mail;
- paid ads;
- Google Business Profile;
- photography coordination;
- listing marketing.

These are capabilities and campaign types under Marketing Management.

## Two commercial modes beneath one product

### Project / campaign

For a defined one-time need such as:

- listing campaign;
- farm campaign;
- database reactivation;
- website/landing page project;
- brand setup;
- collateral project;
- event/open-house campaign.

Koinonia provides one quoted project fee.

### Recurring management

For Realtors who want ongoing marketing execution.

Rather than immediately publishing three or four complicated tiers, start by defining **one core Marketing Management relationship** with a clear monthly scope and capacity.

If real demand later demonstrates meaningful customer segments, add a second or third level intentionally.

Do not create tiers simply because competitors have tiers.

## Outside expenses

Additional when required and approved:

- ad spend;
- printing/postage;
- premium photography/video;
- drone/3D/floor plans;
- specialty web/software costs;
- specialty production vendors.

Koinonia earns its margin through the visible management/service fee, not hidden markups on these costs by default.

# 7. Product 5 — Koinonia Partnership

## Working customer-facing name

**Koinonia Partnership**

Alternative names can be tested later, including Realtor Management Partnership, but the initial product architecture should not fragment the recurring relationship into multiple overlapping products.

## Customer promise

> **A recurring support relationship built around the parts of your real-estate business you want off your plate.**

## Why this replaces overlapping monthly concepts

The legacy architecture risks creating several recurring products that compete with each other:

- Monthly Operations Partnership;
- Marketing Office;
- Database & Referral Engine;
- Realtor Support Plus;
- Realtor Management Partnership;
- Social Presence;
- CRM management.

That becomes confusing quickly.

The recommendation is to make **Koinonia Partnership** the primary recurring umbrella product.

The partnership can be configured around the client's recurring needs across approved Koinonia capabilities.

## Potential recurring scope

Depending on selected plan/scope:

- CRM and pipeline;
- lead/client follow-up;
- past-client/review/referral systems;
- calendar/tasks/inbox support;
- recurring marketing;
- database campaigns;
- workflow/SOP support;
- listing support allowances;
- contract/document support where lawfully structured;
- transaction-management preferred pricing or allowances;
- field-coverage preferred pricing or allowances;
- vacation/overflow planning;
- vendor coordination;
- periodic operations/marketing review.

## Important pricing principle

The website should sell **support level and outcomes**, not merely hours.

Hours/capacity can remain an internal and contractual boundary.

Do not lead with:

- 3 hours;
- 7 hours;
- 12 hours.

Lead with what the partnership actually changes for the Realtor.

## Tier recommendation

Do not lock three tiers yet.

First define one standard partnership scope and model its economics.

Then determine whether demand and workload genuinely justify:

- two levels; or
- three levels.

The business should not create artificial tiers before understanding real usage.

# 8. What Happens to Database & Referral Engine

**Do not make it a permanent sixth named product at launch.**

It is an excellent **solution/use case**, but most of its work belongs to Marketing Management + CRM & Business Operations.

Present it on the website as a popular solution:

> **Database & Referral Engine** — Clean up the database, segment contacts, create recurring sphere/past-client communication, automate review/referral touchpoints, and keep follow-up moving.

Commercially, it can be:

- a scoped project;
- part of Marketing Management;
- part of Koinonia Partnership.

This gives it sales visibility without product proliferation.

# 9. What Happens to Vacation & Overflow Coverage

**Do not make it a permanent sixth named product.**

This keeps the website simple while still communicating the outcome.

- one clear starting monthly price or limited number of partnership levels — amount(s) to determine

## Custom project

- quoted before work begins

This is enough.

Do not display dozens of prices simply because dozens of tasks exist.

# 14. Product Design Rules Going Forward

A new capability does **not** become a new product unless it passes all of these tests:

1. The customer recognizes it as a distinct buying decision.
2. It has a meaningfully different outcome from existing products.
3. It has a natural scope/start/end or recurring relationship.
4. It cannot be explained cleanly as an included capability, assignment type, project, or solution.
5. A separate product makes buying easier rather than harder.

If those tests fail, keep the capability inside the existing architecture.
