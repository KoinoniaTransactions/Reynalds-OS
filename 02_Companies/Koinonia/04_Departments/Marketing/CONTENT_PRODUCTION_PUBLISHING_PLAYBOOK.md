# Koinonia Transactions — Content Production & Publishing Playbook

Status: Build-Mode Operating Playbook  
Business: Koinonia Transactions  
Owner: Marketing  
Effective Date: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

Define the complete operating workflow for Koinonia social content before any live publishing begins.

This document governs how a content idea becomes:

**Idea -> Brief -> Script/Copy -> Asset -> Review -> Approval -> Scheduled -> Published -> Engagement Captured -> Performance Reviewed -> Learning Applied**

This playbook is a build-stage system definition. It does not authorize publishing or scheduling.

Until marketing activation is explicitly approved:

- do not publish Facebook posts
- do not publish Instagram posts, Reels, or Stories
- do not publish TikTok videos
- do not schedule posts in Meta Business Suite or TikTok tools
- do not turn draft content into live campaigns

---

# Governing Content Strategy

Koinonia's public category is:

**Real Estate Operations Support for Colorado Realtors**

Primary promise:

**You focus on your clients. We'll keep the business running behind you.**

Tagline:

**Real Estate Operations. Elevated.**

Campaign frame:

**More Transactions Should Create More Opportunity, Not More Chaos.**

Koinonia sells dependable real-estate operations support.

The portal, software, workflows, templates, and internal systems are delivery tools—not the product.

Content should lead with:

1. Realtor pressure/problem
2. recognizable operating scenario
3. useful education or relief
4. Koinonia's role when relevant
5. low-pressure next action

Do not lead with:

- software features
- internal portal screens
- generic "we do everything" messaging
- constant pricing
- constant referral messaging

# Weekly Content Jobs

Each week should intentionally include multiple content jobs rather than repeated service advertisements.

## Job 1 — Problem Recognition

Goal:

Help a Realtor recognize an operational pressure they may have normalized.

Examples:

- two clients need showings at the same time
- transaction follow-through keeps returning to the Realtor
- open-house weekends consume client/family time
- CRM follow-up becomes a second job

## Job 2 — Education

Goal:

Teach something useful even if the prospect does not buy today.

Examples:

- what starts after a contract is signed
- how to think about showing backup
- where operational coordination ends and Realtor judgment begins

## Job 3 — Proof / Process

Goal:

Show that Koinonia operates with real structure and professionalism.

Examples:

- behind an open house
- how next actions stay visible
- preparation/workflow snapshots that do not reveal confidential information

## Job 4 — Invitation / Service Use Case

Goal:

Show one clear situation where Koinonia is useful.

Examples:

- "You already know the decision; you need the document workflow organized."
- "You have the client; you cannot be at two showings at once."

Do not require all four jobs every single week when content quality would suffer.

# Content Pipeline Statuses

Use one content record per planned asset.

## Idea

A raw content thought exists.

## Briefed

The purpose, audience, channel, problem, CTA, and format are defined.

## Drafting

Script/copy is being written.

## Draft Ready

Copy/script is complete enough for review.

## Asset Production

Video, carousel, graphic, photo, or Story asset is being created.

## Review

Copy and asset are reviewed together.

## Revision

Changes are required.

## Approved — Not Scheduled

Content is approved for future implementation but must not yet be published.

## Scheduled

Use only after activation. Platform schedule/date/time is confirmed.

## Published

Use only after actual live publication.

## Engagement Review

Comments, DMs, questions, and conversion signals are being assessed.

## Performance Reviewed

Results and learning are recorded.

## Retired / Do Not Use

Content is outdated, inaccurate, redundant, or strategically no longer useful.

Problem Recognition / Education / Proof / Invitation

# Copy / Script Standard

## Every piece should do one primary job

Avoid content that tries to simultaneously explain:

- all five services
- full pricing
- referral program
- brand story
- consultation process

One post = one useful idea.

## Preferred structure

1. Hook
2. Recognizable scenario/problem
3. Useful insight
4. Koinonia fit when relevant
5. Soft CTA

## Tone

- professional
- calm
- useful
- confident
- human
- not pushy
- not fear-based
- not patronizing

## Avoid

- "Hustle harder"
- "You're doing real estate wrong"
- shaming agents for being busy
- exaggerated guarantees
- implying legal advice
- implying brokerage supervision
- implying Koinonia replaces Realtor judgment

# Platform Adaptation Rule

Start from the **idea**, not from a finished Facebook caption.

Then adapt the idea to each platform.

## Facebook adaptation

May include:

- more context
- short paragraphs
- a clear link when relevant
- static/carousel/video

## Instagram adaptation

May include:

- shorter caption
- carousel or Reel
- stronger visual structure
- Story extension
- bio-link CTA where useful

## TikTok adaptation

Must be rewritten natively when necessary.

TikTok should emphasize:

- spoken hook
- pace
- one scenario
- natural delivery
- on-screen captions

Do not simply paste a long Facebook caption into TikTok text.

- dead air removed

# Visual Quality Standard

Koinonia's visual direction should feel:

- light and airy
- clean
- calm
- organized
- approachable
- premium without becoming dark/moody
- warm cream/white
- restrained black/gold
- realistic professional workspaces

For generated or staged marketing imagery:

- keep marketing copy outside the image when used as website hero-style imagery
- do not make every image look like software
- use real-estate/operations cues
- avoid overdesigned stock-photo feel

For social graphics, limited embedded copy is allowed when the graphic itself is the communication format, such as a carousel headline or educational card.

# Video Production Standard

## Recording environment

Prefer:

- clean background
- good natural or soft light
- clear audio
- camera at eye level
- minimal visual clutter

Do not delay useful content indefinitely for studio-level production.

## Talking-head structure

Recommended:

- hook in first sentence
- scenario
- one explanation
- solution/insight
- CTA

## B-roll

Use when it strengthens credibility:

- preparing an open house
- organized checklists
- reviewing non-confidential documents
- professional workspace
- signage/material setup

Never expose:

- private client data
- contracts with readable personal information
- lockbox/access details
- confidential transaction communications

# Approval Rule

During build mode:

Content may reach:

**Approved — Not Scheduled**

It may not move to Scheduled or Published.

Once implementation is activated, content can move from Approved to Scheduled only when:

1. platform access is verified
2. profile setup is correct
3. destination links are live
4. content is still current
5. publishing date fits the active campaign
6. user-approved activation is in effect

# Scheduling Philosophy — Future Implementation

Do not optimize for posting volume at the expense of quality.

Initial working cadence:

- Facebook: 3 primary posts/week
- Instagram: 3 primary feed/Reel posts/week
- Instagram Stories: when useful
- TikTok: 3–5 videos/week during initial learning phase

The calendar is a framework, not a reason to publish weak content.

If a planned post is not ready, delay it rather than publish something inaccurate or off-brand.

# Engagement Response Workflow

A comment or DM should be classified before responding aggressively.

## Type A — General positive engagement

Examples:

- "This is helpful"
- emoji/reaction
- general agreement

Response:

Thank/acknowledge naturally.

Do not immediately pitch.

## Type B — Question about service

Examples:

- "Do you cover Parker?"
- "How does showing coverage work?"
- "What does an open house cost?"

Response:

Answer the actual question clearly.

Then offer the next useful step.

## Type C — Prospect describes pressure

Example:

"I keep having two buyer clients needing tours at the same time."

Response:

Acknowledge the specific situation and ask one diagnostic question.

This becomes a meaningful Relationship signal.

## Type D — Buying intent

Examples:

- "Can you help Saturday?"
- "Can we talk?"
- "How do I hire you?"

Response:

Move into the Lead Response & Consultation workflow.

## Type E — Referral-path question

Clarify whether the Realtor wants to:

- keep the client and needs capacity support, or
- intentionally refer the client

Never blur those paths.

## Type F — Complaint / concern

Do not argue publicly.

Respond professionally, clarify what can be clarified, and move sensitive details to an appropriate private channel.

# Engagement Capture Rule

Do not create a CRM Relationship record for every like or generic follower.

Create/update a Relationship when there is meaningful business context such as:

- DM
- real service question
- described pressure
- consultation intent
- professional introduction
- ongoing meaningful interaction

Capture:

- source platform
- source content ID
- exact language
- pressure category if known
- next action
- next-action date

# Content Performance Hierarchy

Do not treat reach as the goal.

## Primary success signals

- meaningful Realtor conversations
- consultation requests
- service inquiries
- qualified DMs
- paid engagements
- repeat/cross-service behavior later

## Secondary learning signals

- profile visits
- website clicks
- saves
- shares
- comments with real questions
- Story responses

## Diagnostic signals

- impressions
- views
- average watch time
- completion rate
- likes
- follower growth

Diagnostic metrics matter, but they do not outrank business outcomes.

- questions/objections

- CTA performance

## Monthly

Review:

- content to repeat/remake
- content to retire

Do not add new pillars simply because one post underperformed.

---

# Repeat / Remake Rule

A strong content idea should be reused intelligently.

Repeat when:

- the same pressure keeps appearing in conversations
- a post generated qualified interaction
- the concept remains strategically important

Remake by changing:

- hook
- scenario
- format
- platform treatment

Do not merely repost the identical asset repeatedly without purpose.

---

# Content Learning Loop

Use:

**Publish -> Listen -> Capture -> Diagnose -> Learn -> Improve -> Republish**

Examples:

If several Realtors respond to showing-conflict content:

- preserve their exact language
- compare what part of showing coverage matters
- create the next educational piece from that language
- improve service messaging if necessary

If a high-view video creates no meaningful Realtor response:

- do not automatically promote it with paid spend
- determine whether the audience/hook was too broad
