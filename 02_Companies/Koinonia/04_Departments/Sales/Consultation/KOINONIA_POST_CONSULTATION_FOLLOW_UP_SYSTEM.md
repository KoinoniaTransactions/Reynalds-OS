# Koinonia Post-Consultation Follow-Up System

Status: Active Supporting System  
Date: 2026-08-14  
Governing Source: `BRAIN/KOINONIA_CONSULTATION_INTERVIEW_SYSTEM.md`

## Purpose

Define what Koinonia sends after a consultation, when it is sent, what the message is meant to accomplish, and when follow-up should stop.

Every follow-up should do one of three things:

1. Activate an agreed engagement.
2. Advance a specific pending decision.
3. Respectfully preserve a future relationship when now is not the right time.

Do not send generic "just checking in" messages.

Every follow-up should be tied to something the Realtor actually said, agreed to, or requested.

---

# 1. First Engagement Accepted

## Objective

Convert the verbal yes into operational clarity.

Confirm:

- diagnosed starting problem;
- selected service;
- verified price/billing structure;
- what Koinonia will handle;
- what remains with the Realtor;
- what Koinonia needs next;
- what happens after those items are received.

## Broker-Sharing Content

When a broker needs review, prioritize:

- service scope;
- licensed activity;
- professional boundaries;
- what remains with the Realtor/brokerage;
- workflow/communication expectations;
- pricing/billing if relevant.

Do not imply Koinonia can override brokerage policy.

# 10. Clear Decline

Subject: Thank you

Hi [First Name],

Thank you for taking the time to talk through your business with me.

I appreciate the clarity around your decision not to move forward with **[service]**.

I don't want to turn a clear no into a string of sales follow-ups, so I'll leave the conversation there.

If your operating needs change later and Koinonia becomes relevant, you're always welcome to reach back out.

I appreciate the conversation.

Jeremiah

No sales chase after a clear decline unless the prospect explicitly invites future contact.

---

# 11. No Fit — Koinonia Declines

Subject: Follow-up from our conversation

Hi [First Name],

Thank you for walking me through what you're looking for.

After considering the scope, I don't think Koinonia is the right fit for **[REQUEST / NEED]**.

The primary issue is **[brief neutral explanation]**.

Koinonia is designed around defined real estate operations support with clear professional boundaries. I would rather be transparent than accept work that we cannot responsibly support the way you need it handled.

I appreciate the conversation and wish you the best as you find the right solution.

Jeremiah

Keep the decision about fit and scope, not about rejecting the person.

# 13. Consultation No-Show

First message:

Hi [Name] — we had a consultation scheduled for [time] today. I wanted to make sure I didn't miss you or misunderstand the schedule. If you still want to talk through Koinonia support, send me a good time and we can reschedule.

If there is no reply, one additional closure message is enough:

Hi [Name] — closing the loop on our missed consultation. If operational support becomes relevant later, you're welcome to reach back out and we can find a new time.

Then stop.

# 14. Reschedule

Hi [Name] — no problem. Let's move the consultation rather than rush it. I have you rescheduled for **[date/time]**. We'll use the conversation to understand how you're operating and determine whether there's actually a useful place for Koinonia to help.

# 15. After the First Successful Engagement

Do not immediately upsell.

Ask about the working relationship first:

Hi [Name],

Now that we've completed the first **[transaction/document/showing/etc.]** together, I wanted to check in on the working relationship itself.

From your perspective:

- Did communication feel clear?
- Did you know what was happening?
- Did the support actually remove the pressure we discussed?
- Is there anything you would want handled differently next time?

I'd rather make sure the first engagement worked the way it should before talking about anything broader.

Jeremiah

---

# 16. Earned Expansion

Only expand after delivery evidence exists.

Use:

"When we first met, we identified [original problem], and it seems like we've gotten some traction there. You also mentioned [secondary pressure]. Is that still something worth solving, or is the current level of support enough?"

Do not ask prospects to hear about every other service merely because the first engagement succeeded.

---

# 17. Follow-Up Cadence

## Accepted

Immediate/same-day onboarding confirmation, then current service workflow governs communication.

## Think About It

Same-day recap → agreed decision date → one reasonable second attempt if needed → then exit active-decision status.

## Broker / Approval

Same-day decision materials → agreed date → one additional attempt if needed.

## Not Now

Follow only according to the agreed date or trigger.

## Clear No

No sales chase.

## No Fit

No sales chase.

---

# 18. Text vs. Email

Use email for:

- scope;
- pricing confirmation;
- proposals;
- onboarding instructions;
- professional boundaries;
- broker-sharing materials;
- detailed recaps.

Use text for:

- appointment confirmations;
- short decision follow-up;
- notifying that requested information was sent;
- quick scheduling;
- simple operational coordination.

Do not bury important scope/pricing agreements inside casual text threads when documented communication is appropriate.

---

# 19. Subject Line Standard

Prefer useful subjects such as:

- Koinonia next steps — [Service]
- Recap from today
- Transaction Support — [Property]
- Koinonia scope for brokerage review
- Monthly Operations — first month
- Our follow-up for [date]
- Koinonia custom scope — next steps

Avoid hype, false urgency, clickbait, or exaggerated marketing language.

---

# 20. Follow-Up Formula

Context → Diagnosis → Agreed next step → Responsibility → Timing.

Example:

"When we spoke Tuesday, you said contract drafting is repeatedly pulling you out of client appointments. We recommended starting with Contract & Document Support. You were going to check with your broker on the workflow, and I sent the scope summary. We agreed I'd reconnect Friday. Were you able to get the clarification you needed?"

---

# 21. Never Send

Avoid:

- "Just checking in."
- "Bumping this to the top of your inbox."
- "Haven't heard from you."
- "Are you still interested?"
- "Last chance."
- fake scarcity;
- manufactured deadlines;
- guilt;
- repeated automated nudges pretending to be personal.

The standard is:

**Specific. Relevant. Expected. Useful.**
