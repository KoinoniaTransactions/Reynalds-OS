# Koinonia Consultation Role-Play & Certification System

Status: Active Supporting Training System  
Date: 2026-08-14  
Governing Source: `BRAIN/KOINONIA_CONSULTATION_INTERVIEW_SYSTEM.md`

## Purpose

Train and evaluate whether a Koinonia team member can run the consultation system naturally, accurately, and within approved professional boundaries.

The objective is not memorization.

A consultation-ready person can consistently:

**Diagnose accurately → reflect the problem → prescribe one appropriate service → explain boundaries → present price calmly → ask directly for the first engagement → leave with a clear outcome.**

A perfect recitation with a bad diagnosis fails.

A natural conversation that follows the operating logic succeeds.

---

# Consultation Evaluation Scorecard

Score each category 0–2:

- 0 = missed / harmful
- 1 = partially effective
- 2 = strong / consistent

Categories:

1. Opening
2. Discovery
3. Listening
4. Consequence
5. Existing support
6. Reflection
7. Koinonia reveal
8. Prescription
9. Boundaries
10. Price
11. Objection handling
12. Close
13. Follow-up
14. Fit judgment

Maximum: 28.

Suggested standard:

- 24–28: Consultation-ready
- 19–23: Good foundation; targeted coaching needed
- 14–18: Process inconsistencies
- Below 14: Retrain before live consultations

## Automatic Fail

Serious misrepresentation of scope, licensing, pricing, brokerage authority, professional responsibilities, or current client-facing capabilities is an automatic fail regardless of numerical score.

# Scenario 1 — Overloaded Solo Realtor

Profile:

Amanda, 7 years in real estate, approximately four closings per month, six active files, no assistant.

Opening statement:

"I mostly wanted to see what you guys do. I've been getting really busy lately."

Hidden reality:

Amanda personally manages lender/title follow-up, transaction deadlines, client communication, inspection scheduling, document organization, and late-night file cleanup. Every transaction creates another mental checklist. Prospecting has been inconsistent for six weeks.

Target diagnosis:

Transaction overload.

Key consequence:

Active files are consuming attention that would otherwise go to prospecting/business development.

Trust requirement:

Proactive communication.

Trap:

She asks early, "So are you basically a TC?"

A weak response launches into services. A strong response gives a category-level answer and returns to diagnosis.

Likely prescription:

Transaction Support.

Likely close:

Use an active under-contract file as the first engagement when appropriate.

# Scenario 2 — High Producer With Existing TC

Profile:

Marcus, 12 years in real estate, eight to ten transactions per month, happy with current TC.

Opening statement:

"I'll save you some time. I already have a TC and I'm happy with her."

Hidden reality:

The current TC handles contract-to-close well. Marcus personally writes offers, counters, amendments, and notices, which interrupt appointments and evenings.

Target diagnosis:

Existing-support gap focused on contract/document preparation.

Critical behavior:

Do not attack or attempt to displace the current TC.

Strong diagnostic move:

"That's good. If that relationship is working, I'm not interested in disrupting it. What still stays on your plate even with that support?"

Likely prescription:

Contract & Document Support.

# Scenario 3 — Price-Sensitive Newer Agent

Profile:

Taylor, 18 months licensed, approximately one closing every month or two, cash-conscious.

Opening statement:

"I saw the services online. Honestly, I mostly want to know how cheap I can get transaction coordination."

Hidden reality:

Current transaction volume is manageable. The immediate pain is writing offers while moving between showings and buyers.

Target diagnosis:

Contract & Document Support, not Transaction Support.

Objection:

"$75? I can probably just write it myself."

Strong response principle:

Agree they can do it; reconnect to the diagnosed interruption rather than inventing ROI.

A valid outcome may be Not Now.

This scenario tests whether Koinonia avoids forcing a sale.

# Scenario 4 — Busy Team Lead With Several Problems

Profile:

Rachel, four-agent team, meaningful production.

Reported problems include CRM cleanup, agent follow-up, transaction issues, drafting, calendar problems, training gaps, lead routing, reporting, marketing tasks, and listing coordination.

Trap:

Selling everything or immediately recommending the largest monthly tier.

Required move:

Prioritize the first domino.

Useful question:

"There are several real opportunities there. Which one creates the biggest recurring problem when it isn't handled consistently?"

Hidden primary problem:

Pipeline and follow-up collapse during active transaction periods because nobody owns recurring operations.

Target diagnosis:

Recurring business operations overload.

Likely prescription:

Monthly Operations Partnership at the level supported by diagnosed scope, not merely by team size.

# Scenario 5 — Employing Broker

Profile:

David, employing broker. Two agents have mentioned Koinonia.

Opening question:

"What exactly are you doing for my agents, and who's responsible if something gets missed?"

This is not a normal individual-Realtor sales consultation and should not be forced mechanically through the standard discovery sequence.

Broker priorities:

- scope;
- licensing;
- professional responsibilities;
- brokerage policy;
- communication;
- risk;
- consistency.

Strong response:

"Those are exactly the right questions. Koinonia provides defined operational support, but the agent and brokerage retain the professional responsibilities that belong with them. I'd like to understand how your office currently handles outside support first, then I can show you exactly where our role starts and stops."

Possible successful outcome:

Brokerage understanding/approval and a path for interested agents to engage appropriately.

# Scenario 6 — Growth Before Pain

Profile:

Nicole, three years in business, organized, manageable current volume, planning significant growth.

Opening statement:

"I'm actually not overwhelmed right now. I'm trying to make sure I don't become overwhelmed."

Bad behavior:

Trying to prove she is secretly overwhelmed.

Strong response:

"Then I don't want to invent a problem you don't have. Let's look at where the current model depends heavily on you and what would happen if volume increased."

Target diagnosis:

Growth-before-pain capacity planning.

Possible outcome:

No immediate purchase, but a defined future trigger for the first transaction/document engagement.

# Scenario 7 — Boundary-Breaker / No-Fit Prospect

Profile:

Chris, experienced high producer.

Statements:

- "Once I send it to you, I don't want to think about it again."
- "If something needs changing in the contract, just fix it. I don't need you asking me every time."
- "My broker doesn't need to know every little thing we're doing."
- "I expect you to answer whenever I call, nights and weekends included."

Target diagnosis:

Relationship and professional-boundary problem.

Required behavior:

Clearly restate Koinonia boundaries and be willing to decline the relationship.

Strong response:

"We can take defined operational responsibility, but we don't take over your professional judgment, make negotiation decisions for you, or work around brokerage requirements. We also define communication and availability expectations rather than promising unlimited access."

Correct outcome if rejected:

No Fit.

# Scenario 8 — Send Me Something

Profile:

Lauren, productive solo Realtor, agrees with diagnosis and likes the proposed service.

Closing response:

"This sounds interesting. Send me something and I'll look at it."

Weak response:

Agreeing to send generic collateral and ending the meeting.

Strong response:

"Absolutely. What would be most helpful for making the decision — the scope, pricing, professional boundaries, or are you sharing it with someone else?"

Then set a specific follow-up date.

This tests next-step discipline.

---

# Scenario 9 — Comparison Shopper

Profile:

Brandon, interviewing multiple transaction coordinators.

Opening:

"The other company charges less. Why should I pay you more?"

Strong diagnostic move:

"Before I try to answer that, what are you actually looking for the person or company to own? If the scope is genuinely identical, price is a reasonable comparison."

Hidden reality:

He wants broader contract and recurring operations support, while the cheaper provider only handles post-contract files.

Training objective:

Compare actual scope without insulting or inventing weaknesses in competitors.

---

# Scenario 10 — Extremely Talkative Prospect

Profile:

Jennifer. Every answer becomes a long story containing useful information and tangents.

Training challenge:

Maintain rapport while controlling the structure.

Useful language:

- "That's helpful. Let me pull one thing out of what you just said..."
- "I want to make sure I don't lose the part about [issue]. When that happens, what usually gets pushed aside?"

This tests facilitation and meeting control without becoming rude or mechanical.

# Rapid Objection Drill

Train the first move, not clever comebacks.

- "That's expensive." → Diagnose amount vs. value.
- "Someone else is cheaper." → Compare actual scope.
- "I already have a TC." → Find remaining gap.
- "I can do it myself." → Agree; explore consequence.
- "I need to think." → Find unresolved issue.
- "Send me something." → Find what they need to decide.
- "Talk to my broker." → Identify broker's needed information.
- "Can you discount it?" → Check scope before changing economics.
- "Can you do everything for me?" → Define boundaries.
- "Can I just use you when needed?" → Match structure to need.
- "I'll call you when I'm ready." → Respect it; clarify whether follow-up is invited.
- "Why can't you just decide that for me?" → Re-establish professional responsibility.

The goal is calm diagnosis, not verbal sparring.

---

# Coaching Red Flags

Stop and correct immediately when the trainee:

- pitches before diagnosing;
- answers the first symptom with a service;
- talks more than the prospect through discovery;
- skips the consequence question;
- attacks existing support;
- uses the Koinonia meaning before it is relevant;
- feature-dumps;
- presents all four services equally;
- leads with price;
- defends price before understanding the objection;
- discounts reflexively;
- keeps selling after yes;
- ends with "let me know";
- fails to say no to clearly inappropriate scope;
- promises unfinished technology or unsupported capabilities.

# Training Progression

## Round 1 — Structure

Trainee may use the full field guide.

Goal: follow the seven stages correctly.

## Round 2 — Natural Conversation

Use only the compact field guide.

Goal: stop sounding scripted while preserving the logic.

## Round 3 — Pressure Test

No script.

The prospect interrupts, objects, changes priorities, mentions competitors, existing support, broker concerns, and budget.

Goal: maintain the method without losing the human conversation.

Only after Round 3 should someone be considered consultation-ready.

---

# Certification Test

Use three unknown prospects:

1. one strong fit;
2. one complicated but legitimate fit;
3. one prospect Koinonia should decline.

The trainee should not know which is which in advance.

Certification tests the ability to:

- know when to prescribe;
- know when to wait;
- know when to walk away.

The training system must not teach that every consultation is supposed to end in a sale.
