# Koinonia Consultation Presentation System

Status: Active Supporting System  
Date: 2026-08-14  
Governing Source: `BRAIN/KOINONIA_CONSULTATION_INTERVIEW_SYSTEM.md`

## Purpose

Define what Koinonia should visually show a Realtor during the consultation, when each page should appear, and what should deliberately remain hidden until later.

The presentation supports the conversation. It does not lead it.

Core rule:

**Talk first → diagnose → confirm the problem → reveal Koinonia → show only the relevant support → show investment → agree on the next step.**

The presentation should be modular. Not every page must be shown in every consultation.

---

# Page 1 — Cover

Show briefly at the beginning or while everyone settles in.

Recommended content:

**Koinonia Transactions**

**Real Estate Operations. Elevated.**

**You focus on your clients. We'll keep the business running.**

Do not show a service menu or price table on the cover.

Recommended transition:

"Before I walk you through anything on here, I'd rather understand how you're operating first."

Then move away from the presentation.

---

# Discovery — No Slides

During Stages 1–3, keep the prospect focused on the conversation rather than marketing collateral.

Koinonia may use a private field guide or scorecard, but the prospect should not be reading a deck during diagnostic discovery.

The presentation resumes only after the diagnosis is confirmed.

---

# Page 2 — The Two Jobs of a Realtor

Headline:

**A Realtor is running two businesses at the same time.**

Suggested visual structure:

## What Clients See

- Relationships
- Advice
- Negotiations
- Showings
- Strategy
- Decisions
- Client experience

## What Keeps the Business Moving

- Deadlines
- Documents
- Coordination
- Follow-up
- Scheduling
- CRM & tasks
- Operational details

Bottom statement:

**The second job doesn't disappear when the first one gets busy.**

Use this only after the prospect has confirmed the diagnosis.

Personalize immediately:

"And in your case, the pressure is showing up specifically around [their diagnosed problem]."

Do not over-explain the diagram.

---

# Page 3 — What Koinonia Is

Headline:

**Real estate operations support behind the client relationship.**

Center statement:

**You focus on your clients. We'll keep the business running.**

Supporting copy:

Koinonia provides organized, real-estate-aware operational support so Realtors can protect client service while the work behind the business stays visible and moving.

Boundary line:

**Your relationship. Your judgment. Your business. Koinonia supports the operation behind it.**

The slide should explain the operating-partner position, not list every feature.

---

# Page 4 — Why Koinonia

Headline:

**Koinonia**  
*koy-noh-NEE-uh*

Meaning:

**Fellowship. Joint participation. Sharing in common.**

Supporting statement:

**Support should feel like partnership — not another disconnected vendor.**

Use this page for approximately 30–45 seconds.

Do not turn the name explanation into a long theology or founder-story segment.

---

# Page 5 — The Four Support Paths

The recommended service should visually dominate. The other three should remain available as context but secondary.

Service paths:

- Transaction Support
- Contract & Document Support
- Licensed Showing Coverage
- Monthly Operations Partnership

Bottom statement:

**Start with the pressure that matters most. Expand only when it makes sense.**

Recommended language:

"Koinonia has four support paths, but I'm not recommending you buy four services. Based on what you told me, I would start here."

Do not make the prospect self-diagnose from a menu.

---

# Page 6 — Prescribed Service Module

Build four interchangeable versions. Show only the relevant module.

## Transaction Support

Headline:

**When another transaction should not mean another entire operating load.**

Koinonia supports:

- Contract-to-close timeline
- Deadline and document tracking
- Communication coordination
- File organization
- Closing preparation

Realtor retains:

- Client relationship
- Professional advice
- Negotiations
- Brokerage responsibilities
- Final professional decisions

Intended operating effect:

**The file keeps moving while you stay focused on clients and growth.**

## Contract & Document Support

Headline:

**When document work hits at exactly the wrong time.**

Koinonia supports:

- Draft preparation from Realtor instructions
- Required-term organization
- Missing-information review
- Revisions and version control
- Clean draft for Realtor approval

Realtor retains:

- Business terms
- Negotiation decisions
- Client advice
- Brokerage compliance
- Final approval

Intended operating effect:

**Document preparation stops competing with the work that requires the Realtor personally.**

## Licensed Showing Coverage

Headline:

**Your calendar should not determine whether the client gets served.**

Koinonia supports:

- Licensed showing coverage
- Access coordination
- Appointment confirmation
- Client instruction review
- Completion and feedback

Realtor retains:

- Client relationship
- Advice
- Negotiation
- Representation decisions
- Ongoing client strategy

Intended operating effect:

**A scheduling conflict does not have to become a client-service problem.**

# Page 7 — How Working Together Works

Use a four-step visual:

## Clarify

Confirm the need, scope, responsibilities, timing, and required information.

## Coordinate

Koinonia takes ownership of the defined operational work.

## Keep Visible

Communication, next actions, and outstanding items remain organized and clear.

## Complete

The engagement is finished, reviewed, and the next appropriate action is identified.

Bottom statement:

**Defined scope. Clear communication. Dependable follow-through.**

When possible, tie this page to the prospect's trust requirement.

Example:

"You told me earlier that proactive communication matters to you. This is how we structure the relationship around that."

---

# Page 8 — Investment

Do not default to a complete price catalog.

Show only the recommended current verified engagement.

Suggested layout:

## Your Recommended Starting Point

**[Service]**

[brief scope]

### Investment

**[CURRENT VERIFIED PRICE]**

**[CURRENT VERIFIED BILLING STRUCTURE]**

### Recommended First Engagement

[Active property / next transaction / first document / first month / showing]

Recommended verbal line:

"For what we just discussed, this is the current structure."

Then pause.

The price displayed must be verified against current controlling pricing sources before use.

# Page 9 — First Engagement

Headline:

**Start with one real operating problem.**

Personalized sections:

## What We Identified

[their problem]

## Where I Recommend Starting

[service]

## First Engagement

[actual file / document / showing / monthly priorities]

## What Success Should Look Like

[operational effect based on desired outcome]

Bottom statement:

**Solve one thing well. Earn the right to expand.**

Then ask:

"Based on everything we covered, I would start with [service] for [need]. Are you comfortable moving forward with that as the first engagement?"

The consultation presentation should end on the decision, not a generic Thank You slide.

# Realtor Leave-Behind

The presentation deck and leave-behind should not be identical.

Recommended leave-behind: polished two-sided one-sheet.

## Front

- Koinonia Transactions
- Real Estate Operations. Elevated.
- You focus on your clients. We'll keep the business running.
- Short real-estate-operations positioning paragraph
- Four compact service summaries
- Professional support without giving up the parts of the business that belong with the Realtor

## Back

### How Koinonia Works

Clarify → Coordinate → Keep Visible → Complete

### Koinonia Supports

Operational execution, organization, coordination, licensed document preparation within scope, and licensed showing support.

### Realtor Retains

Client advice, negotiations, brokerage compliance, professional judgment, legal questions, and final approvals.

### Why Koinonia

- Licensed perspective
- Organized execution
- Clear communication
- Defined boundaries

Bottom CTA:

**Schedule a Consultation**

Use current verified contact information and current approved QR destination.

# Separate Pricing Card

Keep pricing collateral separate from the main leave-behind because pricing can change more frequently than positioning.

The pricing card may contain current approved service starting points and monthly tiers.

Include a freshness indicator such as:

**Pricing current as of [date]. Scope confirmed before work begins.**

Pricing card content must be updated whenever controlling pricing changes.

---

# Separate Brokerage Scope Sheet

Brokerage collateral should prioritize evaluation rather than sales language.

It should clearly state:

- what Koinonia does;
- what Koinonia does not do;
- licensed/operating role;
- Realtor responsibilities;
- brokerage responsibilities;
- communication structure;
- available services;
- current scope/billing information when relevant.

Use this when a Realtor needs brokerage review or when building a direct brokerage relationship.

---

# What Must Not Appear as a Current Client Promise

Do not present:

- unfinished client portal functionality;
- development roadmap items;
- internal Reynalds OS architecture;
- internal playbooks;
- internal health scores;
- internal automation systems;
- speculative future services;
- outdated pricing;
- unverified competitor comparisons;
- every internal package variation;
- unnecessary founder credentials;
- internal operating machinery that does not help the prospect decide.

The prospect does not need to see the machinery to believe Koinonia is organized. They need to experience the organization.

---

# Recommended In-Person Consultation Kit

A simple professional kit may include:

- tablet/laptop with modular consultation visuals;
- printed two-sided leave-behind;
- current pricing card;
- brokerage scope sheet;
- private consultation scorecard.

Avoid oversized binders, unnecessary service catalogs, premature swag, or collateral volume that makes the meeting feel like a conventional pitch.

The desired experience is calm, prepared, organized, and premium.
