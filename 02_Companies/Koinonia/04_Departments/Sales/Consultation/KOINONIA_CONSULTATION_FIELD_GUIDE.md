# Koinonia Consultation Field Guide

Status: Active Supporting Guide  
Date: 2026-08-14  
Governing Source: `BRAIN/KOINONIA_CONSULTATION_INTERVIEW_SYSTEM.md`

## Purpose

A compact live-use guide for a 20–30 minute Koinonia consultation.

This guide compresses the canonical consultation system. It does not replace it.

## Core Sequence

Diagnose → Reflect → Reveal → Prescribe → Investment → Decision.

For roughly the first third of the conversation, the Realtor should do most of the talking.

---

# 1. Open — ~2 minutes

Use:

"Thanks for taking the time to meet with me. Before I tell you much about Koinonia, I'd rather understand how you're running your business today. Every Realtor operates a little differently, and I don't want to assume I know where support would actually help.

I'll ask you a few questions about your business, what's working, and where things start getting heavy. Then, if I think Koinonia can genuinely help, I'll show you where I think it fits. Sound fair?"

If asked what Koinonia does immediately:

"At the highest level, we provide real estate operations support for Realtors. I'll absolutely walk you through it. I just want to understand your business first so I don't give you a generic presentation."

Do not lead with the full services menu, pricing, portal/technology, or a long company story.

---

# 2. Diagnose — ~7–10 minutes

Ask naturally. Do not interrogate from a checklist.

## Business

"Tell me a little about your business right now. What does a normal month look like for you?"

## Current Operating Load

"When business gets moving, what are you still personally handling behind the scenes?"

## Primary Pressure

"Of everything you're carrying, what tends to consume the most time or mental energy?"

## Consequence

"When two or three things hit at the same time, what usually starts slipping, getting delayed, or getting pushed into evenings and weekends?"

Then:

"What does that cost you — time with clients, prospecting, responsiveness, personal time, or just mental bandwidth?"

## Existing Support

"What support do you have today — a TC, assistant, team resources, brokerage support, vendors — and what still stays with you?"

## Desired Outcome & Trust

"If you could take one recurring category of work off your plate without losing visibility or control, what would you want it to be?"

"If that gave you meaningful time back, where would you put it?"

"What would a support partner have to do consistently for you to trust them with part of your business?"

## Quietly Capture

Current model | current support | personally owned work | primary pressure | consequence | busy-period failure point | desired capacity | trust requirement.

Before moving on, you should be able to answer:

1. How are they operating today?
2. What is costing them the most?
3. What would they rather be doing?

---

# 3. Reflect — ~2–3 minutes

Use their language.

"So from what you've told me, you're still personally carrying [A], [B], and [C], while also trying to stay focused on [clients / prospecting / growth]."

"And when business gets busy, that turns into [their consequence], which is pulling you away from [desired outcome]."

Then:

"So it sounds like the issue isn't that you don't know how to do the work. The issue is that too much of the operation still depends on you personally. Is that fair?"

If they disagree:

"What part did I get wrong?"

Do not defend the diagnosis. Correct it.

When confirmed:

"That's exactly the kind of problem Koinonia was built around."

---

# 4. Reveal Koinonia — ~3–4 minutes

"One of the things I've seen in real estate is that Realtors are expected to do two jobs at once. You have the part the client sees — the relationship, advice, negotiations, showings, decisions — and then there's an entire operating business running behind that relationship.

The problem is that the second job doesn't disappear when the first one gets busy."

Connect directly:

"And for you, that's showing up in [their problem]."

Then:

"Koinonia exists to provide dependable real estate operations support behind the client relationship.

The simplest way I describe it is: you focus on your clients; we'll keep the business running behind you.

You still own your client relationship, professional judgment, negotiations, and the decisions that belong to you and your brokerage. We create organized support around the operational work that shouldn't have to depend on you personally every time."

Name explanation:

"Koinonia — koy-noh-NEE-uh — carries the idea of fellowship, joint participation, and sharing in common. That's intentional. The model is partnership: dependable support working alongside your business."

Keep the explanation practical and short.

---

# 5. Prescribe — ~4–6 minutes

Use the smallest sufficient first engagement.

## Service Selection

- Contract-to-close load, deadlines, coordination → Transaction Support.
- Offers, amendments, drafting, documents → Contract & Document Support.
- Schedule overlap, distance, buyer access → Licensed Showing Coverage.
- CRM, follow-up, tasks, recurring business operations → Monthly Operations Partnership.

## Prescription Formula

"Because you told me [problem] is causing [consequence], I would start with [service] so we can [specific outcome]."

## Transaction Support

"Once you're under contract, Koinonia can take the operational lead on the contract-to-close process — timeline, deadlines, documents, follow-up, coordination, and closing preparation. You remain the Realtor and retain the client relationship and professional decisions.

The goal is that the transaction doesn't stop moving just because your attention has to go somewhere else."

## Contract & Document Support

"You give us the terms and direction, and Koinonia handles the organized preparation side — the draft, required information, missing-item review, revisions, and a clean version back to you for approval.

You still choose the terms and make the professional decisions."

## Licensed Showing Coverage

"When your calendar overlaps or distance creates a problem, Koinonia can provide defined licensed showing coverage, follow your instructions, complete the showing professionally, and get the feedback back to you.

You remain the client's Realtor."

## Monthly Operations Partnership

"Because what you described keeps recurring, I'd build a defined operating rhythm around it — CRM, follow-up, tasks, checklists, workflows, and the priorities we agree need to stay moving.

It's structured recurring support, not unlimited assistant access."

Then ask:

"Does that sound like it addresses the part you were describing?"

If yes:

"Good. Then let me show you how we structure that engagement and what the investment looks like."

---

# 6. Investment — ~2–4 minutes

Before a real consultation, verify current pricing from controlling Koinonia pricing/package sources and the production site where applicable.

Do not treat numbers copied into this guide as authority over the current pricing system.

Current reference at publication:

- Transaction Support — $389 prepaid.
- Transaction pay-at-closing — $599 after successful closing.
- Contract & Document Support — starting at $75.
- Licensed Showing Coverage — starting at $50.
- Monthly Operations — Starter $299 / Growth $599 / Partner $999.

State the verified price and scope, then stop talking.

Do not say "only $___". Do not apologize. Do not immediately discount.

If they say it is expensive:

"Is the concern the amount itself, or whether the scope is worth that amount?"

If another provider is cheaper:

"They very well may be. I'd compare whether the scope, communication, boundaries, and operational ownership are actually the same."

If asked for a discount:

"I don't want to make up a different price in the room just to get a yes. What I can do is make sure we're not asking you to buy more scope than you actually need."

Prefer legitimate scope reduction before price erosion.

---

# 7. Ask — ~2–3 minutes

Use:

"Based on everything we covered, I would start with [service] for [specific need]. Are you comfortable moving forward with that as the first engagement?"

Then be quiet.

## Yes

"Great. Let's make the next step concrete."

Confirm the first engagement, billing structure, required information, timing, approvals, and next action.

Stop selling after yes.

## Think About It

"Absolutely. What part do you still need to think through — the fit, trust, investment, timing, or something else?"

Resolve a real question once, then:

"When would it make sense for us to reconnect?"

Get a real date or defined window.

## Send Me Something

"Absolutely. What would be most useful for the decision — the scope, pricing, professional boundaries, or something you need to share with someone else?"

Send only what is useful and set a follow-up date.

## Broker / Team Approval

"What will they need to understand or approve before you can move forward?"

Provide the relevant scope/boundary/pricing information and agree on the next follow-up.

## Not Now

"What would have to change for this to become worth revisiting?"

Identify the trigger and whether follow-up is invited.

## No

"Understood. Is that a no to this particular starting point, a no for now, or does outside operational support simply not fit how you want to run the business?"

Use the answer for learning, not to trap the prospect back into a sale.

# Final 20-Second Recap

If moving forward:

"Just so we're aligned: the issue we're starting with is [problem]. We're starting with [service] at the structure we discussed. Koinonia will handle [scope], you retain [professional responsibility], and our next step is [action]."

If pending:

"The issue we identified is [problem], my recommendation is [service], you're going to [action], I'm going to [action], and we'll reconnect [date/trigger]."

---

# Five Things to Remember if You Lose Your Place

Ask. Listen. Reflect. Prescribe. Ask for the work.

The operating sequence in plain language is:

What is happening? → Why does it matter? → Did I understand you? → Here is where Koinonia fits. → Here is where I would start. → Here is what it costs. → Do you want to start?
