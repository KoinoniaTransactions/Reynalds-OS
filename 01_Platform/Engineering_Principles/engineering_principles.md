# Engineering Principles

- Engineering Principle #1: The system should reduce decisions, not create more of them.
- Engineering Principle #2: Information should be entered once and reused everywhere.
- Engineering Principle #3: Every item needs a next action or a reason it is waiting.
- Engineering Principle #4: The system should remember what matters and release what does not.
- Engineering Principle #5: A system should make good decisions easier, not remove human judgment.
- Engineering Principle #6: Intelligence should reduce cognitive load, not increase information volume.
- Engineering Principle #7: Automation should increase confidence, not reduce visibility.
- Engineering Principle #8: Every new capability must plug into the platform without changing the platform.
- Engineering Principle #9: Every transaction should be understandable in less than 30 seconds.
- Engineering Principle #10: Communication should never depend on memory. It should depend on the system.
- Engineering Principle #11: Every error is either a process problem, a training problem, or an automation opportunity.
- Engineering Principle #12: The dashboard exists to direct attention, not display information.
- Engineering Principle #13: Every dashboard, automation, and AI recommendation must trace back to a defined object and field.
- Engineering Principle #14: Every repetitive request should eventually become a documented workflow, reusable template, or automation.
- Engineering Principle #15: Every handoff should transfer knowledge, not just responsibility.
- Engineering Principle #16: Data belongs to the platform, not to a department. Departments contribute to shared knowledge.
- Engineering Principle #17: The transaction is finite. The relationship is continuous.
- Engineering Principle #18: Marketing exists to build trust at scale.
