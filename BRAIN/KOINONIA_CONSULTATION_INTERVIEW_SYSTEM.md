# Koinonia Consultation & Interview System

Status: Canonical — Active Build  
Version: 0.2  
Date: 2026-08-14  
Business: Koinonia Transactions  
Primary Audience: Colorado Realtors, producing agents, growing teams, and brokerage relationships

---

## Purpose

Define the repeatable Koinonia consultation conversation that moves a qualified Realtor from interest to a clear first engagement.

This is not a presentation with discovery questions inserted into it.

It is a diagnostic business interview that earns the right to present Koinonia.

The consultation should help Koinonia understand how the Realtor operates today, identify where operational pressure is limiting the business or client experience, reflect that problem accurately, and only then prescribe the most appropriate Koinonia support path.

The consultation must remain consistent with the canonical Koinonia brand, service objects, packages, pricing, and professional boundaries.

---

## Governing Sales Principle

Diagnose first. Prescribe second. Present third.

For approximately the first third of the conversation, the Realtor should do most of the talking.

Koinonia should not lead with services, pricing, software, or a generic transaction-coordination pitch.

The goal is for the Realtor to compare their current operating problem against a better operating model — not immediately compare Koinonia against a list of vendors and prices.

---

## Consultation Objective

A successful consultation should end with five things being clear:

1. How the Realtor currently operates.
2. Where the greatest operational pressure exists.
3. What that pressure is costing in time, consistency, capacity, or client experience.
4. Which Koinonia support path best addresses the immediate need.
5. What the first engagement should be.

The purpose of the first consultation is not to sell every Koinonia service.

The purpose is to identify and earn the right first engagement.

# 20–30 Minute Consultation Architecture

## Stage 1 — Opening & Permission

Target: approximately 2 minutes.

Purpose:

- Lower sales resistance.
- Establish the agenda.
- Make clear that Koinonia will diagnose before presenting.
- Get permission to ask business questions.

Detailed script is defined below.

## Stage 2 — Business Diagnosis

Target: approximately 7–10 minutes.

Purpose:

- Understand the Realtor's business and workload.
- Identify current support and current operating model.
- Surface the largest time drain, stress point, or failure point.
- Understand what happens when workload increases.
- Identify the consequence of the problem.
- Understand the Realtor's desired future state and trust requirements.

Detailed question sequence is defined below.

## Stage 3 — Problem Reflection

Target: approximately 2–3 minutes.

Purpose:

- Summarize what was heard.
- Name the operational problem without exaggeration.
- Confirm the Realtor agrees with the diagnosis before Koinonia is presented.
- Create the bridge from the Realtor's own experience into the Koinonia reveal.

Detailed script and branching patterns are defined below.

## Stage 4 — Koinonia Reveal

Target: approximately 3–4 minutes.

Purpose:

- Explain why Koinonia exists.
- Introduce the operating-partner concept.
- Introduce the meaning of Koinonia only after the prospect understands the problem being solved.

Detailed scripting: pending approval.

## Stage 5 — Prescribed Service Reveal

Target: approximately 4–6 minutes.

Purpose:

- Lead with the service that directly addresses the diagnosed need.
- Show the other Koinonia services as the broader support system only after the primary recommendation is clear.
- Preserve professional boundaries.

Detailed scripting: pending approval.

## Stage 6 — Investment & Engagement Structure

Target: approximately 2–4 minutes.

Purpose:

- Introduce price only after value, need, and fit are established.
- Present the appropriate current package, pricing model, or scoped first engagement.
- Avoid turning the conversation into a menu of prices.

Detailed scripting: pending approval.

## Stage 7 — Decision & First Engagement

Target: approximately 2–3 minutes.

Purpose:

- Ask what would make the biggest immediate difference.
- Recommend a clear starting point.
- Ask directly for the first engagement.
- Establish the next concrete action.

Detailed scripting: pending approval.

# What Must Not Be Revealed Too Early

During Stage 1 and most of Stage 2, do not:

- Lead with the four Koinonia services.
- Show or discuss the full pricing menu.
- Walk through every Koinonia capability.
- Explain the client portal or unfinished product functionality.
- Lead with the meaning or pronunciation of Koinonia.
- Defend Koinonia against other transaction coordinators before the prospect raises a real comparison.
- Assume transaction coordination is the prospect's primary problem.
- Start explaining Koinonia every time the prospect names a pain point.
- Overwhelm the Realtor with internal systems, workflows, technology, or process terminology.

When a prospect names a problem that Koinonia solves, resist the urge to pitch immediately.

Ask one level deeper first.

Example:

Prospect: "Contracts take me forever."

Do not immediately answer: "We offer contract support."

Prefer: "Tell me what usually happens when a contract needs to get written while you are already with another client."

The diagnosis becomes stronger when the Realtor describes the real consequence in their own words.

# Stage 1 — Exact Opening & Permission Sequence

## Desired Tone

Calm, curious, professional, conversational, and confident.

Do not sound scripted, rushed, or overly formal.

## Recommended Opening

"Thanks for taking the time to meet with me. Before I tell you much about Koinonia, I'd rather understand how you're running your business today. Every Realtor operates a little differently, and I don't want to assume I know where support would actually help.

I'll ask you a few questions about your business, what's working, and where things start getting heavy. Then, if I think Koinonia can genuinely help, I'll show you where I think it fits. Sound fair?"

## If They Immediately Ask, "What exactly does Koinonia do?"

Give only the category-level answer:

"At the highest level, we provide real estate operations support for Realtors. I can absolutely walk you through it. I just want to understand your business first so I don't give you a generic presentation that may not match what you actually need."

Then move into Stage 2.

## Stage 1 Success Condition

Do not move forward because the words were delivered.

Move forward when the prospect understands that:

- this is a two-way business conversation;
- the first part is about their operation;
- Koinonia will make a recommendation only after understanding the need.

---

# Stage 2 — Business Diagnosis

## Operating Rule

The questions below are a sequence, not an interrogation checklist.

Use the six core questions in order. Use optional probes only when they help clarify the answer.

Do not ask every probe.

The prospect should feel heard, not processed.

---

## Question 1 — Understand the Business

Ask:

"Tell me a little about your business right now. What does a normal month look like for you?"

Listen for:

- solo agent versus team environment;
- approximate transaction/client volume;
- buyer/seller mix when relevant;
- growth stage;
- seasonality or workload volatility;
- whether the Realtor describes the business as controlled, busy, reactive, or overloaded.

Optional probes:

- "About how many active clients or transactions are you normally carrying at once?"
- "Has that been pretty consistent, or are you trying to grow from there?"
- "Are you operating mostly on your own, or do you already have people supporting parts of the business?"

Do not turn this into an income interview.

The purpose is to understand operating complexity and capacity.

---

## Question 2 — Map the Current Operating Model

Ask:

"When business gets moving, what are you still personally handling behind the scenes?"

If useful, follow with:

"Walk me through a busy week. Once you're working with clients or you get something under contract, what still comes back to you?"

Listen for:

- transaction timelines;
- deadlines;
- contract/document preparation;
- signatures and missing information;
- lender/title coordination;
- client updates;
- showing conflicts;
- CRM and follow-up;
- scheduling;
- task tracking;
- file organization;
- administrative cleanup;
- dependence on memory, inboxes, texts, or disconnected systems.

Do not start matching each answer to a Koinonia service out loud.

Keep diagnosing.

---

## Question 3 — Find the Primary Pressure Point

Ask:

"Of everything you're carrying, what tends to consume the most time or mental energy?"

Optional probe:

"What is the part you most often wish somebody dependable could just take ownership of?"

Listen for the prospect's language.

Record the problem using their wording whenever possible.

Examples of diagnostic categories:

- time drain;
- mental load;
- client-service conflict;
- deadline/document risk;
- scheduling conflict;
- inconsistent follow-through;
- growth/capacity limitation;
- lack of dependable support.

---

## Question 4 — Expose the Consequence

Ask:

"When two or three things hit at the same time, what usually starts slipping, getting delayed, or getting pushed into evenings and weekends?"

Then, when appropriate:

"What does that cost you — time with clients, prospecting, responsiveness, personal time, or just mental bandwidth?"

This question is critical.

A pain point without a consequence is rarely urgent enough to change behavior.

Listen for:

- lost prospecting time;
- delayed follow-up;
- slower client communication;
- late-night administrative work;
- missed family/personal time;
- fear of missing a deadline;
- inability to take another client;
- inconsistent client experience;
- burnout or operational stress.

Do not dramatize the consequence beyond what the Realtor says.

---

## Question 5 — Understand Existing Support & the Gap

Ask:

"What support do you have today — a TC, assistant, team resources, brokerage support, vendors, anything like that — and what still stays with you?"

Optional probes:

- "What's working well with the support you already have?"
- "Where do you still feel like the final catch-all?"
- "Have you tried delegating any of this before? What worked or didn't work?"

Purpose:

Koinonia should not assume the prospect has no support.

The goal is to understand the gap that remains.

Do not attack an existing TC, assistant, brokerage, or vendor.

---

## Question 6 — Define Capacity, Desired Outcome & Trust

Ask:

"If you could take one recurring category of work off your plate without losing visibility or control, what would you want it to be?"

Then ask:

"And if that gave you back five hours in a typical week, where would you put that time?"

Finish with:

"What would a support partner have to do consistently for you to trust them with part of your business?"

Listen for desired outcomes such as:

- more client-facing time;
- more prospecting;
- capacity for additional transactions;
- faster follow-up;
- fewer nights/weekends;
- better organization;
- peace of mind;
- predictable communication;
- confidence that work will not disappear when delegated.

The final trust question is important because Koinonia is asking to become part of the Realtor's operating environment, not merely perform an isolated task.

---

# Optional Capacity Stress-Test Question

Use when the prospect is growing or claims the current system is working but appears close to capacity:

"If two more clients called you this week and both needed your full attention, what would happen to everything you're already carrying?"

This question should not be used to manufacture fear.

Use it to test whether the current operating model scales.

---

# Diagnostic Listening Framework

During Stage 2, quietly capture these eight items:

1. Current business model / volume.
2. Current support structure.
3. Work the Realtor still owns personally.
4. Primary operational pressure.
5. Real-world consequence of that pressure.
6. What happens when workload increases.
7. Desired reclaimed time or capacity.
8. Trust requirement for a support partner.

Do not present this framework to the Realtor during the interview.

It is an internal guide for producing an accurate Stage 3 reflection.

---

# Diagnosis Completion Test

Stage 2 is complete only when Koinonia can accurately state, in plain language:

1. "Here is how you are operating today."
2. "Here is the operational pressure that appears to be costing you the most."
3. "Here is what you would rather be able to do with that time/capacity."

If those three statements are not clear, ask one more useful question before moving to the Koinonia reveal.

Do not move to Stage 3 merely because the clock says ten minutes have passed.

---

# Early Fit Signals

Positive fit signals include:

- The Realtor has active or growing business volume.
- Operational work is competing with client-facing or growth activity.
- The Realtor values organization, responsiveness, and professional communication.
- The Realtor is willing to delegate defined work while retaining professional responsibility.
- There is a specific recurring pressure Koinonia can address within approved service scope.

Caution signals include:

- The prospect wants legal advice, negotiation authority, or brokerage supervision transferred to Koinonia.
- The prospect expects unlimited undefined assistant access without scope.
- The prospect is unwilling to provide timely instructions, approvals, or required information.
- The prospect expects Koinonia to replace professional judgment that remains with the Realtor or brokerage.

Do not force a first engagement where the fit is poor.

# Stage 3 — Problem Reflection

## Purpose

Stage 3 converts discovery into a shared diagnosis.

The Realtor should hear an accurate summary of their own operating reality and agree that Koinonia understands the problem before any meaningful Koinonia presentation begins.

This is the hinge between discovery and prescription.

Do not use this stage to impress the prospect.

Use it to prove that Koinonia listened.

## Core Reflection Pattern

Use three parts:

### Part 1 — Reflect the Current Reality

Summarize the important work the Realtor is still personally carrying.

Preferred structure:

"So from what you've told me, you're currently handling [A], [B], and [C] yourself, even while you're also trying to stay focused on [clients / prospecting / growth / negotiations]."

### Part 2 — Name the Consequence

Use the Realtor's own language whenever possible.

Preferred structure:

"And when business gets busy, that seems to turn into [their consequence] — which is pulling you away from [their desired use of time/capacity]."

### Part 3 — Confirm the Diagnosis

Preferred structure:

"So it sounds like the issue isn't that you don't know how to do the work. The issue is that too much of the operation still depends on you personally. Is that fair?"

This sentence is the default capacity diagnosis.

It should not be used mechanically when another diagnosis is clearly more accurate.

---

## Why the Confirmation Question Matters

Do not transition into Koinonia until the Realtor confirms, corrects, or refines the diagnosis.

A simple "yes," "exactly," or useful correction is enough.

If the Realtor disagrees, that is not resistance to overcome. It means the reflection was incomplete or inaccurate.

Ask:

"What part did I get wrong?"

or:

"How would you describe the real issue?"

Then reflect again.

Do not defend the first interpretation.

---

# Reflection Branch A — Transaction Overload

Use when the primary pressure is contract-to-close management, deadlines, file organization, lender/title coordination, client updates, or closing preparation.

Recommended reflection:

"So you're doing the client-facing work, but once something goes under contract, you're also still carrying the timeline, follow-up, documents, coordination, and making sure nothing gets missed. And when several files are active at once, that's where your attention gets split and the back-end work starts following you into the rest of your day.

It doesn't sound like the problem is that you can't manage a transaction. It's that every additional transaction adds another operating load that still depends on you. Is that fair?"

Use their specific consequence instead of "following you into the rest of your day" when available.

---

# Reflection Branch B — Contract & Document Pressure

Use when the primary pressure is writing offers, amendments, addenda, notices, gathering terms, version control, signatures, or document readiness.

Recommended reflection:

"It sounds like the biggest interruption is document work. You're trying to stay with clients and keep momentum moving, but when something needs to be written, revised, organized, or sent for signature, it comes right back to you — often at exactly the wrong time.

So the issue isn't whether you know how to prepare the documents. It's that the drafting and document workflow are competing with the parts of the business that really require you personally. Is that accurate?"

---

# Reflection Branch C — Showing Coverage / Availability Pressure

Use when scheduling conflicts, distance, overlapping client needs, or buyer availability are the primary issue.

Recommended reflection:

"What I'm hearing is that your client demand sometimes exceeds your physical availability. You can have two good clients needing you at the same time, or a showing that doesn't make sense for you to personally cover, but you still want the client experience protected.

So the pressure isn't a lack of willingness to serve them. It's that your calendar can only put you in one place at a time. Is that the problem you're trying to solve?"

Do not imply that showing coverage replaces the Realtor-client relationship.

---

# Reflection Branch D — Recurring Business Operations Overload

Use when the prospect's pain is primarily CRM, follow-up, task management, organization, recurring processes, calendar support, pipeline maintenance, or general operational follow-through.

Recommended reflection:

"It sounds like the transaction itself isn't the only issue. The bigger problem is all the recurring business work around it — follow-up, CRM, tasks, organization, scheduling, and keeping things moving after the urgent client work takes over.

You know what needs to happen, but you're still the person everything eventually comes back to. And because client work always wins, the business-maintenance work becomes inconsistent. Is that fair?"

---

# Reflection Branch E — Mixed Capacity Problem

Use when no single service category fully describes the pressure.

Recommended reflection:

"What stands out to me is that this isn't really one task. You're carrying several different operating roles at the same time — [name the two or three real categories they described].

Each one by itself may be manageable, but when they stack up, you become the point everything depends on. That's what starts taking time away from [their desired outcome].

Would you say the bigger issue is overall capacity more than one isolated task?"

If yes, the later service reveal should still identify the best starting point rather than trying to sell all services at once.

---

# Reflection Branch F — Existing Support but Remaining Gap

Use when the Realtor already has a TC, assistant, brokerage support, team support, or other vendor.

Recommended reflection:

"You already have support in place, and it sounds like [name what is working] is being handled well. The gap seems to be [remaining responsibility], which still comes back to you and creates [consequence].

So I'm not looking at this as replacing what is already working. The question is whether that remaining gap is important enough to solve. Is that how you see it?"

Never create artificial dissatisfaction with an existing provider.

Koinonia should complement existing support when that is the correct fit.

---

# Reflection Branch G — Growth Before Pain

Use when the Realtor is not currently overwhelmed but expects growth and wants to build capacity before the current system breaks.

Recommended reflection:

"It sounds like things are working today. The concern is really what happens if the business grows the way you want it to. Right now the system works because you are personally absorbing a lot of the moving parts.

So the question isn't necessarily how to fix a crisis. It's how to add capacity before growth creates one. Is that a fair way to describe it?"

Do not manufacture urgency when the prospect is planning proactively.

Proactive buyers should be treated as strong-fit prospects, not as people who need to be frightened into action.

---

# When Multiple Problems Surface

Do not reflect every problem equally.

Identify the problem with the clearest combination of:

- frequency;
- consequence;
- urgency;
- fit with approved Koinonia scope;
- effect on the Realtor's desired outcome.

Then say:

"You mentioned a few different pressure points, but if I had to prioritize what seems to be costing you the most right now, I'd say it's [primary problem]. Would you agree?"

If the Realtor chooses a different priority, follow their priority unless it falls outside Koinonia's approved scope.

The prospect's priority should guide the first engagement.

---

# When the Prospect Says "It's Not That Bad"

Do not argue.

Respond:

"That's helpful. Then I don't want to overstate it. Would you say this is more of an inconvenience right now, or something you want to solve before volume increases?"

This distinguishes current pain from proactive capacity planning.

Either can be a valid reason to work with Koinonia.

---

# When There Is No Clear Koinonia Fit

If the reflection reveals that the real need falls outside Koinonia's approved scope, say so.

Example:

"Based on what you've described, I don't think the main problem is something I should pretend Koinonia solves. What you're really dealing with is [plain-language issue]."

Then give an appropriate next step if one is known and within professional boundaries.

Do not distort Koinonia's services to force a sale.

Long-term trust is more valuable than a poor-fit first engagement.

---

# Stage 3 Transition Rule

Only after the Realtor confirms the diagnosis should Koinonia begin the reveal.

Default bridge:

"That's exactly the kind of problem Koinonia was built around. Let me show you how I think about it."

Alternative, more personal bridge:

"That's actually very close to the reason I built Koinonia in the first place. Let me show you what I mean."

Alternative when the prospect already has support:

"That gap is where I think Koinonia may fit without disrupting what is already working. Let me show you how I would approach it."

Do not jump directly from the bridge into pricing.

The next step is Stage 4 — the Koinonia Reveal.

---

# Stage 3 Success Condition

Stage 3 is complete when:

1. The Realtor has heard an accurate summary of their operating reality.
2. The consequence has been stated using their language or an accurate paraphrase.
3. A primary problem or capacity gap has been identified.
4. The Realtor has confirmed or corrected the diagnosis.
5. Koinonia has a clear basis for deciding what to reveal and eventually prescribe.

If those conditions are not met, remain in diagnosis/reflection.

Do not present Koinonia merely because the meeting is moving toward the halfway point.

---

# Stage 3 Anti-Patterns

Do not:

- tell the Realtor they are disorganized unless they explicitly describe themselves that way;
- exaggerate consequences;
- manufacture fear around deadlines, compliance, or client loss;
- say "you need Koinonia" before fit is established;
- use canned empathy such as "I totally understand" and immediately pivot into a pitch;
- reflect five problems when one primary problem is enough;
- criticize existing support;
- present a service before the Realtor agrees with the problem being solved;
- confuse a symptom with the actual operating problem.

Preferred posture:

Accurate, calm, specific, and useful.

# Relationship to Existing Canonical Material

This system extends — and does not replace — the existing Koinonia Sales Operating System and service-specific sales enablement.

The Transaction Management service already establishes these discovery themes:

- What part of transaction management consumes the most time?
- Where is the greatest operational stress?
- How many active transactions are normally managed?
- What happens when workload increases?
- What does a successful support partnership look like?

This consultation system broadens those questions so Koinonia can diagnose the full real estate operations relationship before assuming transaction management is the correct entry point.

Canonical service claims, scope, packages, and pricing remain governed by their existing business objects and pricing sources.
